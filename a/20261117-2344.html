World News Brief: Nov 17, PM

Top line

The fight over a CNN report of an alleged Iranian "dirty bomb" plot in Washington led the day. Trump warned Iran of "unprecedented consequences," the White House said there is no credible threat, and Tehran denied it. US Central Command says Iranian drones fired at Bahrain were intercepted with no damage. Iran says two more ships hit mines in the Strait of Hormuz. In Ukraine, footage shows a section of the Crimean Bridge lying in the Kerch Strait after Ukraine's overnight strike, and Kyiv is still dealing with the aftermath of Russian strikes on its bridges. All times are ET. Coverage runs from about 6:30 AM to 6:40 PM Tuesday, Nov 17.

Iran and Israel–Iran

  • "Dirty bomb" report. Times of Israel says CNN, citing multiple US officials, reported new intelligence that Iran is working on plans to target senior Trump administration figures in Washington with a "dirty bomb" or a chemical weapon (Times of Israel, 5:05 PM ET). Trump warned Iran of "unprecedented consequences." Iran denies the claims, and the White House says there is no credible threat (Al Jazeera, 10:47 AM ET). Unconfirmed: no evidence has been made public (Al Jazeera, 11:30 AM ET).
  • Bahrain drones intercepted. US Central Command says it intercepted several one-way attack drones that Iran's IRGC launched toward Bahrain earlier Tuesday. CENTCOM says there was no damage to US equipment and no harm to personnel (Al Jazeera, 7:10 AM ET). That contradicts the IRGC's claim this morning that it hit a US radar.
  • Hormuz. The IRGC says two vessels caught fire after hitting a naval mine in the Strait of Hormuz, and one of them was reportedly carrying two million barrels of crude (Al Jazeera, 7:00 AM ET). This account comes from Iran and I haven't seen independent confirmation. Separately, Iran's Fars agency said residents of Qeshm island in the strait heard several explosions and saw smoke northwest of Qeshm city (Al Jazeera, 8:50 AM ET). Unconfirmed: the cause.
  • Threats against infrastructure. An X News summary says parliament speaker Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf warned that Iran would hit dams, ports, and power plants near US bases if the US strikes similar Iranian sites (X News summary). Al Jazeera spoke with Iranians who are worried by US media reports that water, power, and gas infrastructure could be targets (Al Jazeera, 8:21 AM ET).
  • Plot in the Balkans. North Macedonia and Greece say they uncovered an Iran-linked assassination plot (Reuters, 12:35 PM ET).
  • Diplomacy. Qatar met Iranian officials Tuesday to try to bridge differences over current proposals and urged all sides to avoid escalation (Al Jazeera, 9:06 AM ET). The outcome hadn't been reported by publication time. The foreign ministers of Oman, Iran, and Uzbekistan met in Muscat (Al Jazeera, 10:00 AM ET). Reuters analysis: Trump and Tehran are both "gambling on time" as the war's stalemate deepens (Reuters, 11:10 AM ET).
  • Washington. Acting Navy Secretary Hung Cao says the war is straining US maritime forces. Senators raised concerns about long ship deployments, shrinking weapons stockpiles, and resources pulled away from the Asia-Pacific (Al Jazeera, 6:20 PM ET). Hakeem Jeffries, who is expected to become House Speaker, said the war "should end today" (Al Jazeera, 5:42 PM ET).
  • Inside Iran. An X News summary reports anti-government protests in Abdanan after a bus crash killed 13 oil workers. Crowds reportedly chanted for Reza Pahlavi (X News summary). Unconfirmed: no major outlet report in my search. Grand Ayatollah Hossein Vahid Khorasani has died at 105, and Wednesday was declared a national day of mourning (X News summary).
  • Yemen and the Red Sea. Yemen's Houthis say they launched drones toward Saudi Arabia's East-West pipeline (Reuters, 5:35 PM ET). Yemen's recognized government says its forces carried out 660 operations against the Houthis, including 24 air strikes that killed about 178 fighters (Al Jazeera, 2:08 PM ET). Both sets of claims come from the parties themselves.
  • Israel, Syria, West Bank. Syria condemned continued Israeli incursions into Quneitra and Deraa (Al Jazeera, 4:24 PM ET). A Palestinian woman was killed in a settler shooting near Jericho (Al Jazeera, 11:00 AM ET). The UK sanctioned eight Israeli settlement construction firms and the activist group Tzav 9 (Al Jazeera, 7:53 AM ET). A Jerusalem court ordered former ICC prosecutor Karim Khan to pay NIS 25 million (about $7.7 million) to families of former hostages in a civil suit (Times of Israel, 7:01 AM ET).

Russia–Ukraine

  • Crimean Bridge. Ukraine released drone footage that it says shows a section of the bridge falling into the water (Reuters, 1:50 PM ET). AFP says the section appeared to be lying in rubble in the strait (AFP, 2:37 PM ET). The Kyiv Independent reports that Ukraine used Flamingo and Neptune missiles plus long-range drones, the final step of a "seven-phase operation" (Kyiv Independent, 12:33 PM ET). One aviation expert estimated repairs could take "a year or even longer" (Kyiv Independent, 2:06 PM ET). Reuters has an explainer on why the bridge matters (Reuters, 6:10 PM ET). An official Russian damage assessment wasn't in my search.
  • Kyiv after the strikes. AFP video shows commuters crossing a damaged bridge on foot after Russian strikes disrupted traffic and transit across the Dnipro (AFP, 3:30 PM ET; BBC, 12:21 PM ET). The Kyiv Independent also reports a fire at a major Russian petrochemical complex in Bashkortostan during a drone attack (Kyiv Independent, 2:23 PM ET).
  • Moldova. Seven drones crossed into Moldovan airspace during Russia's latest attack, and a Shahed-type drone was spotted over Chișinău (Kyiv Independent, 2:10 PM ET).
  • Money and weapons. According to a draft seen by the Kyiv Independent, the European Commission will propose another €45 billion in loans to Ukraine for 2027, to be repaid only once Russia pays reparations (Kyiv Independent, 4:49 PM ET). Norway and Sweden pledged more than $370 million for weapons (Kyiv Independent, 5:37 PM ET). Zelensky asked allies for missile exchanges and 40 more F-16s (Kyiv Independent, 4:19 PM ET).
  • US–Europe rift. Germany and Britain rejected Pentagon policy chief Elbridge Colby's criticism that Ukraine's strikes on Russian oil are ineffective (Reuters, 5:40 PM ET). Colby also warned NATO allies not to shut US firms out of arms deals (Reuters, 5:40 PM ET).
  • Diplomacy. Zelensky says Ukraine has proposed an Oleshky-style humanitarian operation in 10 more occupied settlements and will discuss it with US mediators (Kyiv Independent, 1:50 PM ET). Trump said he is looking at one-on-one meetings with Putin and Kim Jong Un during his Asia tour (AFP, 8:02 AM ET).

Other world news

  • Trump in Asia. APEC members are expected to look past trade differences and agree on a summit text (Reuters, 9:45 AM ET). BBC looks at how the Nvidia spy scandal could complicate China's AI ambitions as Trump and Xi meet (BBC, 6:05 PM ET). Papua New Guinea's prime minister told Xi it "wasn't easy" to close his country's Taiwan office (Reuters, 6:40 AM ET).
  • Nvidia turmoil. Nvidia's chairman says the board won't reinstate Jensen Huang as CEO (AFP, 5:11 PM ET). AFP says Huang, who resigned over an affair with an alleged Chinese spy, said he wanted his job back (AFP, 5:45 PM ET). He is due to appear before Congress in December (Reuters, 6:20 PM ET).
  • US security and infrastructure. The NSA's classified internal network has reportedly gone offline (BBC, 5:02 PM ET). Unconfirmed: the cause. Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy says no root cause has been found yet for the router failure that shut down US aviation on Friday (Reuters, 6:00 PM ET).
  • Sudan. The UN says drones caused nearly three-quarters of Sudan's war deaths this year (Reuters, 8:50 AM ET).
  • Myanmar and Gaza. A ruling from the top UN court on the Myanmar genocide case will be watched for clues about the Gaza case (Reuters, 10:10 AM ET).
  • Haiti. AP looks at Salvadoran President Bukele presenting himself as the answer to Haiti's gang crisis while his troop deployment draws scrutiny (AP, 5:36 PM ET).
  • France. Turnout fell at largely peaceful public-sector marches across France (Reuters, 6:00 PM ET).
