World News Brief: Nov 1, PM

Covering roughly 7 AM to 7 PM ET Sunday, Nov 1. All times are Eastern. Items drawn from X story summaries (compiled from posts, not wire copy) are marked as such; treat them as less certain than direct outlet reports.

Iran and Israel–Iran

Trump "in no hurry" on a deal. President Trump said Washington is in no hurry to strike a deal with Iran and declined to say whether he expects the US to go back to war with Tehran after Tuesday's midterm elections (Reuters, 10:00 AM). Trump and Netanyahu reportedly spoke by phone Saturday as the US laid plans to resume strikes on Iran (Times of Israel, 9:00 AM).

Doha talks continue. Iranian and Qatari mediators held a third day of talks aimed at ending the US–Iran war (Reuters, 2:20 PM). Per an X story summary, the agenda includes reopening the Strait of Hormuz and nuclear steps, and Tehran is seeking sanctions relief, frozen assets and a reduced US military presence; Iran's Supreme Leader has reportedly ordered forces to update target lists for an immediate response to any US or Israeli attack (X story; X story). The specific demands are not confirmed by a wire.

More tanker blasts in Hormuz. Iran's IRGC Navy says two tankers "exploded" after hitting mines in the Strait of Hormuz (Reuters, 3:20 PM). Vessel names and casualties were not given in the post; this is the IRGC's own claim.

Iran names the Royal Navy and RAF "terrorist" groups. Per an X story summary, Iran's Foreign Ministry designated the UK Royal Navy and Royal Air Force as terrorist organizations and MI6 as hostile, calling it reciprocity for British measures against the IRGC (X story). Still not confirmed by a major wire in this window.

  • Gulf stock markets slid on Iran war worries, with Qatar the exception (Reuters, 3:50 PM).
  • Former Israeli general and opposition figure Yair Golan said Netanyahu lacks the public trust and legitimacy for a new campaign against Iran (Times of Israel, 8:26 AM).
  • Exiled crown prince Reza Pahlavi begins a Europe tour Nov 3 in Belgium and Germany and is set to accept a Freedom Prize in Frankfurt on behalf of Iranians, per an X story summary (X story).
  • Videos showed young Iranians celebrating Halloween in Tehran despite bans, with reports of security forces dispersing and detaining revelers (X story). Unconfirmed.

Russia–Ukraine

Oleshky truce ends. The longest truce of the war, limited to the Kherson region and brokered with the involvement of First Lady Melania Trump, ended Sunday after Russia refused an extension, Ukrainian officials said (AFP, 5:00 PM; AFP, 4:07 PM). Before it expired, Ukraine evacuated 167 residents from the right bank of the Dnipro, including 22 children (Kyiv Independent, 6:48 PM; Reuters, 6:40 PM).

Air raid app hacked. Ukraine's air force says the public air raid alert app was hacked (Reuters, 4:45 PM). Per an X story summary, it pushed fake radiation and chemical-attack warnings before being taken offline; officials urged people to ignore them (X story).

Strikes on both sides. A Russian drone hit near a vocational school dormitory in Vinnytsia Oblast, killing a 13-year-old girl and a woman and injuring more children (Kyiv Independent, 2:10 PM). Russian attacks over the past day killed one and injured 47, leaving hundreds of thousands without power across at least four regions (Kyiv Independent, 8:55 AM; Reuters, 1:10 PM). Zelensky confirmed strikes on a Russian rocket fuel factory plus a Pantsir-S2 and an S-400 command vehicle, estimating $70 million in damage (Kyiv Independent, 10:56 AM).

Front line. Zelensky says Ukraine liberated more territory than Russia seized in October (Kyiv Independent, 8:56 AM), a Ukrainian claim not independently verified. ISW says Ukrainian forces recently advanced northeast of Borova (ISW, 10:35 AM). Per an X story summary, Zelensky said he's ready to meet Putin in Turkey and would pause refinery strikes if Russia stops hitting Ukraine's energy grid first (X story).

  • Bulgaria: Iliana Iotova, who favors dialogue with Moscow and opposes military aid to Ukraine, won the presidential runoff, exit polls show (AFP, 5:30 PM).
  • Foreign Minister Sybiha urged governments to deny entry to Russian athletes as Russia's return to international ice hockey looms (Kyiv Independent, 10:57 AM).
  • Russia's independent newspaper Novaya Gazeta says it will shut down Nov 9 after 30 years, citing repression, per an X story summary (X story).

Other Conflicts

  • Lebanon: Farmers picking olives near Israeli-occupied areas face threats, with some saying Lebanese army protection is no guarantee of safety (Reuters, 9:00 AM).
  • Israel politics: Coalition deadlock after the Oct 27 election continues; commentators are floating cross-party initiatives to avoid another vote (Times of Israel, 5:00 PM).
  • Nigeria: Advocacy groups shared reports of continuing deadly attacks on Christian communities; their death tolls come from advocacy organizations, and Nigeria's government attributes the violence to broader insecurity (X story). Figures unverified.

Top World News

  • Florida jet crash: All nine people aboard a business jet (a Learjet) died when it crashed into a south Florida neighborhood, the US transportation secretary said (AP, 4:46 PM; Reuters, 5:10 PM).
  • US–Canada: Trump floated the "concept" of a US–Canada deal including a pipeline after speaking with PM Carney (Reuters, 6:00 PM).

  • Germany: Engineering industry workers began wage strikes (Reuters, 2:30 PM).
  • China: State broadcaster CCTV dropped a Real Madrid match after Kylian Mbappe's remarks about Mao Zedong caused uproar (AFP, 11:55 AM).
  • The crew of NASA's simulated Mars mission emerged after 378 days in isolation (Reuters, 4:00 PM).
