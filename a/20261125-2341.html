World News Brief: Nov 25, PM

Covering roughly 6:40 AM to 6:40 PM ET Wednesday, Nov. 25, 2026. Times are ET. Claims not independently confirmed are labeled.

Iran and Israel–Iran

More mine strikes in Hormuz. Iran said ships hit mines in the Strait of Hormuz and warned vessels to coordinate their passage with it (Reuters, 3:10 PM). Weekly crossings through the strait fell from 217 to 147 between late October and mid-November (Al Jazeera, 12:29 PM). U.S. Under-Secretary of State Allison Hooker said she held "productive" talks with Gulf states and Jordan, saying coordinated action in Hormuz is increasing pressure on Iran (Al Jazeera, 3:11 PM).

Houthis hit Saudi targets. Yemen's Iran-aligned Houthis said they attacked Saudi Arabia's largest refinery, Ras Tanura, as satellite images showed thick black smoke over the site (Reuters, 2:30 PM). The Saudi-led coalition said a Houthi projectile caused minor damage at Jazan port (Iran International, 12:03 PM), that it downed a drone near Jeddah port (Iran International, 2:48 PM), and later that it intercepted two ballistic missiles (Iran International, 5:44 PM). A Reuters witness heard several loud booms in Riyadh; the cause was not immediately clear (Iran International citing Reuters, 4:06 PM).

Economic squeeze. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent called the Iranian regime "a sinking ship," saying Iran has not loaded a single tanker since Operation Economic Outcast began in August and has no oil left on the water to sell (Bessent on X, 9:09 AM; Iran International, 6:28 PM). Iran's agriculture minister claimed essential-goods reserves are higher than before the war (Iran International, 12:01 PM). Activist accounts report a truckers' strike in its fourth day across 15+ cities over freight rates and costs; officials deny an organized walkout. Scope not independently confirmed (activist post, 12:25 PM).

Threats and accusations. Iran stepped up warnings to the U.S., Israel and Gulf states, threatening new military capabilities and demanding compensation from neighbors it accuses of helping attacks (Iran International, 2:09 PM). Iran's UN ambassador accused the U.S. of using AI in deadly strikes on Iran (Al Jazeera, 2:19 PM). FM Abbas Araghchi dismissed Israeli claims, made Tuesday by Defense Minister Israel Katz, that Iranian sleeper cells in the U.S. were ordered to kill President Trump; that claim is unverified (Araghchi, 10:51 AM). An Iranian lawmaker said Russia withheld S-400 air defenses and that Moscow and Beijing expected the Islamic Republic to fall within weeks (Iran International, 3:57 PM).

New underground site. Satellite imagery shows rapid construction of a hardened underground facility at Behjatabad, roughly midway between Isfahan and the Natanz-area "Pickaxe Mountain" site. Its purpose is unknown (Institute for Science and International Security, 10:15 AM).

Repression. Security forces arrested activist Mehdi Mahmoudian a day after he publicized execution notices for two 2022 protesters (Iran International, 2:58 PM). Exiled prince Reza Pahlavi urged governments to expel Iran's ambassadors (Iran International, 3:12 PM).

Regional proxies. CENTCOM's Adm. Brad Cooper praised Syrian forces for seizing IRGC weapons headed to Lebanon (Iran International, 2:00 PM). Hezbollah's Naim Qassem stressed keeping the alliance with Tehran (Iran International, 3:02 PM).

Russia–Ukraine

Ballistic attack on Kyiv. Russia launched a large ballistic-missile attack Wednesday evening, hitting a shopping center in Kyiv and injuring three, one seriously, per authorities (Kyiv Independent, 4:45 PM). Earlier, Putin said Russia "will respond to every strike by Kyiv" (WarTranslated, 2:55 PM).

Ukraine's deep strikes. Ukraine said it hit five Russian military jets at an airfield (Reuters, 9:50 AM); reports say three MiG-31s and two Su-34s at Savasleyka, plus the Rosneft Kuibyshev and Syzran refineries. Kyiv told Putin to "stop complaining" about refinery attacks (The Hill, 5:46 PM). Ukraine's Blysk laser system downed a Russian jet-powered drone in combat for the first time (Kyiv Independent, 12:52 PM).

Diplomacy. Zelensky said there is no good news from the U.S. on speeding up air-defense missile deliveries (Kyiv Independent, 6:12 PM), and that Kyiv is waiting for Washington to set a date for the next round of peace talks (Ukrinform, 1:14 PM). Lavrov said Europe must first explain its goals before joining talks (Russian MFA, 12:54 PM). Poland is planning a no-fly zone near the border and asked NATO to intercept Russian missiles over Ukraine (Kyiv Independent, 11:17 AM).

Casualty leak. The Kremlin declined to comment on reports, based on leaked Rosstat death records analyzed by Mediazona and Meduza, that up to 700,000+ Russian soldiers have been killed (AFP, 10:57 AM). These are estimates from hacked data; some analysts dispute them.

Israel, Gaza and the wider region

An Israeli strike killed Hamas's internal security chief and six others in Gaza City despite the ceasefire (Al Jazeera, 11:09 AM; BBC, 9:09 AM). Al Jazeera, citing a hospital source, reported three killed in a drone strike in eastern Gaza City (Al Jazeera, 5:09 PM). Gaza municipalities warn winter rain could flood about 1,000 displacement sites (Al Jazeera, 6:20 PM). Israel's parliament gave initial approval to an independent Oct. 7 inquiry that Netanyahu opposes (Reuters, 2:45 PM). In Syria, officials said an ISIS attack in Hasakah killed two security personnel (Al Jazeera, 3:39 PM).
