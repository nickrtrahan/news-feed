World News Brief: Nov 12, PM

Top line

Tonight's lead: the Strait of Hormuz got more dangerous today. Iran says two more tankers were "disabled." Reuters reported a tanker struck off the UAE. Then Iranian state media said the port city of Sirik had been hit by "enemy fire." President Trump said he would have "no hesitation" in resuming strikes on Iran. On Ukraine, US envoys Witkoff and Kushner met Putin for about three hours and then briefed Zelensky in Dublin. Afterward, Kushner said Putin has agreed to let Europeans into the next round of talks, and Zelensky announced what he called the largest prisoner exchange of the war. Outside the war zones, Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang resigned. All times are ET, Thursday, Nov 12.

Iran and Israel–Iran

  • Sirik hit by "enemy fire" (Iranian state media). Iran's state news agency IRNA said the southern port city of Sirik in Hormozgan province was hit by "enemy fire." It called the strike military in nature but gave no details on damage or casualties (Al Jazeera, 2:28 PM ET). Unconfirmed: social media posts link the blasts to US strikes on radar and military sites (X News summary). No US statement confirming this has turned up.
  • More tankers hit around Hormuz. The IRGC says two oil tankers were "disabled" by explosions in the strait (Reuters, 9:10 AM ET). Separately, an oil tanker was struck off Ras Al-Khaimah in the UAE, and all crew were reported safe (Reuters, 11:50 AM ET). Oman is monitoring a tanker that ran aground off its coast (Reuters, 11:40 AM ET). Reported but not independently confirmed: monitors say more than 10 missiles were fired from Bandar Abbas toward the strait. Iranian statements say the latest ships hit mines in restricted zones, bringing the total to four vessels in recent days (X News summary).
  • Trump: "no hesitation." Trump said there would be "no hesitation" in resuming strikes on Iran, adding, "it's possible I would do it right now." He also claimed Iran's navy and air force are "gone." Only days ago he had called renewed strikes "quite unlikely" (Al Jazeera, 5:15 PM ET). Tehran, meanwhile, says the "ball is in the American court" on stalled talks, and analysts warn the "no war, no peace" calm is fragile (Al Jazeera, 3:54 PM ET).
  • Israeli politics over a possible strike. Israel's security cabinet has reportedly told the IDF to raise readiness for a possible strike on Iran (public broadcaster KAN, via X News summary). Defense Minister Katz said Israel needs no approval "from the opposition or any legal authority" to strike. Opposition leader Eisenkot urged restraint (Al Jazeera, 10:46 AM ET). Avigdor Lieberman warned that a caretaker government may not make decisions that could drag Israel into war (Al Jazeera, 7:33 AM ET). Netanyahu's office called a report of a "shouting match" with Trump over Iran "nonsense" (Times of Israel, 10:15 AM ET).
  • Hardline signals from Tehran. Iran's Assembly of Experts called reviewing withdrawal from the Nuclear Non-Proliferation Treaty an "unavoidable necessity." This is a statement, not a decision: Iran has filed no withdrawal notice (X News summary). Security official Mohsen Rezaei warned that US bases worldwide, including in Europe, would be targets if Iran is attacked again (X News summary).
  • UK convictions. Two Iranian nationals were convicted under Britain's National Security Act of spying on Iran International journalists, and one was also convicted of preparing a violent attack. Iran denies involvement (Al Jazeera, 2:45 PM ET).

Russia–Ukraine

  • Envoys meet Putin, then Zelensky. Putin hosted Witkoff and Kushner at the Kremlin (Reuters, 10:05 AM ET), and the meeting ended after about three hours (Kyiv Post, 9:41 AM ET). According to AFP, Putin signaled no compromise on his core demands but offered a short-term ceasefire in one key frontline city (AFP, 11:04 AM ET). Kyiv warns that Putin's proposed "humanitarian mission" in Kostiantynivka could be used to advance Russian military goals. Putin claims Russian forces control the city, which Ukraine disputes (Kyiv Independent, 11:29 AM ET). The envoys then briefed Zelensky in Dublin (AFP, 6:00 PM ET).
  • Europe at the table, big prisoner swap. Kushner says Putin has agreed to let European representatives take part in the next round of negotiations (Kyiv Independent, 6:00 PM ET). Zelensky says Ukraine and Russia will carry out the largest prisoner exchange of the war (Kyiv Post, 5:52 PM ET). The Kremlin says Trump and Putin will discuss a settlement when they meet at next week's APEC summit in Shenzhen (Kyiv Independent, 2:59 PM ET).
  • Dublin summit. At the European Political Community summit, Zelensky insisted that Europe must have a direct role in the negotiations (Kyiv Independent, 1:58 PM ET). He named his top needs as Patriot and NASAMS interceptors, F-16 munitions, and winter energy aid (Kyiv Post, 10:57 AM ET). Ireland pledged €25 million more for Ukraine's energy grid (X News summary).
  • Sanctions and pressure. The UK says it is the first country to sanction all of Russia's top 10 oil producers (Kyiv Independent, 12:12 PM ET). Finland is restricting visas for Russian athletes (AFP, 5:45 PM ET). Czechia is set to send up to 16 L-39 jets to Ukraine for anti-drone work (Kyiv Post, 11:42 AM ET).
  • Refinery campaign. Ukraine's military says it has hit at least nine Russian refineries in two weeks, together about 36% of Russia's refining capacity (Kyiv Post, 11:40 AM ET). Unconfirmed, developing tonight: Russian channels report a large Ukrainian drone attack, including on the Lukoil Volgograd refinery and the Moscow region (X News summary). Ukraine's SBU also claims it hit the Russian corvette Naro-Fominsk and oil facilities at Ust-Luga in the Gulf of Finland (X News summary).
  • Kyiv strike. A Russian attack on Kyiv's Solomianskyi district hit a nine-story apartment building and killed a woman, hours after the Abu Dhabi talks ended (X News summary).

Gaza, Yemen and the wider region

  • Nasser Hospital. An Israeli strike on Nasser Medical Complex in southern Gaza killed six people, the health ministry says (BBC, 7:05 AM ET). Among the dead were surgeon Dr. Rizq Abu Shuaira and a child (Al Jazeera, 12:34 PM ET; Reuters, 10:25 AM ET). Floods and cold are adding to the misery in the tent camps (Al Jazeera, 3:14 PM ET).
  • Houthis hit Saudi pipeline (Houthi claim). Yemen's Houthis say they attacked a pumping station on Saudi Arabia's East-West oil pipeline (Reuters, 5:10 PM ET). Saudi Arabia is pressing the EU to label the Houthis a terrorist group (Reuters, 11:20 AM ET). The UN Security Council unanimously extended Yemen-related sanctions (Al Jazeera, 6:30 PM ET).

Other world news

  • Nvidia CEO resigns. Jensen Huang stepped down as Nvidia's chief executive after revelations of two extramarital affairs, including one with a woman accused of spying for China (AFP, 6:45 PM ET). The Justice Department says it has not concluded that she was a Chinese spy (Reuters, 6:10 PM ET). Reuters reports that tighter curbs on AI chip sales to China are now likely (Reuters, 7:00 PM ET).
  • Pope Leo is considering changes to how the Church treats abuse victims, after a meeting in Peru (Reuters, 6:40 PM ET).
  • Spain: a judge suspended the ultra-right group Núcleo Nacional (Reuters, 5:30 PM ET).
