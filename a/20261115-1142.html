World News Brief: Nov 15, AM

Top line

Overnight, Iran's Revolutionary Guards said they struck US military assets in Bahrain. Bahrain sounded sirens and says it intercepted the attack, and US Central Command had not commented as of early morning. Tehran's Foreign Ministry also brushed off the UN nuclear watchdog's latest call to resume inspections. In Ukraine, Russia launched 132 drones overnight in a "mass attack" focused on Odesa Oblast, and Ukraine reportedly hit a Russian airfield in Astrakhan. Elsewhere, Houthis took control of an area near the Bab el-Mandeb Strait, Israel killed Hamas's Rafah Brigade commander in Gaza, and ASEAN leaders are gathering in Manila ahead of Trump's expected arrival Tuesday. All times are ET. Coverage runs from about 6:40 PM Saturday to 6:40 AM Sunday, Nov 15.

Iran and Israel–Iran

  • Iran says it hit US targets in Bahrain. Bahrain says it intercepted Iranian aerial attacks after Tehran said it attacked a US base (Reuters, 4:00 AM ET). Bahrain told residents to stay calm and seek safety as sirens sounded, and CENTCOM had not commented on Iran's claims (Al Jazeera, 12:36 AM ET). The IRGC calls the strikes a response to "the enemy's aerial aggression against a telecommunications tower in Sirik" in southern Iran last week (Al Jazeera, 6:04 AM ET). Unconfirmed: according to a summary of posts on X, the IRGC says it used six drones to hit a US air-defense radar at Bahrain's Al-Riffa base, and explosions were reported near Manama and Sitra. There is no independent word yet on damage or casualties (X News summary).
  • Nuclear standoff. Foreign Ministry spokesman Esmaeil Baghaei called the IAEA "one-sided and biased" after it again urged Iran to let inspections resume (Al Jazeera, 4:20 AM ET). He said there are no talks about Iran pausing or reducing uranium enrichment as part of any deal with the US (Al Jazeera, 4:00 AM ET). He also said Tehran has "documented" evidence that some countries in the region helped with US strikes on Iran, and that it has raised this through diplomatic channels (Al Jazeera, 3:06 AM ET).
  • Missile stockpile. A report says US intelligence believes Iran could still have as many as 2,500 missiles (Times of Israel, 2:15 AM ET).
  • Ghalibaf ultimatum (via X summary). Parliament Speaker Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf reportedly said Iran won't accept a state of "neither war nor peace" with the US. He reportedly offered talks only if Washington meets seven conditions and warned of "the smoke of a new war" otherwise (X News summary). I haven't found a wire report confirming this yet.
  • Alleged drone plot (unconfirmed). An X News summary says Israel warned the US of an alleged Iranian plan to attack Trump and senior cabinet members with explosive drones, and that this came up when Mossad chief Roman Gofman met CIA Director John Ratcliffe in Washington (X News summary). This has not been confirmed by a wire service.
  • Ripple effects. Iraq's prime minister fired nine senior security officials over alleged oil smuggling in Jurf al-Nasr, a stronghold of the Iran-backed Kataib Hezbollah (Al Jazeera, 2:05 AM ET). Oil shortages from the war are high on the agenda as ASEAN leaders meet in the Philippines this week (Al Jazeera, 2:45 AM ET).

Russia–Ukraine

  • Mass attack on Odesa. Russia launched 132 long-range attack drones overnight, 78 of them jet-powered, with Odesa Oblast the main target. Over the past day, strikes across Ukraine killed 4 people and injured 69 (Kyiv Independent, 4:55 AM ET).
  • Strike on Astrakhan airfield (unconfirmed). Ukraine reportedly hit the Akhtubinsk military airfield in Russia's Astrakhan Oblast, with at least 30 explosions heard. The report comes from a Telegram channel, and neither side has confirmed it (Kyiv Independent, 5:26 AM ET).
  • Starshield slowdown. ISW says Ukrainian officials reported a sixth day of speed limits on SpaceX's Starshield network over parts of Ukraine, which makes it harder to defend against Russia's jet-powered drones (ISW, 11:00 PM ET Sat). ISW also repeated its view that Putin is still committed to his original aim of destroying NATO (ISW, 10:30 PM ET Sat).
  • Black Sea shipping. Romanian authorities confirmed that drones struck three commercial vessels near Romania's coast on Nov 13–14. A Ukraine-bound cargo ship was damaged and two other ships caught fire, with no casualties (Kyiv Independent, 7:22 PM ET Sat).
  • Other items. Ukraine is turning to aerial evacuation drones to rescue wounded soldiers stuck in the front-line "kill zone" (AFP, 2:33 AM ET). YouTube blocked 16 Russian channels in Ukraine (Kyiv Independent, 11:50 PM ET Sat). Trump plans to raise nuclear arms reduction at Thursday's summit with China and Russia (Reuters, 6:30 PM ET Sat).

Other conflicts

  • Yemen. Government forces pulled out of an area near the Bab el-Mandeb Strait, leaving the Houthis in control. That matters for Red Sea shipping (Reuters, 3:40 AM ET).
  • Gaza. The IDF confirmed it killed the commander of Hamas's Rafah Brigade in an overnight strike on a Gaza camp (Times of Israel, 2:15 AM ET; BBC, 3:19 AM ET).
  • South China Sea. The rift between the Philippines and China is deepening (Reuters, 4:35 AM ET). ASEAN reaffirmed its engagement with Myanmar and wants to move its peace plan forward (Reuters, 5:35 AM ET).
  • Guatemala. The government declared a state of emergency in two departments after a lawmaker was killed in an attack (Reuters, 4:30 AM ET).

Other world news

  • Jordan. Three men were sentenced to death over a deadly mass shooting at a church service (Reuters, 6:00 AM ET; BBC, 6:10 AM ET).
  • Migration. Nearly 1,500 migrants reached Spain's Canary Islands in a single day (Reuters, 5:00 AM ET).
  • Canada. A fifth Canadian soldier has died of injuries from a large fire at a military base (BBC, 4:28 AM ET).
  • US–China. Ahead of the Xi talks, Trump's push on critical minerals has yet to loosen China's grip on the supply (Reuters, 6:10 AM ET).
  • Pope Leo in Peru. The pope will travel across Peru, with visits to an Inca city and a gateway to the Amazon (Reuters, 5:20 AM ET).
