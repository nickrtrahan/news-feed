Iran and the Gulf

Explosions reported on Qeshm Island (unconfirmed details). Iranian state media reported at least five heavy blasts on Qeshm Island in the Strait of Hormuz on Monday, along with claims that Iranian air defenses shot down a U.S. MQ-9 Reaper drone and captured two smaller reconnaissance drones. The cause of the blasts, the drone claims, and online reports of unannounced U.S. operations against Iranian air defenses have not been independently confirmed. Tehran also warned it would hit U.S. bases in Europe if President Trump orders strikes. (X story summary, updated about 7:05 PM ET)

Talks: no breakthrough, but contacts continue. Iran said contacts over ending the war are continuing after the latest round of talks ended without a breakthrough (Reuters, 8:50 AM ET). Earlier, Iran warned that any renewed war with the U.S. could last longer than the previous round, after Trump said he still had more targets to "take care" of (AFP, 7:08 AM ET).

Pezeshkian says the Supreme Leader granted negotiating powers. President Masoud Pezeshkian said on state media that Supreme Leader has given the government certain powers for talks and stressed dialogue over war. Oil dropped sharply on the news, with WTI briefly falling below $100 (X story summary). Reuters reported oil sliding on U.S.–Iran deal hopes as London's FTSE 100 rose for a fourth straight day (Reuters, 4:40 PM ET).

Gulf states on edge. Reuters reports Trump's Iran threat has renewed Gulf fears of "a war it didn't choose" (Reuters, 4:35 PM ET). Gulf Arab states have reportedly warned Iran they would retaliate if it attacks their infrastructure in response to any U.S. strike, and the UAE and Qatar have run security drills (X story summary). ADNOC's CEO said nearly one billion barrels have been drained from global inventories since the Strait of Hormuz was disrupted (Reuters, 11:50 AM ET).

Iran's Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi visited Turkmenistan (X story summary), and exiled crown prince Reza Pahlavi begins a Belgium–Germany trip Tuesday ahead of accepting a freedom prize on Nov. 7 (X story summary).

Russia–Ukraine

Deadly strike on an aid point in Zaporizhzhia. Russian bombs hit a school used as a humanitarian aid center in Komyshuvakha village. The toll has climbed through the day: AP cited Zelensky saying 18 dead (AP, 12:48 PM ET), then Reuters reported 24 (Reuters, 1:40 PM ET) and later 26, citing emergency services (Reuters, 5:45 PM ET). Zelensky called it a deliberate double-tap strike that killed rescuers and police; Russia has not commented.

New peace talks set. U.S. envoy Steve Witkoff announced U.S.–Russia–Ukraine trilateral talks in Abu Dhabi on Nov. 10–11 (Kyiv Independent, 6:54 PM ET). U.S. negotiators also meet European allies, the EU and NATO in Brussels this week on security guarantees (X story summary). EU officials told the Kyiv Independent the bloc is unlikely to accept a U.S. proposal to resume Russian gas flows to Europe through Ukraine (Kyiv Independent, 10:08 AM ET).

Zelensky's Patriot offer. In a Fox News interview, Zelensky offered a one-month pause in strikes on Russian refineries in exchange for a month's supply of Patriot missiles, which Ukraine would pay for (X story summary).

Alert-app hacks. Both Ukraine and Russia said their emergency alert apps were hacked overnight, sending false warnings of air raids and chemical and nuclear attacks (Reuters, 11:55 AM ET). Zelensky later confirmed Ukraine targeted Russian alert systems in retaliation (Kyiv Independent, 6:08 PM ET).

Oleshky truce fallout. Ukraine says Russia used the six-day Oleshky humanitarian truce to rearm, while Moscow assumes Kyiv did the same (Reuters, 8:45 AM ET). The Kremlin ruled out more operations like it for now (Kyiv Independent, 12:02 PM ET).

  • Russian attacks killed 2 and injured over 30 in the past day, with power outages in 10 regions (Kyiv Independent, 10:43 AM ET).
  • Ukraine's General Staff claims a record 2,500 Russian personnel losses on Nov. 1; this is Ukraine's own figure (Kyiv Independent, 7:29 AM ET).
  • Ukrainian intelligence (HUR) says a Su-57 was destroyed in an engine fire at Akhtubinsk airfield (Kyiv Independent, 8:23 AM ET), and that Russia has adapted its Gerbera decoy drone for one-way attacks up to 1,200 km (Kyiv Independent, 11:30 AM ET).
  • Russian media outlet Verstka reports the Defense Ministry will delay troop salary payments, except for forces fighting in Ukraine (Kyiv Independent, 12:05 PM ET).
  • Russia formally left the Council of Europe's anti-torture convention (Kyiv Independent, 7:33 AM ET).

Top World News

Ebola in DR Congo. The Bundibugyo-strain outbreak has passed 10,000 confirmed cases and 5,000 deaths across eight provinces, making it Congo's deadliest Ebola epidemic; cases have been reported in Kenya, Uganda and Nigeria, and Africa CDC is urging all countries to step up surveillance (X story summary).

Press freedom. On the International Day to End Impunity for Crimes against Journalists, UN Secretary-General Guterres warned of rising impunity, and UNESCO reported an 86% impunity rate for journalist killings (X story summary).
