World News Brief: Nov 11, PM

Top line

Tonight's lead: Iran is widening its threats. Its top security official says any US base used in a new war, including bases in Europe, would be a target. Reuters sources say Iran attacked shipping navigation equipment on Oman's Didamar Island. In Ukraine, the Abu Dhabi talks ended with a "large-scale" prisoner exchange agreed but no breakthrough. Within hours, Russia fired ballistic missiles at Kyiv. In US politics, Democrat Scott Colom upset Sen. Cindy Hyde-Smith in Mississippi.

Iran and Israel–Iran

  • Threats to bases in Europe. Mohsen Rezaei, secretary of Iran's Supreme National Security Council, said on X that if the US launches another war, every military base involved would be a target, including ones in Europe (Iran International, 6:27 PM ET). Commentators on state TV called for fully closing the Strait of Hormuz to force US concessions (Iran International, 6:02 PM ET). Deputy chief of staff Keyoumars Heydari said the armed forces have an updated list of possible targets "in the region and beyond" (Iran International, 2:50 PM ET). An analyst told Al Jazeera that Tehran may be deliberately pushing its retaliation beyond the Middle East (Al Jazeera, 2:14 PM ET).
  • Hormuz and Oman. Sources told Reuters that Iran attacked shipping navigation systems on Oman's Didamar Island (Reuters, 2:10 PM ET). Reuters-verified footage shows a tanker burning near Oman, with oil spills reaching Oman's shores (Iran International, 6:15 PM ET). According to an X story summary, US Central Command says its blockade has turned back 150 vessels and blocked about $30 billion in Iranian oil exports. That figure hasn't been checked against a major outlet (X story summary). Saudi Arabia has raised crude output to its highest level since the war began in February (Al Jazeera, 4:16 PM ET; Reuters, 3:45 PM ET).
  • Washington. At a Veterans Day event at Arlington, President Trump said it was time for the US to secure a "complete victory" over Iran (Al Jazeera, 2:41 PM ET). Citing two officials, The Wall Street Journal reports that Trump has privately questioned whether the Pakistan-brokered June agreement with Iran was a costly mistake that let Tehran regroup (Iran International citing WSJ, 4:50 PM ET).
  • Israel. Israeli politician Gadi Eisenkot wrote to caretaker Prime Minister Netanyahu warning him against dangerous military moves that could wait for a new government. Reports say Netanyahu and military chiefs disagree on possible action (Iran International, 4:56 PM ET). Contradicting Trump, Netanyahu's office says Israel will move off US military aid only "gradually" (Times of Israel, 11:22 AM ET).
  • Diplomacy and the UN. The UN General Assembly elected Iran to its Committee for Programme and Coordination with 144 votes (Iran International, 2:21 PM ET). Canada says it voted no (Iran International, 5:29 PM ET). At the COP31 summit in Turkey, President Pezeshkian said US-Israeli strikes released 1.5 million tonnes of CO2, and Iran is seeking compensation for environmental damage (Al Jazeera, 4:40 PM ET; Reuters, 5:20 PM ET).
  • Inside Iran. Videos show students at Islamic Azad University's Central Tehran branch taking off their hijabs as they leave campus, defying the mandatory hijab rules (Iran International, 5:56 PM ET). Iran is adding 23 commemorations to its official calendar, including days for the war and the killing of former Supreme Leader Ali Khamenei (Iran International, 3:42 PM ET). Rights activists are drawing attention to death sentences against several men arrested in January's protests. This comes from an X story summary of activists' posts (X story summary).

Russia–Ukraine

  • Kyiv under attack. Breaking: Russia fired ballistic missiles at Kyiv hours after the peace talks ended. Kyiv Independent journalists reported several explosions around 1 AM local time, which was about 6 PM ET (Kyiv Independent, 6:37 PM ET). There are no damage or casualty reports yet.
  • Talks end. The second day in Abu Dhabi ended with a "large-scale" prisoner exchange agreed, according to Ukraine's Rustem Umerov. Russia's Vladimir Medinsky confirmed it (Kyiv Independent, 9:58 AM ET). The BBC says the talks turned to post-war security but ended without a breakthrough (BBC, 10:08 AM ET). Medinsky said there was some progress but no role for the EU for now (Reuters, 4:00 PM ET). He also repeated Russia's rejection of Ukraine's territorial integrity (Kyiv Independent, 10:32 AM ET). Zelensky is in Dublin for the European Political Community summit and expects to be briefed by US envoys Witkoff and Kushner (Kyiv Independent, 1:43 PM ET).
  • Strikes. Russia hit six major power substations in Vinnytsia, Kirovohrad, Zhytomyr and Cherkasy regions, causing outages across Ukraine (Kyiv Independent, 11:26 AM ET). Hackers took over the website of Ukraine's state news agency Ukrinform (Kyiv Independent, 2:35 PM ET). Drones hit Gazprom's Omsk refinery in Siberia, Russia's largest, about 2,500 km from Ukraine, in an eight-hour attack (Kyiv Independent, 5:57 PM ET). ISW says neither side advanced on Nov. 10 (ISW, 11:00 AM ET).
  • Iran link. Zelensky warned that the chance of new US strikes on Iran, and air-defense missiles going to the Middle East, are weakening Ukraine's ability to defend itself (Iran International, 5:16 PM ET).
  • Other. Russia's foreign ministry says Russia's ambassador to the US has died (Reuters, 6:10 PM ET). Ukraine captured its first Indonesian prisoner of war as Russia recruits more foreigners (Kyiv Independent, 2:17 PM ET). Ireland and Ukraine are considering producing drones together (BBC, 6:30 PM ET). US companies are avoiding Russian diesel, and a tanker carrying it is idling off the East Coast (Reuters, 3:40 PM ET).

Other conflicts

  • Gaza. Breaking: Gaza's Civil Defence says an Israeli air strike on the Nasser Medical Complex in Khan Younis killed at least four Palestinians (Al Jazeera, 6:38 PM ET). In Deir el-Balah, a sand embankment collapsed in heavy rain and killed three children and a woman (Reuters, 10:00 AM ET). Al Jazeera puts Wednesday's death toll from Israeli attacks at eight or more (Al Jazeera, 2:01 PM ET).
  • Yemen and Saudi Arabia. Saudi air defenses intercepted three Houthi cruise missiles fired at Riyadh (Reuters, 10:20 AM ET). The Houthis say they hit an oil pumping station in Yanbu with a drone. That is their own claim (Iran International, 1:58 PM ET). The WHO counts 2,098 killed and 9,657 wounded in Yemen's fighting as of Nov. 8 (Al Jazeera, 1:42 PM ET).
  • Lebanon. Lebanon's army chief met German defense officials to plan for after the UN peacekeeping mission (UNIFIL) ends on Dec. 31 (Al Jazeera, 2:13 PM ET).
  • US–China. China's foreign minister called his phone call with Secretary of State Rubio "positive and constructive" (Reuters, 6:40 PM ET).

Other world news

  • US politics. Democrat Scott Colom defeated Republican Sen. Cindy Hyde-Smith in Mississippi. He becomes the state's first Black US senator since 1881 (AP, 6:06 PM ET; Reuters, 6:11 PM ET). At the Tomb of the Unknown Soldier, a sentinel rebuked Defense Secretary Hegseth's group after laughter was heard (AP, 5:50 PM ET).
  • Business. The Wall Street Journal reports that Nvidia is preparing to oust CEO Jensen Huang (Reuters citing WSJ, 6:30 PM ET). Markets are pricing in a hawkish Fed (Reuters, 3:50 PM ET).
  • Europe. Millions of Spaniards held the country's first general strike in 14 years, over housing (Reuters, 3:42 PM ET). An advisory panel warned Germany to rein in its military borrowing (AFP, 3:47 PM ET).
  • Africa. Leaders of Uganda's ruling party are pushing back on President Museveni's plan to make his son his successor (AFP, 6:30 PM ET).
  • Church. Pope Leo XIV arrived in Peru, his "second homeland," for the first time since his election (AP, 5:12 PM ET).
  • New York. Emergency crews are searching debris after a building collapsed in Brooklyn (Reuters, 6:19 PM ET).
