World News Brief: Nov 4, PM

Top line

The day after Democrats won both chambers of Congress, the Iran war dominated Washington's post-mortem: Trump said the war was worth it while admitting it hurt Republicans, and Vice President Vance questioned whether it was worth the cost. Gulf states are reportedly bracing for possible new U.S. strikes on Iran, and the U.S. sanctioned Indian and Turkish firms for aiding Tehran. In Ukraine, Russia's winter strike campaign keeps knocking out power, while Ukraine and its partners prepare for U.S.-brokered talks with Russia in Abu Dhabi on Nov 10–11. Covers roughly 7 AM to 7 PM ET Wednesday, Nov 4.

Iran and Israel–Iran

  • Trump: the war was worth it. Trump acknowledged the Iran war and high gas prices played a role in Republican midterm losses but said "the Iran thing we had to do, and we continue to do it" (AP, 3:18 PM ET; Reuters, 3:41 PM ET). AFP reports analysts see few good options for him from here (AFP).
  • Vance distances himself. Reuters reports Vice President JD Vance questioned whether the war was worth the cost and blamed it for the election losses (Reuters, 5:03 PM ET; AP explainer). Republican allies are also pressing Trump to shake up his staff and drop policies including the war and tariffs (Reuters), though Trump publicly backed Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth (Reuters).
  • New U.S. sanctions. The U.S. sanctioned Indian and Turkish companies and individuals for aiding Iran (Reuters, 2:10 PM ET).
  • Gulf states bracing (reported). Citing a Wall Street Journal report, an X news summary says U.S. Gulf allies are preparing for possible heavy U.S. airstrikes on Iran, with airport drills in Qatar and the UAE and Patriot systems being moved to Israel. Trump has said no immediate strike is planned. I haven't seen the WSJ report directly.
  • Disputed chemical-weapons warning. Israel has shared intelligence claiming Iran is advancing chemical-weapon delivery, which has reportedly split U.S. officials; some see it as pressure to pull Trump into new strikes, and Iran denies it (X news summary). Unconfirmed.
  • Hormuz exemptions (Iranian claim). An Iranian lawmaker says ships from India, China, Iraq and Qatar may now pass the Strait of Hormuz, with Indian and Chinese ships still paying fees (X news summary). Not independently confirmed.
  • Embassy-takeover anniversary rallies. Iranians rallied nationwide on the 47th anniversary of the 1979 U.S. Embassy seizure; Supreme Leader Mojtaba Khamenei said U.S. elections won't change American hostility, and some banners urged Iran to quit the NPT (X news summary). Iranian state TV also aired a map showing missile paths to Kyiv, Lviv, Odesa and Rzeszow, Poland; Ukraine's disinformation center called circulating versions altered (X news summary).
  • Israel and the U.S. Reuters says the new Congress and growing Republican unease could bring the biggest reassessment of U.S. support for Israel in decades (Reuters). Israel's UN ambassador Danny Danon said the decision to attack Iran was America's, with Israel in support (X news summary). That summary also says Trump announced an end to $4 billion a year in military aid to Israel by the end of 2026; I couldn't confirm that from a wire report, so treat it as unverified. Israeli opposition figure Gadi Eisenkot pledged to renew a bipartisan U.S.-Israel alliance (Times of Israel, 6:53 PM ET).
  • Death sentence over X posts. A Tehran Revolutionary Court sentenced activist Amirhossein Mousavi to death over his online posts, according to an X news summary.

Russia–Ukraine

  • Grid under attack. ISW says Russia's overnight Nov 3–4 drone strikes hit central and western Ukraine hardest and caused significant outages nationwide, part of a winter campaign doing serious damage to infrastructure and the economy (ISW, 5:34 PM ET; ISW takeaways). Reports describe Kyiv getting only a few hours of power some days and a missile hitting a hospice in Dnipro (X news summary).
  • Komyshuvakha toll rises. The death toll from Russia's Nov 2 bombing of a school where civilians were collecting aid in Zaporizhzhia region is at least 40, with more possibly under rubble (X news summary).
  • Ukraine strikes Ozon again. A Ukrainian drone strike set fire to a large Ozon e-commerce warehouse in Adygeysk, southern Russia; Ozon said staff were evacuated without injuries (Reuters, 1:45 PM ET).
  • Run-up to Abu Dhabi talks. The European Commission hosted talks in Brussels with the U.S., Ukraine, NATO and European states ahead of the Nov 10–11 Abu Dhabi round with Russia (Kyiv Independent, 3:44 PM ET). The Kremlin said it hopes Trump keeps up his peace push despite the midterm results (X news summary).
  • Missile output. Ukraine's military intelligence estimates Russia can produce over 200 ballistic and cruise missiles a month, up to 110 of them ballistic (Kyiv Independent).
  • Kherson ceasefire ends. ISW says a local ceasefire in Kherson Oblast ended Nov 1 after apparently allowing up to 800 civilians to be evacuated from occupied Oleshky (ISW).
  • Kyiv Independent hacked. Intruders used the outlet's newsletter to send fake nuclear alerts; editors say the hole is patched and donor data was untouched (X news summary). Russia is suspected but not confirmed.

Other conflicts

  • Lebanon. Israel carried out airstrikes and shelling across southern Lebanon; an Israeli drone strike near Qabrikha killed a paramedic and wounded a photojournalist, per Lebanon's health ministry, while Israel said it targeted people watching its troops (X news summary).
  • Gaza. Israel's military and Shin Bet say strikes in Khan Younis earlier this week killed six militants, including a Hamas intelligence figure who guarded hostage Omer Shem Tov (X news summary).
  • Yemen–Saudi Arabia. The Houthis claim a ballistic missile strike on Riyadh's main airport and the downing of a Saudi drone over Taiz; Saudi Arabia hasn't confirmed the airport strike and flights continued. Saudi strikes in Yemen reportedly killed at least 12 civilians (X news summary). Unverified.
  • Ethiopia–Sudan. On the sixth anniversary of the Tigray war, Ethiopia expelled six Sudanese diplomats and closed their consulate amid mutual accusations of backing rebels (X news summary).
  • Syria. Masked attackers assaulted Kurdish students protesting limits on Kurdish-language classes in Hasakah (X news summary).

Other world news

  • Greenland. Greenland's parliament ratified a security pact giving the U.S. two new defense areas and an upgraded Pituffik Space Base, with sovereignty staying with Denmark and Greenland; Denmark's approval is still pending (X news summary).
  • NATO deep strikes. NATO is reportedly planning a doctrine of conventional deep strikes on Russian bases and supply lines in the event of an attack (X news summary).
  • North Korea. ISW assesses North Korea is likely using Ukraine-war experience to improve coordination of missiles, artillery and drones (ISW).
  • Midterm ripple effects. Reuters looks at what the U.S. midterms mean abroad, from Iran to trade (Reuters).
