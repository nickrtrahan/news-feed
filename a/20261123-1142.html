World News Brief: Nov 23, AM

Covering roughly 6:30 PM Sunday to 6:40 AM ET Monday, Nov 23, 2026. All times ET. Sources are posts on X from the outlets named.

Iran and Israel–Iran

Israeli media report a split over the timing of new strikes. Israel's public broadcaster Kan says Prime Minister Netanyahu is pressing President Trump to strike Iran "as soon as possible" in a short joint campaign (Al Jazeera, 1:20 AM). Unconfirmed: Ynet reported that Washington has decided for now to put off any strike by at least a week (Iran International, 9:29 PM), while i24NEWS, citing Israeli security officials, said US Central Command is at maximum readiness and pushing for a strike (Iran International, 8:11 PM). Trump said on Sunday that he leans toward acting only after the new year if talks fail.

Tehran hit back at the US naval blockade. Foreign Ministry spokesman Esmaeil Baghaei called it "military aggression" and said Iran would use all means to counter it (Iran International, 3:34 AM). He also said Iran could quickly restore security in the Strait of Hormuz if the US met Tehran's conditions (Al Jazeera, 3:08 AM). An Iranian lawmaker claimed Iran hits about 10 "violating" tankers a day (Iran International, 1:30 AM). Baghaei added that no decision to leave the Nuclear Non-Proliferation Treaty has been sent to the Foreign Ministry (Iran International, 2:47 AM).

US politics: incoming House Speaker Hakeem Jeffries told CBS that Democrats will try to end the war once they control the House in January (Iran International, 8:48 PM). Al Jazeera reports Democrats are weighing a funding cutoff (Al Jazeera, 7:05 PM). The Atlantic reported that the Pentagon has limited congressional access to Middle East bases and public data on war costs and casualties (Iran International, 10:29 PM).

Inside Iran: President Pezeshkian said the US and Europe "cannot topple us unless we topple ourselves" (Iran International, 5:59 AM). The Intelligence Ministry said it arrested 27 people and broke up "four armed cells" in West Azarbaijan (Iran International, 4:55 AM). Rolling power cuts and truckers' strikes over fuel costs continue.

Region: Yemen and Lebanon

  • Houthi-run media say an attack on a telecommunications building in Hodeidah, Yemen, killed at least 10 and wounded more than 20. It was not clear who carried it out (Al Jazeera, 4:51 AM).
  • Israeli forces kept demolishing homes in the south Lebanese village of Zawtar ash-Sharqiyah (Al Jazeera, 8:45 PM).

Russia–Ukraine

Ukraine takes its naval drone war to the Mediterranean. Zelensky said Ukrainian naval drones sank the Russian corvette Merkury in a Libyan port, shortly after a drone strike on a Russian military-linked cargo ship in the Baltic (Reuters, 4:15 AM; AP, 3:20 AM). Earlier the UN called for an independent probe of the attacks off Libya (AFP, 11:10 PM).

Deadly strikes on the Kyiv region. A Russian attack on Dymer killed 11, an official said (Reuters, 4:50 AM). The toll rose overnight, and rescuers pulled a 7-year-old boy and a 79-year-old woman from the rubble (Reuters, 6:30 AM). Kyiv Post says at least 15 people were killed across the Kyiv region on Nov. 22, including in a strike on a supermarket (Kyiv Post, 3:12 AM). Odesa is largely without power after strikes on energy infrastructure (AFP, 12:08 AM).

Ukraine is reported to have struck oil sites inside Russia; reports say the Novoshakhtinsk refinery was on fire (Kyiv Independent, 8:20 PM). Diplomacy: Trump's envoy and son-in-law said the president is open to Article 5-like security guarantees for Ukraine, if they serve US interests (Kyiv Post, 5:48 AM). ISW noted that a Russian jet drone violated Moldovan airspace twice on Nov. 21 (ISW, 8:01 PM).

Other world news

  • Sudan: According to an X news summary of posts, drones hit Khartoum and Omdurman on Sunday. At least four civilians were killed and a UN building with staff inside was damaged. This has not been independently confirmed here.
  • Congo: A Reuters review of satellite images suggests eastern Congo's Ebola deaths are badly undercounted (Reuters, 5:30 AM).
  • Climate: the COP31 summit in Turkey ended in failure after talks on fossil fuels collapsed (AFP, 6:00 AM).
  • Markets: South Korea promised to steady its markets after the KOSPI fell 5%, following global stocks' worst week since early in the pandemic (Reuters, 6:40 AM).
  • US: Democrats now hold their largest House majority in more than 30 years, after Chuck Hubbard beat Rep. Virginia Foxx in North Carolina (AFP, 6:40 AM).
  • Belgium: a strike against austerity measures grounded flights (BBC, 6:28 AM).
