World News Brief: Nov 9, PM

Top line

After a 45-minute Oval Office meeting with Qatar’s prime minister, Trump said there was “positive momentum” toward ending the Iran war and that military action isn’t necessary “at this point,” though he says the US is ready to strike if needed and is content to keep the naval blockade. Tehran’s security chief Mohsen Rezaei called the talk of progress false and demanded the US accept Iran’s seven conditions. Turkey’s intelligence chief says JD Vance and Rezaei will meet in Qatar next week. In Washington, Republican Rep. Thomas Massie filed impeachment articles against Defense Secretary Hegseth over the war. Brent crude fell nearly 7%. In Ukraine, the negotiating team is already in Abu Dhabi for talks starting Tuesday, the EU plans another €45 billion for 2027, and a drone killed a worker at the Zaporizhzhia nuclear plant. Covers roughly 7 AM to 7 PM ET Monday, Nov. 9.

Iran and Israel–Iran

  • Trump–Qatar meeting. Trump and Qatari Prime Minister Sheikh Mohammed bin Abdulrahman Al Thani met for about 45 minutes around noon, focused on Iran talks (Iran International citing Axios, 1:50 PM ET). The Qatari premier also met Vice President Vance and said the meetings focused on advancing US–Iran negotiations (Iran International, 5:01 PM ET); Al Jazeera, citing a source, reported “good progress” (Iran International citing Al Jazeera, 4:18 PM ET). Axios later reported Trump told him he’s willing to give talks a chance but could reverse course if Tehran doesn’t engage seriously (Iran International citing Axios, 6:51 PM ET).
  • Trump’s remarks. Trump said there was “positive momentum” toward a deal (Iran International, 5:49 PM ET), a “good chance” of agreement with Tehran “talking very politely” (Times of Israel, 5:52 PM ET), and that military action isn’t necessary “at this point” (Reuters, 6:00 PM ET). He told Reuters Qatar and Saudi Arabia want a peaceful deal, that he’s content with the Hormuz blockade, and that the US is ready to strike if needed (Reuters, 4:30 PM ET; Iran International, 4:15 PM ET). He also claimed Iran is “militarily essentially gone” and that Iran has accepted it will never have a nuclear weapon, which Tehran publicly denies (Iran International, 4:55 PM ET; Iran International, 5:28 PM ET). On Truth Social he said Qatar, Saudi Arabia, the UAE, Bahrain and Kuwait favor keeping the blockade in place (Iran International, 4:49 PM ET).
  • Tehran’s response. Security chief Mohsen Rezaei accused Washington of spreading false reports about negotiations and demanded the US accept Iran’s seven conditions (Iran International, 5:49 PM ET). Parliament speaker Ghalibaf said Israel is dragging the US into an endless war no president could escape (Iran International, 1:10 PM ET). Turkey’s intelligence chief Ibrahim Kalin said Vance and Rezaei are scheduled to meet in Qatar next week; that hasn’t been confirmed by Washington or Tehran in the posts reviewed (Iran International, 11:07 AM ET). China says it’s in contact about a possible Xi–Putin–Trump meeting (Iran International, 9:14 AM ET).
  • Strikes and Hormuz. A senior Iranian source told Reuters that US strikes over the past week have targeted air-defense and navigation radars, missile-launcher bases and naval mine-laying systems, including on Qeshm Island (Iran International citing Reuters, 10:20 AM ET). UKMTO classified as an attack a Saturday incident in which a tanker caught fire northwest of Khasab, Oman, and its crew evacuated (Iran International, 11:45 AM ET). The UAE city of Khor Fakkan shut a beach after detecting an oil spill (Reuters, 4:50 PM ET). An X news summary says Iran struck radar and communications gear on Oman’s Didamar Island in the strait; that is unconfirmed by the wire services reviewed here (X news summary).
  • Gulf and Yemen. Saudi state media says the East-West oil pipeline was hit in the Medina region; details on who was responsible weren’t in the report’s headline (Reuters, 5:45 PM ET). Earlier, Saudi Arabia said the danger had passed in Riyadh after an alert, a day after a Houthi attack (Reuters, 7:40 AM ET), and the Saudi-led coalition said it destroyed a Houthi ballistic-missile component factory and a missile-fuel plant at unspecified locations (Iran International, 1:51 PM ET).
  • France. French officials blame Iran for cyberattacks on supermarket refrigerators that spoiled tens of millions of euros of food, and vow retaliation (Reuters exclusive, 3:35 PM ET). In Israel, police and Shin Bet are investigating threatening leaflets suspected to come from Iran found in north Tel Aviv mailboxes (Iran International citing Kan, 8:47 AM ET).
  • Washington politics. Rep. Thomas Massie introduced eight articles of impeachment against Hegseth, including waging war on Iran without congressional approval, starting a two-day clock toward an expected House vote (Reuters, 6:14 PM ET; AFP, 4:00 PM ET). Hegseth played down the risk (Reuters, 6:35 PM ET) and Trump called Massie “a sick guy” (Iran International, 5:31 PM ET). House Democratic leader Hakeem Jeffries said Iran is stronger after the campaign and vowed to end the war (Iran International, 4:52 PM ET).
  • Markets. Brent crude fell nearly 7% to $91.61 on hopes of a diplomatic resolution (Iran International, 3:37 PM ET), and the S&P 500 jumped 2.3%, its biggest gain since April (AP, 4:11 PM ET). A Reuters survey found OPEC output in October rose to 90% of its pre-war rate (Reuters, 6:40 PM ET).

Russia–Ukraine

  • Abu Dhabi talks. Zelenskyy said Ukraine’s negotiators are already in the UAE, the first meeting is Tuesday, and European partners will join in separate formats (Zelenskyy, 4:13 PM ET). Ukraine’s negotiator said Italy will also take part, separate from the trilateral format (Kyiv Independent, 8:14 AM ET). At the G7 in Paris, European ministers pressed Secretary of State Rubio to make sure any settlement is fair to Ukraine (AFP, 11:50 AM ET). Zelenskyy also briefed India’s Modi and asked India to help push for an end to the war (Zelenskyy, 9:02 AM ET; Kyiv Independent, 10:29 AM ET). The UN Security Council met on Ukraine (Reuters, 10:29 AM ET).
  • Money and sanctions. The EU plans a new €45 billion for Kyiv in 2027 on top of its existing loan pledge (Kyiv Independent, 11:04 AM ET), and Germany’s Merz complained of unfair burden-sharing and urged the EU to prioritize a new loan (Kyiv Independent, 5:57 PM ET). An IMF mission began talks that could unlock a $1.67 billion tranche (Kyiv Independent, 8:22 AM ET), and Canada sanctioned 38 entities and 24 people tied to Russia’s missile industry and crypto exchanges (Kyiv Independent, 8:57 AM ET). The EU also activated an emergency mechanism to fund urgent energy repairs (Kyiv Independent, 8:10 AM ET).
  • Zaporizhzhia nuclear plant. The IAEA says a drone strike killed a worker at the Russian-occupied plant, and a second drone killed an employee at the nearby thermal plant (Kyiv Independent, 5:00 PM ET).
  • Ukrainian strikes. Ukraine’s General Staff says it hit the Morozov plant near St. Petersburg, which it says makes ballistic-missile fuel (Kyiv Independent, 8:28 AM ET), a Russian satellite-communications jamming station (Kyiv Independent, 11:03 AM ET), and a base of Russia’s elite “Rubicon” drone unit in occupied Berdyansk (Kyiv Independent, 3:26 PM ET). These are Ukrainian military claims. Russian media report Rosneft has imposed gasoline purchase limits in Krasnoyarsk Krai amid the refinery attacks (Kyiv Independent, 7:30 AM ET).
  • Black Sea and postwar planning. Reuters reports Russia has struck cargo ships carrying military goods for Ukraine, with analysts saying it aims to cut Ukraine’s exports and test NATO red lines (Reuters podcast, 8:25 AM ET). A British- and French-led coalition began its first exercise in Poland to prepare for a postwar deployment in Ukraine (Kyiv Independent, 9:50 AM ET). ISW told the FT that Russian videos claiming battlefield gains are part of a campaign to make Russian victory look inevitable (ISW citing FT, 12:09 PM ET).

Other conflicts

  • Gaza. Four people were killed when a sand embankment collapsed onto tents, which Reuters confirms after this morning’s brief flagged it as unconfirmed (Reuters, 9:35 AM ET). The Board of Peace said Israel is holding up Hamas disarmament and must let more winter supplies into Gaza (Times of Israel, 1:07 PM ET).
  • Syria and Jordan. The two countries signed cooperation deals as they try to mend ties after deadly airstrikes (Reuters, 9:50 AM ET).

Other world news

  • Nvidia. Trump says the FBI is investigating CEO Jensen Huang’s affair with a woman US officials say is a Chinese agent, and that Huang told him he had no idea (AP, 5:27 PM ET; BBC, 5:52 PM ET). Nvidia and Huang were sued over related disclosures (Reuters, 6:10 PM ET).
  • Poland. Tens of thousands joined a silent march in Krakow led by the president and prime minister for the victims of the weekend mass shooting (AFP, 6:30 PM ET).
  • South Africa. Analysts call the ANC’s sweeping local-election losses a vote for performance over loyalty (Reuters, 4:31 PM ET).
  • Cyprus. Greek Cypriot and Turkish Cypriot leaders agreed to open new border crossings and hold future talks (Reuters, 3:50 PM ET).
  • Migrants. An X news summary citing Reuters says hundreds of mostly Honduran migrants formed a caravan heading north from Tapachula, Mexico; I didn’t see the Reuters post itself (X news summary).
  • Vatican and UK. Pope Leo met Argentine clergy abuse survivors (Reuters, 6:45 PM ET), and Russell Brand was sentenced to 10 and a half years for rape and sexual assault (AP, 4:30 PM ET).
