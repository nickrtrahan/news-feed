World News Brief: Nov 14, AM

Top line

This morning's lead: Reuters sources say Trump has told advisers he may hold off on new attacks on Iran unless Tehran provokes him again. Meanwhile, Iran now says three oil tankers caught fire after hitting mines in the Strait of Hormuz. On Ukraine, Zelensky says US officials asked for the first time whether Ukraine would leave the Donbas in exchange for NATO membership. The White House says next week's Trump–Xi–Putin summit in Shenzhen will focus on the Iran war, not Ukraine. In the US, flights are resuming after Friday's nationwide ground stop, which disrupted more than 1.5 million passengers. All times are ET. Coverage runs from about 7 PM Friday to 7 AM Saturday, Nov 14.

Iran and Israel–Iran

  • Trump may pause strikes. According to sources, Trump has told advisers he may hold off on attacks on Iran unless there are new provocations (Reuters, 6:10 AM ET). A US official says Iran will be on the agenda when Trump meets Xi and Putin in China next week (AP, 7:12 PM ET Fri).
  • Three tankers on fire in Hormuz (Iranian claim). Iran's navy and the Revolutionary Guards (IRGC) now say fires broke out on a total of three oil tankers that struck mines in the strait, where Tehran has restricted navigation (AFP, 5:45 AM ET). Last night, the IRGC put the number at two (Reuters, 9:40 PM ET Fri). Unconfirmed: the ships' names, casualties and damage have not been independently verified. According to the UN, at least 25 seafarers have been killed in and around the strait since the war began in February (Al Jazeera, 3:17 AM ET).
  • Iran vs. the IAEA. Iran's mission in Vienna attacked IAEA chief Rafael Grossi. It said he relied on "technically and legally flawed" resolutions and that US and Israeli strikes have made inspections impossible (Al Jazeera, 4:19 AM ET). This follows Friday's IAEA report flagging concern about an uninspected enrichment site at Isfahan.
  • Clash at the UN. According to a summary of posts on X, the US and Iranian envoys traded blame over Gulf shipping at the UN Security Council. The US accused Iran of attacking ships with missiles, drones and mines, while Iran said the US and Israel started the wars (X News summary). Unconfirmed: I haven't found a wire-service report of this session yet.
  • Threat to undersea cables (unconfirmed). Posts on X claim Iranian officials plan to target undersea internet cables in Hormuz if US and Israeli strikes resume. Experts quoted there note that less than 1% of global bandwidth passes through the strait, so the damage would mostly be regional, hitting places like Iraq, Kuwait, Bahrain and Qatar (X News summary). No wire service has confirmed this.

Russia–Ukraine

  • NATO for Donbas? Zelensky says US officials asked in recent talks whether Ukraine would withdraw from the Donbas in exchange for NATO membership. He said it was the first time this had been raised (Kyiv Independent, 6:14 AM ET). He added that any concrete package would be put to the Ukrainian public and would not legally recognize Russia's occupation (Kyiv Post, 4:45 AM ET).
  • Moscow's terms. Zelensky says Putin rejected an energy ceasefire. He also dismissed Russia's proposed "humanitarian" truce at Kostiantynivka as a ploy to take the city (Kyiv Post, 5:24 AM ET). In talks with US envoys in Moscow, Russia revived its late-2021 demands that NATO pull back to its 1997 borders and stop military aid to Ukraine. Poland's Tusk called that a direct threat to European security (Kyiv Post, 4:41 AM ET). ISW says the Kremlin's conditions for letting Europe take part in talks amount to NATO's dissolution (ISW, 9:35 PM ET Fri). Kyiv Post cites analysts who warn that by agreeing to settle every issue at once, Witkoff and Kushner have given Putin room to stall (Kyiv Post, 5:17 AM ET).
  • Possible record prisoner swap. After trilateral talks in Abu Dhabi, the White House confirmed that Witkoff and Kushner are preparing what could be the largest prisoner-of-war exchange of the war (Kyiv Post, 4:08 AM ET).
  • Summit not about Ukraine. The White House says the first-ever three-way meeting of the US, Russian and Chinese leaders will happen on the sidelines of APEC in Shenzhen next week (AFP, 6:15 AM ET), but it will not focus on Ukraine (Kyiv Independent, 9:16 PM ET Fri).
  • Strikes inside Russia. Kyiv Post reports that Ukrainian forces hit the Samara oil export hub and the Lipetsk-2 military airbase on Friday. The strikes set crude storage tanks on fire and hit an aviation fuel reservoir, a hangar and a command post (Kyiv Post, 6:08 AM ET). Russia has not confirmed the damage.
  • Starlink/Starshield dispute. ISW says Ukrainian officials are still reporting Starshield outages that badly hurt drone interception and strikes in occupied areas (ISW, 11:00 PM ET Fri). Zelensky said a 161 km/h Starshield speed limit in occupied areas is getting in the way of interceptor drones. Democratic senators told Elon Musk to preserve SpaceX records on Ukraine's Starlink access (Reuters, 11:40 PM ET Fri) by Nov 30 (Kyiv Post, 2:00 AM ET).
  • Also: Ukraine is racing to repair power plants wrecked by Russian missiles before winter (Reuters, 10:30 PM ET Fri). Ukraine and Azerbaijan signed contracts under a "Drone Deal" (Kyiv Post, 3:37 AM ET). France's Marine Le Pen says she is willing to meet Zelensky (Kyiv Post, 2:33 AM ET).

Gaza and the wider region

  • Gaza. Officials at Al-Shifa hospital say an Israeli drone strike in Jabalia, northern Gaza, killed at least two Palestinians (Al Jazeera, 6:02 AM ET). The UN says Israel could have prevented much of the storm damage by letting in enough aid (Al Jazeera, 12:00 AM ET).
  • West Bank. Al Jazeera reports that Israeli settlers attacked Palestinian olive pickers near Susiya in the southern Hebron hills (Al Jazeera, 5:30 AM ET).
  • Yemen. Yemen's government accuses the Houthis of tightening their siege of Taiz, and health officials warn of a hidden disease crisis (Al Jazeera, 3:02 AM ET).
  • Qatar–Turkey pact. The two countries welcomed a new mutual defense agreement that commits each to a common stance against any outside attack on the other (Al Jazeera, 4:56 AM ET).

Other world news

  • US flights resuming. Flights are restarting after a communications outage grounded departures nationwide for several hours on Friday, but authorities say getting back to normal will take time (AFP, 4:45 AM ET). More than 1.5 million passengers were affected (Al Jazeera, 3:31 AM ET). Reuters says the shutdown exposes a deepening crisis in the air traffic network (Reuters, 4:45 AM ET).
  • Trump in Asia. Trump is starting an Asia-Pacific trip as ASEAN leaders meet in Manila (Al Jazeera, 2:24 AM ET). Sources say Quad leaders will meet in Manila on Tuesday (Reuters, 5:20 AM ET). Chinese and US officials discussed tariffs and farm trade ahead of the Xi–Trump meeting (Reuters, 6:30 AM ET). Canada's Carney says trade talks with the US are making progress (Reuters, 5:35 AM ET).
  • Migrant caravan. A caravan of more than 1,000 people is moving toward the US from southern Mexico, and Trump has threatened to double tariffs on Mexico (Al Jazeera, 5:07 AM ET). On Friday, Mexico's Sheinbaum said it was not heading for the US.
  • Measles. The WSJ reports that the US has lost its measles-free status because the disease kept spreading (Reuters, 5:45 AM ET).
  • Europe. Investigators in Hungary recovered about $20 million in hidden cash, some of it buried in forests, in a probe linked to former Orban minister Miklos Sesztak (Reuters, 6:40 AM ET; Al Jazeera, 3:53 AM ET). France faces more protests today after more than a month of school blockades and cost-of-living demonstrations (AFP, 5:15 AM ET). Germany's parliament gave its intelligence agencies new powers to hack and disrupt adversaries (Al Jazeera, 3:42 AM ET).
  • Africa. South Sudan has pushed its first national elections back from December 22 to February (AFP, 4:08 AM ET).
  • Also: Tesla is inviting select customers to buy the Cybercab for personal use (Reuters, 4:40 AM ET). A Philippine court ordered the arrest of preacher Apollo Quiboloy, who is wanted in the US, a step toward possible extradition (Al Jazeera, 1:53 AM ET). The UN climate chief urged faster action as COP31 confronts the divide over fossil fuels (Reuters, 5:00 AM ET).
