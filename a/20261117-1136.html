World News Brief: Nov 17, AM

Top line

Iran's Revolutionary Guards say drones attacked a US military radar in Bahrain overnight. Bahrain says it shot the drones down. The IRGC also says two more ships hit mines and caught fire in the Strait of Hormuz. A CNN report says Iran may be planning a "dirty bomb" attack in Washington. Trump warned Iran against any attack inside the US, and Tehran called the report "baseless." In Ukraine, a section of the Crimean Bridge collapsed after a Ukrainian missile strike overnight, hours after Russian missiles hit two of Kyiv's main bridges. All times are ET. Coverage runs from about 6:30 PM Monday, Nov 16, to 6:35 AM Tuesday, Nov 17.

Iran and Israel–Iran

  • Drone attack on US radar in Bahrain. Reuters reports that Iran attacked a US military radar system in Bahrain with drones (Reuters, 5:30 AM ET). An X News summary says the IRGC named Sheikh Isa Air Base as the target and called it retaliation for US drone flights over Iran. The same summary says Bahrain's Defence Force intercepted the drones and reported no damage. This would be Iran's third claim of this kind in five days. Unconfirmed: whether anything was hit.
  • More ships hit in Hormuz. The IRGC navy says two more ships caught fire after hitting mines in the Strait of Hormuz, one in the western part of the waterway and one in the southern part (Al Jazeera, 5:10 AM ET). Iran's army spokesperson warned ships "not to be fooled" into using what he called "fake American routes" through the strait, and pointed to the danger of mines, fire, and explosions (Al Jazeera, 3:40 AM ET). The ship counts come from Iran and I haven't seen them independently confirmed. Overnight Al Jazeera also reported that the Houthis claim control of the Bab al-Mandeb Strait (Al Jazeera, 7:28 PM ET). Reuters ran Houthi video it says shows destroyed vehicles belonging to Saudi-backed forces near that strait (Reuters, 1:05 AM ET).
  • "Dirty bomb" report. According to Al Jazeera, CNN reported new intelligence suggesting Iran may be planning a "dirty bomb" detonation in Washington, DC. Trump warned Iran against carrying out any attack inside the US (Al Jazeera, 6:18 AM ET). Iran's Foreign Ministry spokesperson Esmaeil Baghaei called the allegation "baseless" (Al Jazeera, 6:28 AM ET). Unconfirmed: the intelligence behind the CNN report hasn't been made public.
  • Diplomacy. Qatar's foreign ministry spokesperson Majed al-Ansari says mediation is still going on, and a meeting with Iranian officials will happen "in the coming hours" (Al Jazeera, 5:40 AM ET). In Muscat, the foreign ministers of Oman and Iran discussed protecting sea lanes (Reuters, 12:50 AM ET). An X News summary says that before the Oman trip, Araghchi also met Pakistan's army chief Asim Munir in Rawalpindi and announced no new agreements (X News summary). The UK prime minister said Britain is backing mediators working toward a deal to end the war, reopen Hormuz, and stop Iran from building a nuclear weapon (Al Jazeera, 7:30 PM ET).
  • Washington. Vice President JD Vance told Fox News that Trump won't let the Iran conflict become a "forever war" and is looking at options to end it. Vance also acknowledged the war's political cost in gas and energy prices (Reuters, 4:55 AM ET; Al Jazeera, 4:30 AM ET). A new Reuters/Ipsos poll found that just 31% of Americans support the war (Al Jazeera, 9:12 PM ET).
  • Energy fears. Al Jazeera says the new tanker attacks and Monday's drone alert at Iraq's Khor Mor gas field are raising concern about the safety of energy infrastructure across the region (Al Jazeera, 1:40 AM ET).
  • Israel politics. AFP says Israel's parliament is set to pick a new Knesset speaker today who opposes Prime Minister Netanyahu (AFP, 6:30 AM ET). The Times of Israel reports that the ultra-Orthodox party UTJ won't back Matan Kahana for the post (Times of Israel, 2:31 AM ET). Separately, the UK sanctioned West Bank settlement construction companies and an activist group that blocks aid to Gaza (Times of Israel, 6:11 AM ET).

Russia–Ukraine

  • Crimean Bridge section collapses. A section of the Crimean Bridge collapsed after a Ukrainian Flamingo missile strike overnight. Zelensky says it was the last step of a "seven-phase operation" carried out over several weeks (Kyiv Independent, 5:22 AM ET; update, 4:47 AM ET). AFP says Ukraine has now hit Moscow's link to Crimea two nights in a row (AFP, 3:09 AM ET). ISW says Ukrainian strikes in southern Russia and near the Black and Azov Seas probably supported the attacks on the bridge (ISW, 9:15 PM ET). Russia's account of the damage wasn't in my search.
  • Kyiv bridges hit. Late Monday, Russian missiles struck Kyiv's Northern and Southern bridges over the Dnipro. Mayor Vitali Klitschko says the strikes caused water and power cuts (Reuters, 1:50 AM ET; Reuters, 9:40 PM ET). Kyiv Independent reporters described some of the loudest blasts in months. Both bridges are still closed while specialists inspect them (Kyiv Independent, 3:21 AM ET).
  • Aid and allies. Germany pledged $300 million for weapons for Ukraine, mainly PAC-2 and PAC-3 Patriot missiles (Kyiv Independent, 5:21 AM ET). Estonia plans to send more than 70 locomotives to keep Ukraine's railways running (Kyiv Independent, 9:19 PM ET). Soldiers from an 11-nation force led by the UK and France tested drone defenses in Poland, part of preparations to support Ukraine after the war ends (Reuters, 12:55 AM ET).
  • Diplomacy. Finnish President Alexander Stubb urged European leaders to talk directly with Putin, calling reliance on the US "irresponsible" (Kyiv Independent, 4:45 AM ET). Lithuania's foreign minister warned Europe not to fall for Russia's "traps" in peace talks (Kyiv Independent, 3:56 AM ET). AFP says these strikes come ahead of a Xi-Trump-Putin summit (AFP, 3:09 AM ET).
  • Starshield problems. ISW says Ukrainian officials keep reporting speed limits on drones that use Starshield. SpaceX reportedly acknowledged the problem and called it a technical issue (ISW, 12:45 AM ET).
  • Nuclear risk. Reuters carried the warning from regulators in 38 countries that the risk of a nuclear accident in Ukraine is rising this winter because of Russian attacks on the power grid (Reuters, 5:20 AM ET).

Other world news

  • US–China chip fight. The Trump administration is closing loopholes that let Chinese firms get advanced AI chips through data centers in Southeast Asia, ahead of talks with China (Reuters, 5:45 AM ET; BBC, 5:24 AM ET). Shares in the region fell (Reuters, 6:10 AM ET).
  • Taiwan. In a Reuters exclusive, the US is pressing a UN agency over Taiwan flight restrictions, and China has hit back (Reuters, 5:40 AM ET).
  • Canada–China. Xi met Prime Minister Carney before the APEC summit, and the two countries extended trade deals (Reuters, 5:45 AM ET).
  • Sudan. The UN says drones account for nearly three-quarters of Sudan's war deaths this year (Reuters, 4:45 AM ET).
  • Protests. Garment workers in Gazipur, Bangladesh, blocked a major road as their protests over wages continue (Reuters, 4:55 AM ET). In Belgium, students protested tuition hikes and education cuts (Reuters, 5:13 AM ET).
  • Indonesia. Political turmoil is adding to market worries in Indonesia (Reuters, 6:35 AM ET).
