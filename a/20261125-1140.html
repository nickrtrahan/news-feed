World News Brief: Nov 25, AM

Covering roughly 6:30 PM Tuesday to 6:40 AM ET Wednesday, Nov. 25, 2026. Times are ET. Claims not independently confirmed are labeled.

Iran and Israel–Iran

Hormuz standoff hardens. An Iranian diplomat said Iran will not let ships through the Strait of Hormuz while the U.S. naval blockade stays in place (Al Jazeera, 4:35 AM). Iran's army spokesman said the military will watch the strait more strictly and told shipowners to coordinate with it for safe passage (Al Jazeera, 5:19 AM; Iran International, 3:18 AM). The IRGC navy said two more tankers using an "unauthorized" route hit naval mines, causing engine-room fires (Iran International, 11:44 PM). Iran's army also claimed it shot down a U.S. MQ-9 drone over the eastern strait; this is Iranian state media's claim and is not confirmed by the U.S. (Al Jazeera, 11:25 PM).

Tehran downplays the blockade. Iran's first vice president said Tehran is not worried about the blockade because cargo through other borders has risen sharply, and that the government is trying to rein in prices (Reuters, 3:31 AM). A deputy parliament speaker warned that if war resumes, Iran's response would be "neither limited nor short-term" (Iran International, 2:42 AM). Oil prices fell more than 1% on supply and surplus expectations despite the tanker incidents (Al Jazeera, 6:21 AM).

New missile variants. The Institute for the Study of War said Iran appears to be developing ballistic missiles with stronger propulsion and maneuvering warheads to improve accuracy and evade interception; how many it can mass-produce is unclear (ISW, 9:36 PM; Iran International).

Washington and Jerusalem. Trump told the New York Post the Iran war is funded through the end of January and warned Democrats against ending it early (Iran International, 8:27 PM). After Defense Minister Israel Katz said Iran is developing chemical weapons and plotting to kill Trump (Haaretz), opposition figure Avigdor Liberman said such claims give no basis for an immediate Israeli strike (Haaretz, 3:45 AM). Katz's intelligence claims have not been made public and remain unconfirmed.

Ekbatan protesters: executions reportedly halted, sentences stand. Activist and family accounts said the planned dawn executions of Milad Armoon and Navid Najjaran at Ghezel Hesar Prison were suspended after protests at home and abroad (unconfirmed by Iranian authorities). The judiciary's Mizan agency said their death sentences have not been overturned (Iran International, 12:48 AM; Iran International, 6:00 AM). U.S. UN Ambassador Mike Waltz condemned the flogging of singer Parastoo Ahmadi over a hijab-free online concert as "pure medieval" (Iran International).

Huawei convicted. A federal jury in Brooklyn convicted Huawei and three subsidiaries on charges including evading U.S. sanctions on Iran, bank and wire fraud, and trade-secret theft conspiracy (Reuters, 7:20 PM).

Russia–Ukraine

Gap over security guarantees. U.S. negotiators have privately ruled out sending troops or building bases in post-war Ukraine as part of security guarantees, per people familiar with the talks. Zelensky worries guarantees without foreign troops would not deter another Russian attack (Kyiv Independent, 3:34 AM; Kyiv Independent).

Patriot delays. ISW said the U.S. continues to delay delivery of Western-funded Patriot interceptors, the only ones that can reliably stop Russian ballistic missiles, as Russia intensifies strikes (ISW, 8:43 PM; ISW). ISW also noted Putin acknowledged that Ukraine's strikes on Russian oil infrastructure have placed a heavy financial burden on the government (ISW).

Russia's own data: ~500,000 war deaths. Leaked Rosstat records, verified by Mediazona and Meduza, show nearly 500,000 registered military deaths by late September 2026 (BBC, 7:59 PM).

Overnight strikes and politics. A Russian attack killed a man and damaged commercial buildings in Kyiv Oblast's Bila Tserkva district overnight (Kyiv Independent, 12:25 AM). EU countries agree on backing Ukraine but remain split on how to fund it ahead of a key summit (Kyiv Independent). Putin removed filmmaker Alexander Sokurov from his human rights council (Kyiv Independent, 6:15 AM). Ukraine's parliament passed a crypto tax bill expected to raise about $300 million a year (Kyiv Independent, 6:39 AM).

Other conflicts

Yemen–Saudi escalation. At least 11 people were wounded in the second Houthi strike on Riyadh in a week; the Houthis claimed the attack on a financial-district tower and said they also targeted the Ras Tanura refinery (Haaretz, 4:15 AM; Iran International). The Saudi-led coalition ordered evacuation of Hodeidah port, and the Houthis threatened Jeddah and other Saudi Red Sea ports in return (Al Jazeera, 7:36 PM). Houthi-affiliated media reported airstrikes on Hodeidah airport and Saada (Al Jazeera; Al Jazeera).

Gaza and Lebanon. An Israeli strike in Shati camp killed a senior Palestinian security official, reported to be a Hamas official, along with his wife and two daughters, per Palestinian authorities (Al Jazeera, 2:06 AM; Haaretz). MSF said Israeli attacks in southern Lebanon have killed and hurt civilians, including children and health workers (Al Jazeera, 6:27 AM).

Sudan. RSF suicide-drone strikes on Ed Damazin and Roseires in Blue Nile killed at least 14 civilians, a local civil society group said; other local tallies are higher (Sudan Tribune, 4:10 AM). Sudan's government rejected Ethiopia's offer to host peace talks, calling it not neutral (Sudan Tribune).
