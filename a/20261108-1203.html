World News Brief: Nov 8, AM

Top line

Iran’s judiciary said it executed three men arrested during January protests in Karaj, the IRGC said three oil tankers caught fire in the Strait of Hormuz early Sunday, and the UK’s maritime warning center separately reported a tanker struck by an unknown projectile and another on fire near Oman. Israel’s defense minister said the mission against Iran’s nuclear threat is “not yet complete.” In Ukraine, Zelenskyy said Russia hit energy infrastructure overnight, leaving more than 600,000 people without power in the Dnipro region, and that an FPV drone struck a village where rescuers are still searching after a bombing that killed 55. Houthi attacks on Saudi Arabia damaged an Aramco building in Riyadh with falling debris; no injuries were reported. Elsewhere, Poland mourned six people killed in a Krakow shooting. Covers roughly 7 PM Saturday through 7 AM ET Sunday, Nov. 8.

Iran and Israel–Iran

  • Executions. Iran’s judiciary said early Sunday that it had executed Hassan Divband, Hamid Kazemi, and Reza Zanganeh after the Supreme Court upheld their death sentences. The three were arrested during January protests in Karaj, west of Tehran, and had also been ordered to forfeit their assets. Authorities accused them of taking part in operations for Israel, the United States, and “hostile groups,” and of attacking public and government buildings, damaging electricity infrastructure, and disrupting services during protests on January 8 and 9. The judiciary said Divband was arrested on January 11 after a report from the Intelligence Ministry’s Alborz office and accused him of setting fire to public property; ten other people were later arrested on accusations of participating in the protests and collaborating with him. These are the judiciary’s allegations (Iran International, 11:39 PM ET Saturday).
  • Hormuz tankers. The IRGC Navy said three oil tankers caught fire early Sunday along what it called an “unauthorized route” in the Strait of Hormuz, and that smoke had spread across the waterway. It blamed the ships for switching off their navigation and identification systems and “accompanying the US military,” and warned that vessels that defy Iran’s transit rules and cooperate with the US military “will have no fate other than this.” The statement gave no names, flags, cargo details, or casualty information, and there is no independent confirmation of the cause; treat this as Iran’s account (Iran International, 5:51 AM ET). Separately, the UK Maritime Trade Operations center said Sunday, citing a company security officer, that a tanker was struck by an unknown projectile while sailing outbound through the strait on Saturday; all crew were safe and no environmental impact was reported. UKMTO also said, citing military authorities, that a tanker was on fire and its crew evacuated 8 nautical miles northwest of Khasab, Oman, on Saturday, near the strait. Both were delayed reports; UKMTO advised ships to transit with caution while authorities investigate. It is not clear from the reporting whether these are the same vessels described by the IRGC (projectile, 6:21 AM ET; Khasab fire, 4:25 AM ET).
  • Blockade and signaling. IRGC spokesman Hossein Mohebi said Sunday that the US plan to restrict overland routes for weapons and sanctioned goods would fail, calling Task Force Iron Gate a plan that “will remain only on paper.” He said earlier US efforts to impose naval, air, and land blockades had also failed (Iran International, 4:17 AM ET). Armed forces spokesman Abolfazl Shekarchi described President Trump’s political and election setbacks as evidence of a broader defeat against Iran rather than ordinary domestic politics, and said US military pressure had reached a dead end (Iran International, 2:47 AM ET). Lawmaker Ebrahim Azizi, a member of parliament’s national security committee, said US midterm results would not change Iran’s nuclear strategy and warned that any US or Israeli action would bring a severe response (Iran International, 2:10 AM ET). Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth, in a video message, told US troops to “stay the course,” “ignore the noise,” and finish “what we have started” (Iran International, 10:30 PM ET Saturday). A senior Emirati official, Ali Rashid Al Nuaimi, told Fox News Digital he saw some progress in US–Iran talks through Qatari mediation but said renewed confrontation remained possible: “We plan for the worst. We hope for the best” (Iran International, 9:39 PM ET Saturday). Gulf stock markets opened Sunday lower as investors waited for signs of whether Trump would order fresh attacks on Iran (Reuters, 5:30 AM ET).
  • Katz on Iran. Israeli Defense Minister Israel Katz said Israel’s mission against Iran’s nuclear threat was “not yet complete,” warning that Tehran was renewing efforts to acquire nuclear weapons and carrying out other activities that threaten Israel. Speaking at a memorial for soldiers killed in Operation Kadesh, he said Israel would not accept those threats and would follow a proactive, offensive policy. He also warned that if Hezbollah joined an Iranian attack on Israel, it would “suffer a severe blow and lose additional territory,” according to the Israeli military spokesperson (Iran International, 5:45 AM ET; more, 6:33 AM ET).
  • Israel’s next government. President Isaac Herzog has said he will task opposition leader Gadi Eisenkot with forming the next government after last week’s election, a step Netanyahu’s rivals see as the start of an effort to replace him. The formal mandate was due later Sunday. The Times of Israel reported that Ra’am was willing to vote for an Eisenkot-led government even without a coalition deal (AFP, 4:59 AM ET; Times of Israel, 2:45 AM ET).
  • Domestic pressure. About 8 million people in Iran face extreme poverty and malnutrition, and up to 50 million more struggle to afford basic goods and meet nutritional needs, parliament’s social affairs committee chairman Ali Babai Karamani said Sunday. He said a four-person household needs at least 1 billion rials a month—about $376 at the rate he cited—to maintain a “normal life” (Iran International, 6:16 AM ET). Several Iranian media outlets published footage described as showing a missile fuel test at a school in Chaharmahal and Bakhtiari province in which part of the roof collapsed; that description has not been independently confirmed (Iran International, 5:46 AM ET). Thick smoke rose from the Tehran oil refinery on Sunday morning; officials attributed it to electrical and technical problems that activated flare systems and said no fire had broken out (Iran International, 3:43 AM ET; more, 3:18 AM ET). A group of hardline women wearing white burial shrouds rallied outside IRGC headquarters in Tehran, accusing the armed forces of defying Supreme Leader Mojtaba Khamenei and demanding retaliation over threats against him. Tabnak, a site linked to security chief Mohsen Rezaei, published commentary rebuking the demonstrators (Iran International, 9:31 PM ET Saturday). The Tehran-backed Iraqi group Kataib Sayyid al-Shuhada denied on Saturday that it had agreed to surrender its weapons, rejecting remarks by Iraqi Foreign Minister Fuad Hussein; it said a “sovereignty agreement” it signed did not include handing over its arsenal (Iran International, 7:54 PM ET Saturday).

Russia–Ukraine

  • Energy and Komyshuvakha. Zelenskyy said Russia struck energy infrastructure overnight in the Dnipro region, leaving more than 600,000 people in Dnipro and the surrounding region without power, and that 140 apartment buildings in the Zhytomyr region lost heating after a Russian strike. He said there were widespread outages across the country and that Kharkiv was also under strain. This morning, he said, Russian forces hit Komyshuvakha with an FPV drone at the site where rescue operations have continued for seven days after an aerial-bomb strike that killed 55 people, with more people possibly still under the rubble. Zelenskyy said Russia launched 115 missiles of various types, nearly 1,900 guided aerial bombs, and more than 3,000 attack drones against Ukrainian cities and communities in the past week alone, including many ballistic missiles manufactured this year, and called for a strong 22nd EU sanctions package aimed at missile production (Zelenskyy, 4:46 AM ET).
  • Overnight toll. The Kyiv Independent reported that Russian attacks killed 5 people and injured 10 across Ukraine over the past day. In Zaporizhzhia Oblast, Governor Ivan Fedorov said Russian forces carried out 725 strikes on 53 settlements, injuring one person (Kyiv Independent, 4:05 AM ET).
  • Recruitment claim and Kyiv politics. Moscow police detained more than 200 people, and detainees told the Russian Telegram channel Ostorozhno, Novosti that those over 18 were “persistently” urged to sign a contract with Russia’s Defense Ministry; that account has not been independently confirmed (Kyiv Independent, 6:08 AM ET). Zelenskyy said more personnel decisions were coming at the Security Service of Ukraine after senior officials were dismissed for alleged involvement with criminal gangs (Kyiv Independent, 12:16 AM ET). Sen. Richard Blumenthal said Saturday he would introduce legislation supporting Ukraine, called on Washington to assign new negotiators to peace talks, and urged Congress to impose public-interest controls on Starlink and Starshield (Kyiv Independent, 9:03 PM ET Saturday).

Other conflicts

  • Yemen and Saudi Arabia. Loud booms were heard in Riyadh shortly after Saudi Civil Defense issued an early-warning alert, a Reuters witness said. The Saudi-led coalition said it intercepted two ballistic missiles launched toward Riyadh by Yemen’s Houthis, later reported intercepting two cruise missiles in Riyadh and the south, and said it destroyed a Houthi drone headed toward the southern region. It had earlier said it intercepted a ballistic missile toward the Eastern Province. Saudi Arabia’s Energy Ministry said an Aramco administrative building in Riyadh was damaged by falling debris after an attempted attack, with no injuries or deaths. A Reuters witness saw windows blown out across several floors of the Aramco Tower; the ministry described the damage as limited. No link between the interceptions and that damage was confirmed. An earlier Reuters report, citing a witness and three people briefed on the matter, said a tower in Riyadh’s King Abdullah Financial District was damaged by a projectile whose origin was not identified (Reuters, 12:40 AM ET; intercepts, 1:30 AM ET; Aramco, 5:20 AM ET; Iran International, 5:17 AM ET; Eastern Province, 10:14 PM ET Saturday). Houthi-run health authorities raised Saturday’s death toll from airstrikes they blamed on Saudi Arabia near Sanaa to 33 killed and 39 wounded, up from the 27 deaths reported earlier; Saudi Arabia has not been independently confirmed as the attacker in the reporting cited here (Reuters, 7:10 PM ET Saturday; Iran International, 7:00 PM ET Saturday).

Other world news

  • Poland. Krakow’s Main Market Square remained sealed off Sunday as mourners brought candles for six people killed by a gunman who authorities said was “fascinated by far-right ideologies.” A British citizen was among the dead. Reuters said the 24-year-old suspect’s parents and sister were later found dead at their home, and that he remained in hospital in critical condition (AFP, 4:31 AM ET; BBC, 3:17 AM ET; Reuters, 5:35 AM ET).
  • New Zealand. The country faces weeks or even months of political wrangling after a knife-edge election that produced no clear winner (AFP, 11:02 PM ET Saturday).
  • Argentina. Pope Leo headed Sunday to Argentina on the second leg of a 12-day South America tour, where he is due to speak to President Javier Milei amid tensions over austerity and Iran (Reuters, 5:45 AM ET).
  • Climate talks. Ahead of COP31 in Turkey, Reuters reported that Turkish villagers are demanding that conference leaders see for themselves the devastation caused by coal (Reuters, 6:20 AM ET).
