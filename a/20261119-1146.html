World News Brief: Nov 19, AM

Top line

Trump, Xi and Putin opened their first three-way summit in Shenzhen. Trump called Putin his "friend," and the three leaders signed a "declaration of peace" that the Kyiv Independent says largely sidesteps Ukraine. On Iran, the Times of Israel reports that Trump is weighing another offensive while Netanyahu pushes a 4- to 7-day campaign. Iran's Revolutionary Guard (IRGC) navy says two more supertankers are burning after hitting mines in the Strait of Hormuz. Iranian officials say mediators are passing messages between Tehran and Washington. Ukraine hit the Crimean Bridge again overnight and halted rail traffic across it, and the UK pledged 1,000 long-range strike drones to Kyiv. The UN's top court ruled that Myanmar committed genocide against the Rohingya. All times are ET. Coverage runs from about 6:40 PM Wednesday to 6:45 AM Thursday, Nov 19.

Iran and Israel–Iran

  • Another offensive under discussion. The Times of Israel reports that Trump is weighing another offensive on Iran, as Netanyahu pushes for a 4- to 7-day campaign (Times of Israel, 1:40 AM ET). Trump also claimed there is an "indication" that Iran is pursuing biological weapons (Times of Israel, 2:17 AM ET). He has not presented public evidence for this claim. On Wednesday evening, Trump said he would make the same decision to go to war with Iran again. He called negotiations with Tehran a "waste of time" (Al Jazeera, 9:44 PM ET) and said Washington doesn't need help from China or Russia to end the war (Al Jazeera, 8:19 PM ET).
  • Back-channel messages. Behnam Saeedi, a senior member of the Iranian parliament's national security committee, told Iranian media that mediators are passing messages between Iran and the US (Al Jazeera, 3:48 AM ET). This comes from the Iranian side, and neither Washington nor the mediators have confirmed it.
  • Iran on the Shenzhen agenda. In a one-on-one meeting with Xi, Trump said the two countries could work together on Iran (AP, 1:03 AM ET). Trump also said the US and China both reject Iran having nuclear weapons (Times of Israel, 3:28 AM ET).
  • Hormuz: more tankers reported burning. The IRGC navy says two supertankers caught fire after hitting naval mines in the southern Strait of Hormuz (Al Jazeera, 5:59 AM ET). This follows Iran's claim Wednesday afternoon that three tankers were burning. Both claims come from Iran's military and have not been independently confirmed. ISW says Iran is attacking US military infrastructure in the region almost daily in response to continued US strikes near the strait, and it keeps attacking commercial ships while claiming they are hitting mines (ISW, 11:09 PM ET). Al Jazeera says it is "too early" to judge the effect of the US blockade on Iran, and Tehran remains defiant (Al Jazeera, 3:17 AM ET).
  • Energy shock. The International Energy Agency (IEA) called the Middle East conflict and the disruption at Hormuz "the largest energy supply shock in history" (Al Jazeera, 3:11 AM ET). The IEA projects that countries' energy-security response could cut oil demand by 4% by 2035 (Reuters, 5:10 AM ET).
  • Houthis and Saudi Arabia. The Saudi-led coalition says it destroyed a ballistic missile launch platform in Yemen's Saada province (Al Jazeera, 7:21 PM ET). It later said it dealt with a Houthi missile fired toward Riyadh after a loud boom was heard in the capital, and that the threat caused damage (Reuters, 10:30 PM ET; 12:04 AM ET).

Russia–Ukraine

  • Crimean Bridge hit again. Sergey Aksyonov, the Moscow-installed head of occupied Crimea, confirmed that the bridge was hit again just after midnight local time, only days after a devastating earlier strike (Kyiv Independent, 7:06 PM ET). Russia's Transportation Ministry halted train traffic across the bridge (Kyiv Independent, 12:31 AM ET). Ukraine's Defense Ministry confirmed the strike used Neptune missiles (Kyiv Independent, 6:24 AM ET). ISW says the bridge strikes are part of a wider Ukrainian effort to cut Russian supply lines across the front (ISW, 10:30 PM ET).
  • Russian strikes. Russian attacks across Ukraine killed four people and injured 36 over the past day, including an 11-year-old boy and a 12-year-old girl. Overnight, Russia launched Onyx anti-ship missiles and 98 drones (Kyiv Independent, 5:19 AM ET). ISW says civilian casualties keep rising and that ongoing Starshield speed restrictions are hampering Ukraine's air defense (ISW, 9:14 PM ET).
  • UK drones. Britain will give Ukraine 1,000 long-range strike drones (BBC, 4:13 AM ET).
  • Shenzhen summit. Before the meeting, Trump told Xi that both of them should help end the war in Ukraine (Reuters, 2:35 AM ET). Greeting Putin, Trump said, "Who is he? He's my friend" (AP; Reuters, 4:10 AM ET). The three-way talks began around 3:40 AM ET, with Trump saying "we are really making history" (AFP, 3:40 AM ET). Trump said he hopes Putin "would like to see an end of that war" (Kyiv Independent, 4:08 AM ET). In the talks he called for nuclear arms reductions and criticized the International Criminal Court, which has an arrest warrant out for Putin (Al Jazeera, 5:53 AM ET). The three leaders signed a "declaration of peace" on UN reform, energy supplies, international law and nuclear security. According to the Kyiv Independent, the summit largely sidestepped Ukraine (Kyiv Independent, 6:21 AM ET).
  • Other items. A Pew poll of more than 10,000 US adults found that two-thirds disapprove of Trump's handling of the war (Al Jazeera, 5:44 AM ET). NGOs say Russia and the US are blocking a binding agreement on lethal autonomous weapons (AFP, 3:08 AM ET).

Other conflicts

  • Gaza. AFP reports that Israel's army launched a ground operation in a Hamas-controlled area of Gaza. A Gaza security source described intense artillery shelling north of Nuseirat refugee camp and Israeli military vehicles in central Gaza (AFP, 11:54 PM ET).
  • Lebanon. Lebanon's National News Agency says Israeli forces set off a large explosion in the southern town of Qantara (Al Jazeera, 6:02 AM ET).
  • Venezuela. Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth made a surprise visit to Caracas, the highest-level US visit since Maduro was ousted in January, and then went on to Colombia (AFP, 5:50 AM ET).

Top world news

  • Rohingya genocide ruling. The International Court of Justice ruled in The Gambia's case that Myanmar committed genocide against the Rohingya (AFP, 6:43 AM ET). Al Jazeera says the court found "genocidal intent" in the 2017 military operations that killed 10,000 Rohingya (Al Jazeera, 5:52 AM ET).
  • US systems disruptions. The top US intelligence official said last week's disruptions to critical systems were not a coordinated or state-sponsored attack. The administration is facing growing criticism over its response (Reuters, 10:30 PM ET).
  • APEC. Japan's Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi got a muted welcome on her first visit to China, after her comments on Taiwan last year strained ties (Reuters, 6:35 AM ET).
  • UN leadership. Reuters says the UN could soon be without a leader because the race to succeed António Guterres has stalled (Reuters, 6:45 AM ET).
  • Migrant boat. An NGO says dozens of people are missing after a migrant boat capsized off Senegal's coast (Reuters, 5:50 AM ET).
  • Markets. Anthropic starts trading on Nasdaq today. AFP says it priced its IPO at $83 a share, above the expected range, setting a new record (AFP, 6:00 AM ET).
