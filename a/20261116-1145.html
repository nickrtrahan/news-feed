World News Brief: Nov 16, AM

Top line

Overnight, Iran's Revolutionary Guard said it captured an intact US drone near the Strait of Hormuz, as a third US aircraft carrier, the USS Theodore Roosevelt, arrived in the region. Tehran says UN inspectors can't visit its damaged nuclear sites until the war ends. In Russia–Ukraine, Moscow says Ukraine launched a record number of drones at Russia overnight, with Moscow Oblast among the main targets, and reports say the Crimean Bridge was hit. China confirmed the first Trump–Xi–Putin summit, set for Shenzhen on Nov. 19. All times are ET. Coverage runs from about 6:40 PM Sunday, Nov 15 to 6:45 AM Monday, Nov 16.

Iran and Israel–Iran

  • US drone captured, per IRGC. The IRGC says it captured an intact US MQM-170 drone after an "electronic warfare operation" near the Strait of Hormuz (Al Jazeera, 5:09 AM ET). This is Iran's claim; I found no US comment yet.
  • Third carrier arrives. A US official told Al Jazeera the USS Theodore Roosevelt has reached the Middle East, joining two other carriers in CENTCOM's area (Al Jazeera, 8:30 PM ET Sun). Trump shared a message saying "May God protect our troops" (Al Jazeera, 4:21 AM ET).
  • Nuclear inspections. Iran says UN inspectors won't be allowed to visit its damaged nuclear sites until the war ends (Times of Israel, 3:05 AM ET).
  • Tehran's rhetoric. Foreign Ministry spokesman Esmaeil Baghaei accused the US and Israel of "state terrorism" through assassinations of Iranian officials and attacks on civilians (Al Jazeera, 4:50 AM ET). Iran also says it has at least six months of essential food reserves despite the US sea-and-land blockade (Al Jazeera, 4:00 AM ET). The IRGC says it foiled an attack by a group linked to the Kurdish PJAK (Al Jazeera, 1:36 AM ET).
  • Oil and shipping. Oil prices fell on expectations of a supply surplus. Shipments through Hormuz are nearly back to pre-war levels despite attacks on shipping (Al Jazeera, 12:42 AM ET).
  • Tanker incidents (partly unconfirmed). An X News summary says UKMTO reported a tanker hit by an unknown projectile in the Strait on Monday, causing an engine-room fire with no crew casualties. It also says the IRGC Navy claimed two US-escorted tankers struck mines on an "unauthorized" route (X News summary; X News summary). I haven't found wire confirmation of these details. The IRGC mine claims in particular are unverified.
  • Alleged plot against Trump (unconfirmed). An X News summary cites an Israel Hayom report that Mossad warned the CIA of an Iranian drone plot targeting Trump and senior officials (X News summary). No wire confirmation.
  • Ron Arad report. According to a New York Times report cited by the Times of Israel, Israeli agents kidnapped and tortured senior Iranian generals, killing one, while hunting for missing navigator Ron Arad. Israel now sees that focus on Iran as a mistake (Times of Israel, 5:01 AM ET).
  • US politics. Former VP Mike Pence argued that Republicans lost the midterms not because of the Iran war but because the US "stopped short" after five weeks (Al Jazeera, 9:00 PM ET Sun).

Russia–Ukraine

  • Record drone barrage. Russia's Defense Ministry says it intercepted Ukrainian drones over 15 Russian regions, occupied Crimea, and the Azov and Black seas overnight. The Kyiv Independent calls it a record launch, with Moscow Oblast among the main targets (Kyiv Independent, 6:41 AM ET). AFP says hundreds of drones were fired toward Moscow (AFP, 6:10 AM ET).
  • Crimean Bridge. The pro-Ukrainian monitoring channel Crimean Wind reported a strike on one of the arches of the bridge's navigable span around 5:38 AM local time, and traffic was temporarily halted (Kyiv Independent, 4:04 AM ET). AFP reports the bridge was hit (AFP, 6:10 AM ET). The extent of the damage isn't clear.
  • Azov depot (reported). Ukrainian monitoring channels say an oil depot in Azov, Rostov Oblast, was on fire after drone attacks (Kyiv Independent, 12:55 AM ET). ISW says strikes on the night of Nov. 14–15 advanced Ukraine's campaign against Russian petrochemical, military-industrial and missile sites (ISW, 10:56 PM ET Sun).
  • Trump–Xi–Putin summit. China's Foreign Ministry confirmed the three leaders will meet in Shenzhen on Nov. 19 (Kyiv Independent, 2:59 AM ET). Beijing says they'll discuss "world peace" and called the meeting of "groundbreaking significance" (AFP, 6:30 AM ET; Reuters, 2:50 AM ET).
  • Europe and the talks. Von der Leyen says Europe will "closely engage" in the peace negotiations (Kyiv Independent, 10:09 PM ET Sun). Kaja Kallas called Russia's demands "absurd" and said a ceasefire must come first (Kyiv Independent, 6:36 AM ET). The EU approved its largest-ever cash-for-reforms payment to Ukraine, €7.9 billion (Kyiv Independent, 5:22 AM ET).
  • Hybrid threats. Estonia says it will close its border with Russia after stopping what it called a GRU-attempted arson attack on a drone factory (AFP, 12:31 AM ET). France and the UK will host a December conference on Russia's shadow fleet (Reuters, 5:10 AM ET). Moldova has barred Transnistria's leader from leaving the country after he returned from Moscow (Kyiv Independent, 5:23 AM ET).
  • Diplomacy elsewhere. Russia is seeking senior posts at the UN telecoms agency to reduce its isolation (Reuters, 2:10 AM ET). The US and Russia are expected to oppose launching talks on binding rules for autonomous weapons at a Geneva meeting this week (Reuters, 9:35 PM ET Sun). The UN chief called for binding rules on "killer robots" (AFP, 6:40 AM ET).

Other conflicts

  • Yemen. The Houthi advance is threatening one of the government's last strongholds and bringing fighting closer to the Bab el-Mandeb (Al Jazeera, 9:45 PM ET Sun). The Houthis released video they say shows clashes with pro-government troops in Lahj province (Reuters, 3:05 AM ET). ISW notes Houthi claims of strikes on Saudi critical infrastructure on Nov. 15 (ISW, 11:30 PM ET Sun).
  • Lebanon. The IDF confirmed it struck a Hezbollah weapons shipment in eastern Lebanon (Times of Israel, 3:59 AM ET).
  • Gaza. A hospital source told Al Jazeera that Israeli soldiers killed a Palestinian in Jabalia (Al Jazeera, 3:11 AM ET). The UK and allies again urged Israel to allow rapid humanitarian access (BBC, 3:58 AM ET).
  • Myanmar. Rebels and the military are fighting over small-scale oil prospecting, which has grown more lucrative during the Middle East energy shock (AFP, 6:20 AM ET).

Other world news

  • Trump to Asia. Trump heads to Asia this week after a midterm defeat and amid Chinese spy allegations (Reuters, 1:56 AM ET). Analysts expect possible tighter US curbs on AI chips to overshadow his meeting with Xi (Reuters, 4:30 AM ET). The WSJ reports the administration will name Nvidia and other AI firms US "national champions" (Reuters, 4:00 AM ET).
  • Markets and economy. Asian shares fell ahead of Nvidia's earnings (Reuters, 1:10 AM ET). Japan's economy unexpectedly shrank (Reuters, 3:10 AM ET).
  • US air travel. Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy predicted a return to normal after three days of disruption following the systemwide ground stop (Reuters, 3:30 AM ET).
  • Europe. Germany, Austria, Greece, Denmark and the Netherlands are expected to announce a deportation hub in Rwanda for rejected asylum seekers (Reuters, 2:05 AM ET; BBC, 6:20 AM ET). UK PM Andy Burnham will say Britain lost more than it gained from Brexit, ahead of a UK–EU summit next week (BBC, 1:36 AM ET; Reuters, 3:35 AM ET). France temporarily banned firework mortars that protesters have used against police (Reuters, 4:10 AM ET).
  • Middle East. Jordan is blocking Telegram over national-security concerns (Reuters, 2:35 AM ET).
  • Africa. Africa CDC says Congo's Ebola outbreak is still out of control six months on (Reuters, 12:35 AM ET). Two mass shootings in South Africa left 17 dead (BBC, 5:59 AM ET). Somaliland briefly detained Americans it accused of threatening national security (Reuters, 6:30 AM ET). Cape Verde's President Neves won a second term, according to provisional results (Reuters, 3:30 AM ET).
  • Americas. The widow of slain Haitian President Jovenel Moïse has registered to run for president (BBC, 12:09 AM ET). Pope Leo wrapped up his Latin America tour in Peru, urging Peruvians to oppose Amazon deforestation and pollution from gold and oil production (Reuters, 3:35 AM ET; Reuters, 6:38 AM ET).
  • Climate. The US is officially absent from COP31 in Türkiye, but American groups have a visible presence there (Reuters, 4:00 AM ET).
