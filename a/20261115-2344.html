World News Brief: Nov 15, PM

Top line

Iran's drone attack on a US radar site at Bahrain's Riffa Air Base drew sharp condemnation from Bahrain and other Arab states today. Tehran again rejected European demands to stop enriching uranium. In Ukraine, President Zelensky said US negotiators floated the idea of Kyiv giving up the Donbas in exchange for NATO membership. He also pressed the US and Elon Musk to lift Starlink/Starshield speed limits. In Yemen, the Houthis took over Red Sea coastal positions, said they fired ballistic missiles toward Riyadh, and were reported about 60 km from Aden. All times are ET. Coverage runs from about 6:40 AM to 6:40 PM Sunday, Nov 15.

Iran and Israel–Iran

  • Bahrain attack fallout. The IRGC says it launched six drones at a US radar installation at an air base in Bahrain, calling it retaliation for a US strike on a telecom tower in Sirik. Arab states condemned the attack (Al Jazeera, 7:17 AM ET). The Bahrain Defence Force says its air defenses stopped several Iranian "aerial attacks" (Al Jazeera, 7:10 AM ET). Bahrain's Foreign Ministry denounced "a resumption of Iran's aggression" against Riffa Air Base (Al Jazeera, 8:45 AM ET; BBC, 8:45 AM ET). No wire report I found gives a damage or casualty figure.
  • Enrichment. Foreign Ministry spokesman Esmaeil Baghaei rejected Germany's demand that Iran halt uranium enrichment. He said Iran's "right to enrichment is not something Germany, France or others have granted us" (Al Jazeera, 9:00 AM ET).
  • Diplomacy. After a visit to Islamabad, Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi is set to meet his Omani counterpart this week (Al Jazeera, 8:03 AM ET).
  • Markets. Gulf stock markets slid as Hormuz tensions and Houthi strikes rattled traders (Reuters, 10:30 AM ET).
  • US strikes on launchers (unconfirmed). An X News summary of posts says US forces hit Iranian missile launchers at Bandar Lengeh and Sirik on Saturday, and that Iranian reports describe tankers catching fire after hitting mines in the Strait of Hormuz (X News summary). I haven't found wire confirmation of these details.
  • Drone downed (unconfirmed). According to an X News summary, Iranian air defenses shot down an Israeli-made Orbiter 4 reconnaissance drone over Nahavand in Hamedan province on Saturday (X News summary). There is no independent confirmation.

Russia–Ukraine

  • Peace talks. Zelensky says US negotiators floated the idea of Kyiv ceding the Donbas in exchange for joining NATO (Reuters, 3:00 PM ET). He also said meetings are being prepared to define a European position in the war talks (AFP, 2:41 PM ET). Via X summary, unconfirmed by a wire: Zelensky reportedly said there would be no territorial concessions without a referendum on a full security package (X News summary).
  • Starlink/Starshield. Zelensky urged the US and Musk to lift Starlink restrictions and be on the "right side of history" (BBC, 12:59 PM ET). He said the speed limits are letting Russian Shahed strikes on power substations get through (Kyiv Independent, 8:25 AM ET).
  • Ukrainian strikes in Russia. Ukraine's General Staff confirmed strikes on the Akhtubinsk military airfield in Astrakhan Oblast and a drone launch site in Oryol Oblast (Kyiv Independent, 9:27 AM ET). The military also confirmed hits on major Russian petrochemical and oil-refining plants and a helicopter manufacturer (Kyiv Independent, 7:47 AM ET). Via X summary: Zelensky reportedly claimed record Russian losses of over 50,000 killed or wounded in October. That is a Ukrainian figure and hasn't been independently verified (X News summary).
  • Shadow fleet. EU forces boarded a suspected Russian shadow-fleet tanker in the Mediterranean, according to EU foreign policy chief Kaja Kallas (Kyiv Independent, 7:07 AM ET). Romania's prime minister says three ships were struck by drones in the Black Sea near a Romanian shipping canal (Reuters, 12:10 PM ET).
  • EU path. Ukraine and the EU agreed on reform steps to move accession talks forward in 2027 (Kyiv Independent, 4:53 PM ET).
  • Other items. Prince Harry made a surprise visit to Ukraine, meeting wounded veterans in Lviv (BBC, 5:01 PM ET; AFP, 5:22 PM ET). Ukraine's embassy blamed Russia for pro-UPA graffiti on two Warsaw monuments, calling it an attempt to stir up hostility between Ukrainians and Poles (Kyiv Independent, 11:51 AM ET). The IIHF confirmed Russia is out of the 2027 World Juniors (Kyiv Independent, 2:47 PM ET).

Other conflicts

  • Yemen escalates. Government forces pulled out of an area near the Bab el-Mandeb Strait, leaving the Houthis in control (Reuters, 7:45 AM ET; Reuters, 11:10 AM ET). The Houthis say they fired ballistic missiles toward Riyadh's airport and financial center (Reuters, 1:20 PM ET). Sources told AFP the Houthis were advancing on Aden, the government's interim seat, and were about 60 km away (AFP, 4:25 PM ET). ISW says government-aligned forces left most of their Red Sea coastal positions, likely to avoid being encircled and to keep the ability to defend Aden (ISW, 6:05 PM ET).
  • Gaza. Gaza Civil Defence says an Israeli drone attack on al-Mawasi killed one person and wounded many (Al Jazeera, 11:52 AM ET). A mass funeral was held for 70 Palestinians recovered from under rubble (Al Jazeera, 11:45 AM ET). The foreign ministers of Australia, Canada, Italy and the UK urged Israel to allow immediate humanitarian access throughout Gaza (Al Jazeera, 3:24 PM ET).
  • Lebanon. Israeli drone flights, shelling and demolitions continue in southern Lebanon despite the ceasefire. Israel says it is targeting Hezbollah (Al Jazeera, 3:45 PM ET).
  • Israeli politics. The bloc led by Gadi Eisenkot's Yashar party is preparing to replace Knesset Speaker Amir Ohana and start repealing parts of the judicial overhaul (Times of Israel, 8:30 AM ET). A report says a probe is underway into whether Netanyahu's office orchestrated an Arab vote-buying scandal (Times of Israel, 5:05 PM ET).
  • South China Sea / ASEAN. ASEAN and China again failed to finish a South China Sea code of conduct (Reuters, 12:50 PM ET). Hundreds of protesters against the Middle East conflict and Trump's expected visit clashed with police near the summit venue in Manila (Reuters, 9:00 AM ET).

Other world news

  • US flights. Thousands of US flights are still canceled or delayed two days after a nationwide grounding (BBC, 2:22 PM ET; Reuters, 4:10 PM ET).
  • Space. For the first time, SpaceX caught Starship's upper stage on its return (BBC, 2:56 PM ET). Separately, a US rocket failed after launch, destroying a payload of Amazon internet satellites (Reuters, 3:35 PM ET).
  • Nvidia and China. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said Nvidia must explain whether it knowingly let advanced AI chips reach China, and that it should look outside the company for a new CEO (AFP, 3:34 PM ET; Reuters, 3:10 PM ET).
  • Europe. Tens of thousands rallied in Prague against a government plan to change how public media is funded (Reuters, 1:00 PM ET). Paris police banned protests near 10 schools and five universities (BBC, 5:31 PM ET). UK Prime Minister Burnham says he's ready to confront the "obvious truth" of Brexit's costs (Reuters, 5:40 PM ET).
  • Mexico. A journalist who reported on missing people was found dead in Acapulco (Reuters, 4:20 PM ET). Separately, Milenio reports that the 80-year-old founder of the Guadalajara cartel died in hospital (Reuters, 4:35 PM ET).
  • Pope Leo in Peru. On the last full day of his South America tour, the pope visited high-altitude Cusco and demanded justice for slain Indigenous leaders (AFP, 12:35 PM ET; AFP, 3:43 PM ET).
  • Elsewhere. Cape Verde's President Jose Maria Neves easily won re-election, according to provisional results (AFP, 6:40 PM ET). Indonesia's president apologized to Singapore and Malaysia over wildfire haze (Reuters, 3:30 PM ET).
