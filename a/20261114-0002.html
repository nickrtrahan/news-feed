World News Brief: Nov 13, PM

Top line

Tonight's lead: the US is pushing back on Iran's claims. Central Command denies that Iran destroyed a US radar in Bahrain and says two Iranian drones were intercepted. Iran's Revolutionary Guards (IRGC) now say two tankers hit sea mines in the Strait of Hormuz. The White House confirmed that Trump, Xi and Putin will hold unprecedented three-way talks in China next week. In Ukraine, Russian strikes on Zaporizhzhia injured about 50 people, and the EU has picked five nations to speak for Europe at peace talks. In the US, the FAA briefly issued a rare nationwide ground stop because of a communications outage, and Microsoft apologized for its day-long global outage. All times are ET. Coverage runs from about 7 AM to 7 PM Friday, Nov 13.

Iran and Israel–Iran

  • US denies the Bahrain radar claim. CENTCOM denies Iran's claim that it destroyed a US radar system in Bahrain (Al Jazeera, 10:24 AM ET). According to a summary of posts on X, CENTCOM says two Iranian attack drones headed for Bahrain were both intercepted, with no injuries or damage. The IRGC still calls the attack retaliation for an overnight strike on a telecom tower in Sirik. Neither side has offered independent evidence (X News summary).
  • More mines in Hormuz (IRGC claims). The IRGC now says two tankers hit sea mines in the strait (Reuters, 5:30 PM ET). Earlier, it said a liquefied gas carrier caught fire after hitting mines because the ship ignored warnings and tried to use an "unauthorised route" (Al Jazeera, 3:37 PM ET). This follows the morning's report of an LNG tanker hit by a mine (Reuters, 7:00 AM ET). Unconfirmed: the ships' names and the extent of damage have not been independently verified.
  • IAEA vs. Tehran on inspections. The UN nuclear watchdog urged Iran to let inspections resume. Iran told the agency that isn't possible "in the current security situation" (Al Jazeera, 11:53 AM ET). The agency's report says it is increasingly concerned about an underground enrichment plant at Isfahan (Reuters, 8:50 AM ET). Posts on X say the agency also cannot account for about 441 kg of 60%-enriched uranium (X News summary). Unconfirmed: X posts also say the IRGC has dropped its self-imposed 2,000 km range limit on ballistic missiles and that 71 Iranian lawmakers called for nuclear weapons. I haven't found a wire-service report of either yet (X News summary).
  • Washington. US media report that Mossad chief Roman Gofman is in Washington for talks with senior Trump administration officials about the war on Iran (Al Jazeera, 10:28 AM ET). Before the radar claim, Trump said he told Vice President Vance, "We don't need a deal with Iran" (Al Jazeera, 7:45 AM ET).
  • Economic fallout. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said the war has caused an "energy shock" but that the underlying economy remains strong (Al Jazeera, 10:05 AM ET). He also urged the Federal Reserve not to raise interest rates because of it (Al Jazeera, 4:07 PM ET). In an exclusive, Reuters reports that Iraq is trucking oil out overland through Syria (Reuters, 6:30 PM ET).
  • Inside Iran. Thousands gathered in Tehran for a mass funeral for 110 unidentified Iran–Iraq War soldiers, alongside a missile display (Al Jazeera, 2:57 PM ET). In an op-ed for Kyodo News, Foreign Minister Araghchi argued that US and Israeli strikes violate the UN Charter (X News summary). Fact-check: a viral video said to show Iranians celebrating the regime's fall is actually footage from protests in January 2026 (X News summary).

Russia–Ukraine

  • Zaporizhzhia and Odesa hit. Russian strikes injured 50 people in Zaporizhzhia, including eight children. A rescue worker was killed when a concrete slab fell on him while he was clearing debris (Kyiv Independent, 2:35 PM ET). Reuters reports one person killed in Zaporizhzhia and two in Odesa (Reuters, 6:35 PM ET). Kyiv Post earlier counted two dead in Zaporizhzhia, including a 17-year-old, plus four civilians killed overnight in Odesa and Sumy (Kyiv Post, 10:28 AM ET). These tolls differ and may still change.
  • Trump–Xi–Putin summit. The White House says the three presidents will hold unprecedented talks in China next week, which could cover nuclear stockpiles (AP, 6:55 PM ET). The New York Post reported that the meeting is set for next Thursday on the sidelines of APEC in Shenzhen (Kyiv Post, 5:19 PM ET).
  • Europe's seat at the table. The EU has settled on a five-nation group to speak for Europe at Ukraine peace talks (Reuters, 4:00 PM ET). Moscow pushed back on Washington's insistence that Europeans take part (AFP, 10:43 AM ET). Germany's Merz confirmed that Germany will join and urged Trump to avoid "surprises" with Putin at APEC. In Dublin, US envoy Jared Kushner agreed that no deal can be concluded without Europe (Kyiv Post, 11:36 AM ET). France's ambassador to Moscow called Russia's demand for security guarantees "legitimate" (Kyiv Post, 11:00 AM ET).
  • Energy war. Reuters reports that Russian missiles wrecked a DTEK power plant this month, as Ukraine struggles to protect its plants after the US cut Patriot interceptor supplies (Reuters, 3:36 PM ET). Zelensky criticized the "too slow" pace of energy repairs and accused local officials of inaccurate reporting (Kyiv Post, 12:07 PM ET).
  • Air defense and strike trends. For the first time, Ukraine shot down a jet-powered Russian Geran-5 drone with a Swedish RBS-70 system (Kyiv Post, 1:11 PM ET). ISW says that since mid-2026, Russia has been adding new weapons to its strike packages that could strain Ukraine's air defenses at lower cost (ISW, 12:20 PM ET).
  • Other items. Ukraine and Russia have started a "new format" for evacuating civilians from occupied areas, and 16 Ukrainians were returned on Nov 12, Ukraine's ombudsman says (Kyiv Independent, 2:20 PM ET). A German drone maker that supplies Ukraine confirmed it was hit by a cyberattack in late October (Kyiv Independent, 12:08 PM ET). Russian officials say the Moscow car blast wounded Sergei Zavorotny, a former adviser to ex-Ukrainian PM Mykola Azarov, and have opened a criminal probe (AFP, 11:13 AM ET).

Gaza and the wider region

  • Deir el-Balah. Hospital sources say two Palestinians were killed by Israeli fire in eastern Deir el-Balah in central Gaza (Al Jazeera, 9:43 AM ET).
  • Winter crisis. A Palestinian baby died of cold, and 53 countries urged Israel to let aid in (Al Jazeera, 12:00 PM ET). The WHO warns that flooding is already driving up respiratory illness among displaced people (Al Jazeera, 12:32 PM ET).
  • Yemen. The UN rights chief demanded that the Houthis return the body of a Yemeni UN staff member (Reuters, 6:20 PM ET).

Other world news

  • US flights grounded. The FAA issued an extremely rare nationwide ground stop because of a communications outage. Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin said there was no indication of an attack (AP, 6:08 PM ET; AFP, 6:45 PM ET). As of this writing, it wasn't clear whether flights had resumed.
  • Microsoft outage. Microsoft apologized for the day-long outage that disrupted public services and businesses worldwide (AFP, 4:58 PM ET). It said email queues were approaching normal, though some email delays could last up to 48 hours (Al Jazeera, 12:19 PM ET; Al Jazeera, 1:24 PM ET). England's NHS fell back on paper records during the outage (AFP, 8:37 AM ET).
  • New Orleans. The mayor says more than 30 people were hospitalized after a tanker "lost control" and hit a docked cruise ship (AP, 5:57 PM ET).
  • Mexico. President Sheinbaum says a migrant caravan is not moving toward the US right now, as Trump threatens tariffs (Reuters, 6:45 PM ET).
  • US politics. Sen. Chris Van Hollen will challenge Chuck Schumer for Senate Democratic leader (Reuters, 6:27 PM ET). Protesters demanding an arms embargo on Israel interrupted House Democratic leader Hakeem Jeffries in Denver (AP, 5:04 PM ET).
  • Nvidia. Media reports say Nvidia has approached Arm CEO Rene Haas while some employees want Jensen Huang back (Reuters, 6:00 PM ET). Huang has hired the law firm Paul Weiss (Reuters, 6:40 PM ET).
  • Also: Poland's President Nawrocki vetoed a digital content bill for the second time (Reuters, 5:35 PM ET). SpaceX was preparing a Starship test flight this evening (Reuters, 5:56 PM ET).
