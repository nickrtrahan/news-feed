World News Brief: Nov 12, AM

Top line

This morning's lead: US envoys Jared Kushner and Steve Witkoff are in the Kremlin meeting Vladimir Putin. Overnight, just hours after the Abu Dhabi talks ended, Russia and Ukraine struck each other's capitals. On Iran, the IRGC says two oil tankers were "disabled" by explosions in the Strait of Hormuz. Israel has reportedly told its military to get ready for possible new strikes on Iran. Tehran keeps warning that it would widen the war if it is attacked. In Gaza, an Israeli strike on Nasser Hospital killed at least six people. All times are ET; overnight means Wednesday night into Thursday, Nov 12.

Iran and Israel–Iran

  • Tankers hit in Hormuz (IRGC claim). Iran's Revolutionary Guards say two oil tankers were "disabled" by explosions in the Strait of Hormuz (Reuters, 5:00 AM ET). The details come from the IRGC and have not been independently confirmed. Ship attacks in the strait have been rising for weeks while the US enforces its blockade of Iranian shipping.
  • Israel raising readiness (reported). Israel has reportedly ordered its military to step up readiness for possible strikes on Iran (Al Jazeera, 3:40 AM ET). Defense Minister Israel Katz said Israel will keep acting against Iran "as circumstances required." He also argued that the government can direct military operations without approval from the opposition or legal officials (Iran International, 2:16 AM ET).
  • Tehran's threats and terms. Security chief Mohsen Rezaei repeated that bases used in any new US attack would be targets, including bases in Europe (Al Jazeera, 7:35 PM ET Wed). Basij chief Hossein Taeb said Washington must accept seven conditions before talks can begin (Iran International, 1:47 AM ET). An Iranian lawmaker said Iran could call on Muslim and Shiite communities around the world as part of its response to its adversaries (Iran International, 6:38 AM ET). In an exclusive Al Jazeera interview, chief negotiator Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf said Iran is open to a deal if the US avoids "excessive demands" (Al Jazeera, 3:00 AM ET).
  • Trump: "total victory." In a Veterans Day speech, President Trump said the US has won the war with Iran but won't end it without "a total victory, not a 93% victory" (Iran International, 5:10 AM ET). Earlier he predicted the war would end soon (Iran International, 7:41 PM ET Wed).
  • Europe pushes back. France will tighten sanctions on Iran over an October cyberattack that hit refrigeration systems at more than 700 retail sites, which Paris blames on Iran (Iran International, 3:18 AM ET). In London, a jury found two Iranian men guilty of helping Iranian intelligence target Iran International journalists in Britain (Iran International, 6:41 AM ET).
  • Other items. At the COP31 climate talks, Iran asked for compensation for environmental damage from US-Israeli strikes (Reuters, 9:30 PM ET Wed). Saudi civil defense said debris from two Houthi ballistic missiles fell in the Mecca region after the missiles were intercepted (Iran International, 8:42 PM ET Wed). Sources told Iran International that security agencies are pressuring families of protesters killed in January to accept the official account of their deaths. This is single-source reporting (Iran International, 6:37 AM ET).

Russia–Ukraine

  • Envoys at the Kremlin. Kushner and Witkoff arrived in Moscow and went to the Kremlin to meet Putin. Putin aide Yuri Ushakov said he held preliminary talks with them first (Reuters, 6:55 AM ET; Kyiv Post, 6:16 AM ET). The envoys are expected to brief Zelensky in Dublin afterward (Kyiv Post, 1:35 AM ET). Zelensky again ruled out giving up Donbas and called for firm security guarantees (Kyiv Post, 1:32 AM ET). ISW says senior Kremlin officials are still demanding territorial concessions that go far beyond Donbas (ISW, 9:20 PM ET Wed). ISW also notes the two sides reportedly agreed in Abu Dhabi to a large-scale prisoner exchange (ISW, midnight ET).
  • Strikes on both capitals. Russia and Ukraine attacked each other's capitals early Thursday (Reuters, 1:56 AM ET). In Kyiv, a drone hit an apartment block in the Solomianskyi district. It killed one civilian and set the building on fire (Kyiv Post, 4:18 AM ET). Russia used ballistic and Zircon missiles and 123 long-range drones. Ukraine says Russian attacks killed 10 people and injured 40 across the country over the past day (Kyiv Independent, 5:43 AM ET). Moscow says it carried out a "massive" overnight strike on Ukrainian military factories (Reuters, 6:35 AM ET). Strikes in Khmelnytskyi Oblast also caused power outages (Kyiv Independent, 11:33 PM ET Wed).
  • Starshield limits are back. Zelensky says speed limits on Starshield have returned, cutting the satellite link once an interceptor drone goes faster than 161 km/h. He says this hurts Ukraine's ability to shoot down Russian drones (Kyiv Post, 4:08 AM ET; Kyiv Independent, 5:57 AM ET). This is Kyiv's account; no response from SpaceX is reported yet.
  • Losses. NATO chief Mark Rutte says more than 40,000 Russian troops are killed or seriously wounded every month. Preliminary figures suggest October losses may have topped 50,000 (Kyiv Post, 4:38 AM ET). These are estimates.
  • Diplomacy. Ukraine and hybrid threats will dominate this week's EU summits in Dublin (BBC, 2:33 AM ET). Belarusian opposition leader Sviatlana Tsikhanouskaya said Belarus must be included in the peace talks (Kyiv Independent, 6:51 AM ET).

Gaza, Lebanon and other conflicts

  • Nasser Hospital strike. An Israeli airstrike on Nasser Hospital in Khan Younis killed six people, Gaza officials say. Reuters' civil defense source said a surgeon was among the dead (Reuters, 9:35 PM ET Wed; AP, 1:04 AM ET). The IDF says the targets included a top Hamas commander (Times of Israel, 2:04 AM ET). The hospital director says the hospital is now "paralysed" (Al Jazeera, 6:40 AM ET). A child died from the cold in a tent in Mawasi as winter storms flood displacement camps (Al Jazeera, 4:39 AM ET).
  • Southern Lebanon. AFP photographed Israeli bulldozers demolishing houses in Zawtar al-Sharqiye. The town is next to one of the first "pilot zones" where the Lebanese army has deployed under the Israel–Lebanon framework deal (AFP, 2:04 AM ET).
  • Taiwan. China's Xi Jinping said Taiwan's "reunification" will happen because "the tide of history cannot be stopped." He pledged to pursue it peacefully and did not mention force (Reuters, 12:35 AM ET). Taiwan's president says Taiwan is attending the APEC summit in China on an "equal basis" (Reuters, 3:35 AM ET). Secretary of State Rubio and Wang Yi agreed that leader-level talks matter, including any trilateral summit with Russia (Reuters, 10:15 PM ET Wed). South Korea sees little chance of US–North Korea contact around APEC (Reuters, 6:40 AM ET).
  • Congo Ebola. MSF says the outbreak is still out of control after six months (Reuters, 4:50 AM ET).

Other world news

  • Brooklyn building collapse. The death toll has risen to five after a three-story building collapsed, and rescuers are still searching the rubble (AP, 6:43 AM ET).
  • Nvidia. Reuters reports Nvidia's board is meeting to decide whether to remove CEO Jensen Huang after reports of two extramarital affairs. The company has lost hundreds of billions in market value this week (Reuters, 6:35 AM ET). The firm at the center of the scandal, Megaspeed, has pledged to cooperate with national-security investigations (AP, 5:35 AM ET).
  • COP31. Venezuela told the summit it has a sovereign right to pump more oil (Reuters, 5:20 AM ET).
  • Europe. Protesters rallied in Athens against high housing costs, in support of Spain's general strike on Nov 11 (Reuters, 4:50 AM ET). Manchester United sacked manager Michael Carrick, and USA coach Mauricio Pochettino is expected to replace him (AFP, 5:58 AM ET).
