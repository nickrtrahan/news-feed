World News Brief: Nov 8, PM

Top line

Explosions hit Iran’s Qeshm Island and were heard in Bandar Abbas on Sunday afternoon; the IRGC-affiliated Tasnim agency blamed a US attack, and Iranian state TV said shots near Sirik targeted “violating vessels” in the Strait of Hormuz. President Trump told CNN renewed strikes on Iran were “highly unlikely” if talks produce a strong deal, while Secretary of State Rubio called Iran’s demands unrealistic, and Foreign Minister Araghchi warned Iran would “rain down hell” if Washington chose war. Washington asked Iraq to shut most of its border crossings with Iran, and a drone attack halted production at Iraq’s Khor Mor gas field. In Ukraine, power is being restored in the Dnipro region and Europe says it will take part in peace talks in Abu Dhabi on Nov. 10–11. Elsewhere, South Africa’s ANC posted its worst-ever election result. Covers roughly 7 AM to 7 PM ET Sunday, Nov. 8.

Iran and Israel–Iran

  • Qeshm and Bandar Abbas. State news agency IRNA reported four explosions on Qeshm Island in the Persian Gulf, with several locations apparently hit; local authorities had not yet commented (Iran International, 2:35 PM ET). The IRGC-affiliated Fars agency reported several explosions in the port city of Bandar Abbas (Iran International, 3:06 PM ET). Tasnim, also IRGC-affiliated, said a projectile fired by US forces struck Qeshm and that it was the only place hit (Iran International, 3:57 PM ET). Iranian state TV said three shots heard near Sirik were part of action against “violating vessels” in the strait (Iran International, 3:24 PM ET). There is no US confirmation of a strike in the reporting reviewed here. An X news summary describes this as a third straight night of reported US strikes on air-defense sites and says Iran fired cruise missiles from Sirik; that has not been confirmed by the outlets cited here (X news summary).
  • Trump and Rubio. Trump told CNN in a 20-minute phone interview that it was “highly unlikely” he would order renewed military action if Iran agreed to a strong deal on its nuclear program and reopening the Strait of Hormuz, saying Iran was eager for a deal, while warning that Washington had identified infrastructure targets (Reuters, 1:10 PM ET; Iran International, 12:49 PM ET). The Jerusalem Post, citing two sources, reported that Trump held off ordering strikes over the weekend despite US and Israeli military preparations for a possible operation (Iran International, 5:12 PM ET). Trump also said Task Force Iron Gate was operating at full strength and that, with the naval blockade, very little was moving in or out of Iran (Iran International, 4:04 PM ET). Arriving in France for a G7 foreign ministers’ meeting, Rubio said diplomacy had been unproductive because Tehran’s demands were unacceptable and unrealistic, and said a nuclear Iran “would be completely unacceptable” (Iran International, 5:36 PM ET; more, 6:38 PM ET).
  • Tehran’s line. Araghchi posted that Iran’s forces would “rain down hell” if Washington chose war instead of meeting Tehran’s demands, including an end to fighting on all fronts (Iran International, 12:16 PM ET). He later told state media that mediators had been inactive for several days because of the US election results and more time was needed before diplomacy could resume (Iran International, 2:14 PM ET). An IRGC spokesman said the US could not cut Iran off from its neighbors (Iran International, 3:44 PM ET).
  • Borders and Iraq. Iraqi Foreign Minister Fuad Hussein said the US formally asked Iraq to close all its border crossings with Iran except two (Reuters, 6:20 PM ET). Iraq’s oil minister said Baghdad had reached understandings with the US to allow its oil exports through Hormuz (Reuters, 7:00 PM ET). An explosive-laden drone hit a gas storage facility at the Khor Mor gas field in Iraqi Kurdistan, production was suspended, and the region’s electricity ministry said nearly 2,800 megawatts of generation were lost; no one claimed the attack in the reporting reviewed here (Reuters, 4:00 PM ET; Iran International, 3:47 PM ET). Hundreds of trucks have been stuck for days at the Rimdan crossing between Iran and Pakistan, according to the rights group Haalvsh (Iran International, 2:48 PM ET).
  • Hormuz shipping. ISW said the IRGC Navy kept attacking vessels in and around the strait on Nov. 7 to pressure countries to recognize Iranian control of it, while the US kept enforcing its blockade of Iranian ports (ISW, 4:24 PM ET; more, 6:30 PM ET).
  • US politics. House Democratic leader Hakeem Jeffries told ABC he would support impeaching Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth over what he called a reckless and costly war with Iran (BBC, 2:39 PM ET; Iran International, 4:20 PM ET).
  • Inside Iran. Iran International, citing two sources, reported that 20-year-old Abolfazl Sedaghat was sentenced to death by a Revolutionary Court in Mashhad and denied a lawyer of his choosing; this is the outlet’s sourcing and not independently confirmed (Iran International, 12:20 PM ET). This follows Sunday’s executions of three Karaj protesters covered in the morning brief. Exiled Prince Reza Pahlavi said the government “has never been so brittle” (Iran International, 11:40 AM ET).
  • Israel. Supporters rallied in Tel Aviv’s Habima Square urging opposition leader Gadi Eisenkot to form the next government (Reuters, 3:10 PM ET). The Times of Israel cited a report that Trump is pressing Netanyahu to advance the Gaza plan after the election (Times of Israel, 5:49 PM ET).

Russia–Ukraine

  • Power grid. Zelenskyy said restoration work continued in the Dnipro region after strikes on Dnipro, Pavlohrad, Kamianske, and other communities, where more than 600,000 customers lost power at the peak, and he met regional heads about frontline and border regions, including Sumy (Zelenskyy, 9:27 AM ET; Zelenskyy, 10:14 AM ET). Three people, including a child, died of carbon monoxide poisoning in Kyiv Oblast after running a generator in a basement, the State Emergency Service said (Kyiv Independent, 10:21 AM ET). Zelenskyy asked the prime minister to set up free public transport in Kyiv because Russian attacks on bridges are disrupting travel (Kyiv Independent, 11:29 AM ET).
  • Kremenchuk (unconfirmed by major outlets). An X news summary reports that Russian drones hit a hotel and an apartment building in Kremenchuk Sunday evening local time, injuring at least seven people, including a 16-year-old; I couldn’t find this in Reuters, AP, AFP, or Kyiv Independent posts yet (X news summary).
  • Ukrainian strikes. The governor of Russia’s Rostov region said Ukrainian drones hit power stations and that drone debris damaged a bridge over the Mius River on a road linking Rostov to occupied Ukraine (Kyiv Independent, 1:06 PM ET). ISW said Ukraine is targeting Russian missile launch sites and production plants as its air-defense stocks shrink (ISW, 10:00 AM ET). ISW also said Ukraine gained more territory than Russia in October for the second month in a row, and that Russian forces took more than 54,000 killed or seriously wounded, their highest monthly toll of the war, by its assessment (ISW, 4:00 PM ET).
  • Abu Dhabi talks. France’s foreign minister said Europe will be “well represented” at the trilateral talks in Abu Dhabi on Nov. 10–11, and ISW said both Kyiv and Moscow gave more details on their delegations (Kyiv Independent, 9:25 AM ET; ISW, 2:00 PM ET).
  • Sport. Ukraine’s foreign minister Andrii Sybiha called for a boycott and defunding of the world hockey federation after Russia was let back into the 2027 World Junior Championship in Canada, replacing Norway (Kyiv Independent, 1:57 PM ET).

Other conflicts

  • Yemen and Saudi Arabia. The Saudi-led coalition said it destroyed a ballistic missile launch platform in Sanaa that the Houthis had used against Saudi Arabia’s Eastern Region (Iran International, 1:28 PM ET). The BBC reported on the Aramco Tower in central Riyadh being hit as the Houthis launched fresh strikes (BBC, 4:41 PM ET). Yemen’s internationally recognized government urged Iran to stop arming the Houthis and pull out its military advisers, accusing Tehran of helping plan the group’s operations (Iran International, 5:44 PM ET).

Other world news

  • South Africa. The ANC took just over 30% in municipal elections, its worst result since the end of apartheid (AFP, 1:27 PM ET; BBC, 12:54 PM ET).
  • Senegal. At least 21 people died when a migrant boat sank off the coast, the government said; later local reports cited by an X summary put the toll at 34 or more, which is not yet confirmed (Reuters, 12:40 PM ET; X news summary).
  • Egypt. A train collision killed at least 7 and injured dozens, the health ministry said (Reuters, 2:30 PM ET).
  • Poland. A fellow shooting-club member told Reuters the Krakow gunman appeared highly skilled, and AFP reported the attack has shaken Poland’s sense of being immune to Europe’s mass shootings (Reuters, 3:10 PM ET; AFP, 3:02 PM ET).
  • Germany. Chancellor Friedrich Merz confirmed he intends to nominate Bavarian politician Ilse Aigner, who is expected to become Germany’s first woman president next year (AFP, 6:45 PM ET).
  • G7 and tech. Italy said G7 ministers will discuss recent cyber attacks (Reuters, 4:40 PM ET). Nvidia opened an investigation after a report that CEO Jensen Huang was targeted by foreign intelligence (Reuters, 3:30 PM ET).
  • Argentina. Pope Leo XIV arrived in Argentina and met President Javier Milei on the second leg of his South America tour (AFP, 5:17 PM ET).
