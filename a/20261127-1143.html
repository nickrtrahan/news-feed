World News Brief: Nov 27, AM

Iran and Israel–Iran

Tanker reportedly hits mine in the Strait of Hormuz (unconfirmed, IRGC claim). Iran's Revolutionary Guards navy said an oil tanker hit a naval mine and exploded on Friday morning north of Oman's Ghanam Island, forcing a second tanker to turn back. The IRGC said both ships were using an "unauthorized shipping route." There is no independent confirmation yet (Iran International, 6:24 AM ET).

Iran says it fired warning shots at a U.S. F-16 (unconfirmed, IRGC claim). Iranian state media said IRGC air defenses fired warning shots at a U.S. F-16 near Hengam Island, claiming the jet was covering ships "violating maritime regulations" and then turned away. The U.S. had not commented as of this writing (Al Jazeera, 2:54 AM ET; Iran International).

Hormuz stays shut, Tehran says. Iran's navy commander Shahram Irani said the order is to keep the Strait of Hormuz closed, dismissing U.S. efforts to escort ships through (Iran International). Reuters reports Iran is also warning about foreign military presence in the Gulf as South Korea prepares a bigger naval role (Reuters). Brent crude is trading near its highest level this month (Iran International).

Deadly attack in Kuwait. Two Italians were killed in an attack on a site in Kuwait; Iran denies involvement (Reuters, Thu 6:20 PM ET). Oman condemned the attacks on Kuwait and called for an end to military operations in the region (Iran International).

Trump: give Iran "a chance," but strikes are on the table. Speaking Thursday, President Trump said the U.S. is giving Iran a chance to make a deal and that he has talked with Netanyahu in recent days (Barak Ravid/Axios). He said any U.S. action would target the fortified "Pickaxe Mountain" site near Natanz, and warned that a nuclear-armed Iran could threaten Israel and U.S. cities (Iran International). Separately, open-source analysts point to rapid tunnel construction at a site called Behjatabad between Isfahan and Natanz; its purpose is unconfirmed.

Chemical weapons claim. Israeli Defense Minister Israel Katz said Israel has shared intelligence with the U.S. that Iran is developing chemical weapons. No public evidence has been released. An Iranian lawmaker denied it, calling it a new pretext for pressure (Iran International).

Inside Iran. The rial hit record lows, and the central bank chief said blaming everything on sanctions is "no substitute" for reform (Iran International). Rights cases continue to draw attention: singer Parastoo Ahmadi and four crew members were reportedly lashed 74 times each over a 2024 concert video without hijab, and the Daily Mail reports a 21-year-old protester, Taraneh Rahimi, was sentenced to death after alleged torture. Iran's embassy denies the torture claims (Daily Mail).

Australia sanctions alleged IRGC proxies. Australia imposed counterterrorism financing sanctions on two men it says organized antisemitic arson attacks in Sydney and Melbourne on behalf of the IRGC. Iran denies involvement (Reuters; Times of Israel).

Israeli politics. Ra'am leader Mansour Abbas is expected to call Hamas a terrorist organization in an interview Saturday night, which Gadi Eisenkot has set as a condition for joining a coalition he is trying to form (Times of Israel; Ynet).

Russia–Ukraine

Overnight strikes. Russia launched 155 drones overnight into Nov. 27, including 83 jet-powered ones, according to Ukraine's Air Force. Russian attacks killed 6 and injured 25 over the past day and caused power outages in six oblasts (Kyiv Independent, 5:30 AM ET). Kryvyi Rih, Zelensky's hometown, was hit for a second night, with damage to substations, warehouses and rail infrastructure. In Kharkiv, a drone killed a couple, both 38, in their home early Thursday.

Zelensky warns of a new mass strike. Zelensky said Russia is preparing another large-scale strike in the coming days (Reuters). He also told the BBC the U.S. is too focused on the Middle East and not pressing Russia hard enough, and called Trump's "my friend" description of Putin "strange" (BBC).

Trump's shifting tone on Putin. Trump said he asked Putin to "pull back all military aggression" against Europe (AFP). The Kyiv Independent notes that Trump departed from a joint statement with NATO's Mark Rutte by adding Ukraine to a warning against expanding the war (Kyiv Independent). Talks are expected to resume in December.

Zaporizhzhia nuclear plant. The IAEA said it is working with Russia and Ukraine on a new local ceasefire so repairs can be made after the plant lost offsite power again (Reuters).

Ukraine strikes back. Ukrainian drone units say they destroyed a hidden underground Russian fuel depot in occupied territory in "Operation Inferno-3" (Militarnyi). ACLED data show Ukraine launched nearly 500 strikes on Russian targets more than 500 km away in 2026, more than in the first four years of the war combined (AFP).

Remains exchange. Ukraine received 250 bodies that Russia says are Ukrainian soldiers and returned 22 Russian bodies, according to posts citing Ukrainian officials (not independently verified here).

In Germany, Chancellor Merz defended Ukraine aid in a budget debate, while AfD's Alice Weidel called for peace talks involving Russia and the U.S.

Other conflicts and world news

  • Taiwan: China sent warplanes as Taiwan's local election campaigns entered their final day (Reuters), and Reuters reports the Chinese military tested new Taiwan landing tactics in coastal drills (Reuters).
  • Iraq: Thousands rallied in Erbil against Baghdad's moves to take over Kurdish border crossings and oil exports. Trump voiced support for Iraqi PM Ali al-Zaidi's push to assert government authority (Iran International).
  • Sudan/Horn of Africa: Sudan's government rejected Ethiopian PM Abiy's offer to host peace talks, questioning Ethiopia's neutrality. Abiy also sharply warned Eritrea (Office of the Ethiopian PM). UK MPs are urging the UK to oppose UAE diplomat Lana Nusseibeh's bid for UN Secretary-General, citing the UAE's alleged support for the RSF.
  • DR Congo: Uganda will withdraw troops from a DR Congo town amid a diplomatic row (BBC).
  • Myanmar: Myanmar says it will not fully comply with the ICJ ruling that it committed genocide against the Rohingya (Reuters; BBC).
  • Markets: Chinese stocks had their biggest surge in two years after the State Council promised stronger market support (Reuters). Reuters also reports, citing sources, that the U.S. pushed Venezuela and Iraq to consider leaving OPEC (Reuters).
  • U.S.: Reuters reports, citing three sources, that hackers breached the U.S. Air Force's flagship cloud computing platform (Reuters). Hundreds of thousands are still without power after Thanksgiving storms (BBC).

Covers roughly 6 PM ET Thu Nov 26 to 6:45 AM ET Fri Nov 27. Claims from Iranian state media and the IRGC are labeled unconfirmed unless independently verified.
