World News Brief: Nov 10, AM

Top line

Trump says he's holding off on new strikes against Iran after appeals from Gulf states, and the BBC quotes him calling the war "effectively over" militarily. Tehran isn't echoing that: Foreign Minister Araghchi says there are no plans for a Rezaei–Vance meeting, the Revolutionary Guards say Hormuz won't go back to its prewar status and claim two tankers caught fire in the strait, and state TV showed off a new underground missile site. Qatar's prime minister flew straight from Washington to the G7 in Paris to brief Europeans and the Saudis. The US also pulled aircraft out of a second British base over an Iranian threat, and France summoned Iran's ambassador over the supermarket fridge hack. In Ukraine, the first US–Russia–Ukraine talks in nine months opened in Abu Dhabi, while Russian attacks killed 9 people over the past day. Oil is at its lowest since August. Covers roughly 7 PM Monday to 7 AM ET Tuesday, Nov. 10.

Iran and Israel–Iran

  • Trump holds fire. Trump said he plans to hold off on new military strikes for now after appeals from regional countries, including Qatar's prime minister (Reuters, 9:15 PM ET), and the BBC reports he called the war "effectively over" militarily with further strikes unlikely (BBC, 9:39 PM ET).
  • Tehran's line. Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi said there are no plans for security chief Mohsen Rezaei to meet Vice President Vance, contradicting Turkey's intelligence chief, and said the war won't end through military action or more sanctions (Iran International, 2:44 AM ET). First Vice President Mohammadreza Aref said "confrontation with the enemy will continue" whether or not peace is reached (Iran International, 6:46 AM ET), and one lawmaker called for Iran to build and stockpile nuclear weapons (Iran International, 6:00 AM ET). ISW assesses that Rezaei's growing role in the talks means Iran will likely stick to maximalist demands, including recognized control of Hormuz (ISW, 9:40 PM ET).
  • Mediation. Qatar's prime minister made an unscheduled stop at the G7 foreign ministers' meeting in Paris to brief European powers and Saudi Arabia after his Trump talks (Reuters, 4:10 AM ET; Iran International, 5:48 AM ET). Qatar's foreign ministry says it has received positive responses from both Washington and Tehran (Iran International citing Al Jazeera, 6:34 AM ET). Russia's ambassador says Moscow's offer to take about 400 kg of Iran's highly enriched uranium still stands (Iran International, 3:49 AM ET). Unconfirmed: an X news summary describes a Qatari–Pakistani framework allowing 3.67% enrichment, IAEA inspections and unfreezing $6–9 billion in exchange for lifting the blockade; US officials haven't confirmed any terms (X news summary).
  • Hormuz. The IRGC Navy says two oil tankers caught fire in the strait, including a supertanker it says hit a naval mine; that's the Guards' claim and not independently confirmed in the posts reviewed (Iran International, 5:50 AM ET). The IRGC's Tehran commander said the strait won't return to its prewar status and Iran will keep controlling shipping (Iran International, 1:58 AM ET). State media also aired senior commanders touring a new underground missile facility, without saying where it is or when the visit happened (Iran International, 6:49 AM ET).
  • Threats abroad. The US moved tankers and tiltrotor aircraft out of RAF Mildenhall in England over an Iranian threat, the second British base thinned out since a suspected Iran-linked plot at RAF Fairford in September (Reuters citing WSJ, 2:00 AM ET; Iran International, 5:08 AM ET). France summoned Iran's ambassador over cyberattacks it blames on the IRGC that overheated fridges and freezers in about 700 supermarkets, and its foreign minister says it "rules nothing out" in response (Reuters, 5:00 AM ET; AFP, 3:51 AM ET; Iran International, 5:30 AM ET). Israel's foreign ministry cited both as proof Iran remains a threat beyond the Middle East (Iran International, 3:39 AM ET).
  • Sanctions and Gulf defense. Canada reimposed sanctions on 11 Iranian entities, including the national oil and tanker companies and four major banks, that it had delisted under the 2015 nuclear deal (Reuters, 6:20 AM ET). Gulf Cooperation Council states approved a forward headquarters for their unified military command in Kuwait (Iran International citing Al Ekhbariya, 6:01 AM ET).
  • Inside Iran. The government warns of a winter gas shortfall of up to 430 million cubic meters that could mean cuts even in Tehran (Iran International, 2:57 AM ET), and the judiciary says several people were arrested for encouraging women to defy hijab rules on Tehran's Iranshahr street (Iran International, 5:55 AM ET).
  • US politics and markets. A Reuters/Ipsos poll finds most Americans, including about half of Republicans, want Congress to pass legislation ending the war (Reuters, 12:45 AM ET), and Trump's approval holds at 32% (Reuters, 4:50 AM ET). Hegseth is due to hold a rare news conference today as he fights Rep. Massie's impeachment push (AFP, 4:52 AM ET). Oil has fallen to its lowest level since August (Reuters, 6:00 AM ET) and Gulf stock markets opened higher (Reuters, 5:45 AM ET). Reuters also has a feature on vessels and sailors who have vanished at sea during the war (Reuters, 5:10 AM ET).

Russia–Ukraine

  • Abu Dhabi talks open. US-mediated three-way talks resumed after a nine-month pause, with Ukraine's delegation head Rustem Umerov saying the agenda covers ending hostilities (Kyiv Independent, 4:00 AM ET; AP, 4:29 AM ET). AFP says Washington is calling for a "new" security framework while Europe worries Trump wants a deal with Moscow over the continent's future (AFP, 5:18 AM ET). Ukraine met European and US officials beforehand (Kyiv Independent, 3:18 AM ET). Rubio said Monday night that security guarantees for Ukraine must also be acceptable to Russia, which drew criticism from Ukrainians (Kyiv Independent, 10:07 PM ET). Unconfirmed: an X news summary says US negotiators floated a "cartographic fudge" letting Russia claim all of Donetsk and Luhansk on paper while Ukraine keeps cities like Kramatorsk; I didn't see a wire report on it (X news summary).
  • Zelenskyy. He said the quickest way to end the war is to stop where the lines are, declare a ceasefire, and move to diplomacy (Zelenskyy, 5:56 AM ET), and called ceding land for peace "abstract words," noting Putin talked about Donbas and then went for Kyiv (Zelenskyy, 5:51 AM ET). He's now in Denmark to meet Prime Minister Frederiksen, defense companies, King Frederik and the Nordic prime ministers (Zelenskyy, 7:01 AM ET).
  • Attacks. Russian attacks killed 9 people and injured 39, including a 1-year-old, over the past day; Russia launched one Iskander-M ballistic missile and 162 long-range drones overnight (Kyiv Independent, 5:23 AM ET). Explosions were reported near the 123rd Aircraft Repair Plant in Staraya Russa, Novgorod Oblast, as drones also targeted occupied Crimea (Kyiv Independent, 11:10 PM ET).
  • Front line. ISW says neither side advanced on Nov. 9 and notes a prominent Russian milblogger flagging fewer Russian claims of captured settlements (ISW, 9:03 PM ET). ISW also says Russia's central bank is acknowledging mounting economic strain from the war while calling it temporary (ISW, 11:30 PM ET), and that Russia very likely can't supply Iran with the air defenses it wants (ISW, 10:40 PM ET).
  • Other. Poland arrested two Ukrainians on suspicion of espionage and sabotage for Russia (Reuters, 4:45 AM ET). Foreign Minister Sybiha says a detained Ukrainian OSCE staffer, Vadym Golda, was moved to a medical-correctional facility in Russia's Tambov region (Kyiv Independent, 12:51 AM ET). Russian investigative paper Novaya Gazeta is winding down, citing state pressure (Reuters, 4:55 AM ET).

Other conflicts

  • West Bank. Israeli forces killed a Palestinian boy, according to the health ministry (BBC, 6:26 AM ET).
  • Israel politics. Opposition leaders Eisenkot, Bennett, Golan, Liberman and Lapid issued a joint statement saying they've agreed on a joint nomination ahead of coalition talks; the post was cut off before the details (Times of Israel, 4:26 AM ET).
  • Korea. North Korea accused the UN secretary-general of siding with South Korea over border tensions (Reuters, 7:00 AM ET).

Other world news

  • Bulgaria. At least 12 people were killed and dozens hurt in a string of fog-related crashes on a motorway in eastern Bulgaria (AP, 6:21 AM ET).
  • Hungary. Authorities detained Viktor Orbán's brother in a people-smuggling investigation, the prime minister says (Reuters, 6:45 AM ET).
  • Spain and Poland. A police officer died and another was hurt in a backpack explosion in Oviedo (BBC, 4:29 AM ET). Poland says the Krakow gunman had visited websites about Norwegian mass killer Anders Breivik (Reuters, 6:30 AM ET).
  • Nvidia. Shareholders sued Jensen Huang and Nvidia, alleging they were misled about his affair with an accused Chinese spy (AFP, 6:54 AM ET), as the FBI looks at whether he was targeted by foreign intelligence (Reuters, 6:41 AM ET).
  • Economy and diplomacy. China's trade surplus passed $1 trillion (Reuters, 3:45 AM ET), and the US will skip its UN human rights review, drawing criticism (Reuters, 4:30 AM ET). US October inflation data is due today and is expected to show slower price growth (Reuters, 5:35 AM ET).
  • Culture. M. John Harrison, 81, won the Booker Prize for "The End of Everything," becoming its oldest winner (Reuters, 3:59 AM ET).
