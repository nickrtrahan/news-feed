World News Brief: Nov 24, PM

Covering roughly 6:30 AM to 6:30 PM ET, Tuesday, Nov. 24, 2026. Times are ET. Claims not independently confirmed are labeled.

Iran and Israel–Iran

Katz claims chemical weapons work and plots against Trump (unconfirmed). Israeli Defense Minister Israel Katz said Iran is developing chemical weapons and that Israel and the U.S. have intelligence on it. He also said Iranian cells in the United States have been ordered to kill President Trump, his family and senior officials. He said the IDF and U.S. Central Command have joint strike plans ready, with the final decision up to Trump. Neither Israel nor the U.S. has made the intelligence public (Haaretz, 2:45 PM; Amichai Stein, Kan).

Hormuz blockade standoff. Iran's Revolutionary Guards said a fourth tanker hit a naval mine while using an "unauthorised" route through the Strait of Hormuz (Al Jazeera, 7:27 AM). Tehran repeated that it will use "all available means" against the U.S. naval blockade (Al Jazeera). Security official Mohsen Rezaei warned Iran would respond with "full force" to any new aggression (Al Jazeera, 3:39 PM). Qatar urged the U.S. and Iran to show restraint and return to talks (Al Jazeera).

Israel's leaders split on urgency. Gadi Eisenkot, who is trying to form Israel's next government, said after a security briefing that Iran poses no immediate threat that requires strikes before a new government takes office. He named Iran's roughly 440 kg of enriched uranium as the key concern. Netanyahu pushed back, saying the threats are real (CBN News). Separately, U.S. envoy Jared Kushner warned that Israel risks "global-pariah status" (Times of Israel).

Truckers' strike enters day three (activist reports). Opposition and activist accounts report truck drivers striking in 15+ cities, including Tehran, Shiraz and Isfahan, over freight rates and soaring costs. Iran has also begun rolling power cuts. The scale of the strike is not independently confirmed (Trey Yingst, Fox News, on power cuts; Maryam Rajavi, NCRI, partisan source).

Iran's legal moves. Iran has filed lawsuits in the U.S. and Europe alleging war crimes during the U.S.–Israel war, including a strike on a school (Al Jazeera). An Iranian judiciary official said Iranian courts have issued rulings against the U.S. totaling more than $290 billion. This is an Iranian state claim (BRICS News, aggregator). Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi also accused Washington of crippling the chemical weapons watchdog OPCW by underpaying its dues, citing a Reuters report (Araghchi).

Russia–Ukraine

Overnight strikes. Russian guided bombs hit an apartment block in Zaporizhzhia, killing 2 and injuring 41, including an infant and children aged 4, 6 and 16 (Kateryna Bohuslavska). A strike on the railway station in Korosten, Zhytomyr region, killed 1, according to Ukrzaliznytsia (via Bohuslavska). Russia also struck a building in Lviv (Kyiv Post).

Hungary resets ties. Hungarian Prime Minister Péter Magyar made his first official visit to Ukraine and met Zelensky. He told Ukrainians "the Hungarian people stand with you," and the two sides reached agreements on gas supplies (AFP, 3:30 PM; Kyiv Independent).

Money for 2027. Zelensky said Ukraine needs at least €115 billion in outside funding next year. It expects €90 billion from Europe and has to find the rest (Zelensky). Norway pledged at least $7 billion for 2027 and closer air-defense cooperation (Kyiv Independent).

Ukraine's long-range naval strikes. Zelensky confirmed a Ukrainian attack on a Russian ship in the Gulf of Finland (Kyiv Independent). Kyiv insisted its drone attack on Russian ships off Libya was self-defense and said the drones were launched from a mothership (Reuters; Reuters).

Russian losses. Leaked Rosstat death records analyzed by Mediazona and Meduza confirm about 490,000 Russian military deaths. Estimates top 700,000 once missing soldiers are counted (New York Times; Mediazona). Ukraine's General Staff says Russia set a yearly record of 432,519 casualties. That figure is Ukraine's claim (Kyiv Independent).

Nuclear rhetoric and sabotage. ISW says senior Kremlin officials, including Dmitry Medvedev, are using nuclear threats to pressure the West to stop Ukraine's strikes on Russia (ISW). UK officials warned companies supplying Ukraine's military that they could face Russian sabotage (BBC). A Pew survey found 67% of Americans disapprove of Trump's handling of the war (Kyiv Post).

Other Conflicts

Yemen–Saudi escalation. The Saudi-led coalition told civilians to evacuate Yemen's Hodeidah port before strikes on what it called weapons-smuggling sites (coalition spokesman, 4:57 PM). Houthi military spokesman Yahya Saree threatened Jeddah and other Saudi Red Sea ports if Hodeidah is hit (Saree). Unconfirmed: local sources reported an explosion in Hodeidah soon after (War Monitor). Yemeni government forces said the Houthis will not seize Aden (Reuters).

Gaza. Israeli airstrikes killed seven people, including Hamas's director of internal security, despite the ceasefire (AFP, 11:49 AM). Gaza's health ministry declared an epidemic alert over a surge in meningitis: 1,790 cases and 15 deaths this year, mostly infants (Middle East Eye).

Sudan. Per their own videos: RSF Maj. Gen. Ammar Rabih and RSF counterintelligence chief Al-Tayeb Jabara announced they had defected to the Sudanese army (Clash Report; Clash Report).

Iraq. Kurdistan's main parties, the KDP and PUK, jointly condemned Baghdad's order to take federal control of Kurdish border crossings and oilfields (Rudaw; Diyar Kurda).
