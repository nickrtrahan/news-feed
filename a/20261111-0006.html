World News Brief: Nov 10, PM

Top line

Washington and Tehran are telling two different stories tonight. Secretary of State Marco Rubio said at the G7 in Paris that there's no need for more major combat against Iran because US economic pressure is working, but Iran's top security official says no talks with the US are underway and the Strait of Hormuz stays shut until Tehran's demands are met. In the Gulf, tankers are still burning. Meanwhile, US-mediated Russia–Ukraine talks in Abu Dhabi ran through their first day as Russia hit Ukraine's power grid, and the House voted down an effort to impeach Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth over the Iran war.

Iran and Israel–Iran

  • Rubio says no more major strikes needed. Speaking after the G7 foreign ministers' meeting in Paris, Rubio said major combat operations aren't needed for now because the US economic campaign is "devastating" (Reuters, 1:50 PM ET; AFP, 1:28 PM ET). He also said Washington won't pay Iran reparations (Iran International, 10:19 AM ET).
  • Tehran says no talks. Iran's top security official, Mohsen Rezaei, said no talks are underway with the US (Reuters, 6:35 PM ET). According to an X story summary of his Al-Mayadeen interview, he called reports of a Qatar meeting with Vice President Vance false and said Hormuz stays closed until Iran's conditions, including lifting the naval blockade and releasing frozen assets, are accepted; that detail comes from the summary, not the Reuters post (X story summary).
  • Hormuz. Tankers continue to burn in the region despite hopes for de-escalation (Reuters, 6:30 PM ET). Unconfirmed: Iran's Revolutionary Guards say they struck Omani radar and navigation equipment on Didamar Island, accusing it of guiding "unauthorized" ships through the strait; this comes from IRGC claims and satellite and video posts summarized on X, not from a major outlet (X story summary).
  • Iran-linked plots in the West. The US indicted three men it says are Iran-backed over nearly 20 attacks and attempted attacks in the US and Europe, including the September plot targeting RAF Fairford in England (Al Jazeera, 4:49 PM ET). The Justice Department says an Iran-backed militant helped plan the Fairford plot from a US jail (Reuters, 4:30 PM ET).
  • Nuclear stance. Iran says it remains committed to the Non-Proliferation Treaty, though hardliners may be pushing for a change in nuclear policy (Al Jazeera, 12:40 PM ET). Deputy Foreign Minister Kazem Gharibabadi said he discussed ways to counter US actions, including Washington's "Iron Gate" operation, with Russia's ambassador (Iran International, 10:26 AM ET).
  • Inside Iran. President Pezeshkian told officials to stop feuding and mocking each other, calling internal division unacceptable (Iran International, 10:01 AM ET). Unconfirmed: a fire hit the SPD14D platform in the South Pars gas field, with all workers reported evacuated safely; Iranian officials are reportedly looking at a gas leak and dismissing sabotage claims (X story summary). Activists say several young men arrested in January's protests have been sentenced to death (X story summary).
  • US politics and markets. The House turned aside a resolution to impeach Hegseth in a near party-line vote (AP, 6:21 PM ET), after he called the effort a "farce" and defended his record on the Iran war (AFP, 6:00 PM ET). A Reuters/Ipsos poll found 71% of Americans want the new Democratic-led Congress to pass a law ending the war, including half of Republicans (Iran International citing Reuters/Ipsos, 9:55 AM ET). Gulf stocks closed higher as Trump dialed back talk of strikes (Reuters, 9:50 AM ET).

Russia–Ukraine

  • Abu Dhabi talks. Russia pounded Ukraine's power grid as the US-mediated talks opened (Reuters, 11:35 AM ET). Zelenskyy said prisoner releases are a priority and that the sides discussed in detail how security guarantees for Ukraine would work over the long term (Kyiv Independent, 10:39 AM ET). Reuters' Olena Harmash says hopes for a breakthrough are slim, with territory still the main sticking point (Reuters, 10:10 AM ET). A second day of talks is expected.
  • Rubio on guarantees. Rubio said in Paris that post-war security arrangements for Ukraine only work if Russia also accepts them, drawing criticism from Ukrainian voices online (X story summary).
  • Front line. A Ukrainian commander says Russian forces are trying to isolate Zaporizhzhia by striking bridges and dams (Kyiv Independent, 10:10 AM ET). Ukraine's drone forces say they hit a Russian drone base at the occupied Donetsk airport (Kyiv Independent, 4:50 PM ET). The BBC reports from a devastated frontline city Russia wants Ukraine to give up (BBC, 12:03 PM ET). Ukraine is also trying to arrange another evacuation from occupied Oleshky (Kyiv Independent, 2:35 PM ET).
  • Strikes. Russian bombs hit homes in Sloviansk and a shopping center in Kropyvnytskyi overnight, injuring several people, and Novgorod's governor confirmed a Ukrainian drone attack damaged an aircraft repair plant near Staraya Russa, according to X story summaries (Sloviansk summary; Novgorod summary).
  • Aid. Denmark is giving Ukraine a €645 million defense package plus energy support (Kyiv Independent, 11:30 AM ET), and Poland plans to send two MiG-29 jets in exchange for Ukrainian drone technology (Kyiv Independent, 1:30 PM ET). Outgoing Sen. Richard Durbin urged the incoming Congress to keep restocking Ukraine's air defenses (Kyiv Independent, 6:48 PM ET). The EU extended its training mission and approved its first coordination cell in Kyiv (X story summary).
  • Security and spying. British police arrested a US Army member suspected of spying for Russia (Reuters, 2:40 PM ET). Latvia and Lithuania warned of a threat in their airspace near Belarus (Reuters, 5:20 PM ET). Russia's Yandex says nearly 3% of its cloud customers' data was lost after attacks (Reuters, 5:50 PM ET).
  • Unverified: hacker groups claim they breached Russia's statistics agency and found records pointing to 394,000 to 600,000 war-related Russian deaths. Rosstat confirmed cyberattacks but denies any leak, and the figures haven't been independently verified (X story summary).

Other conflicts

  • Gaza. Israeli warplanes struck an evacuated industrial site in Gaza City's Tel al-Hawa neighborhood (Al Jazeera, 5:00 PM ET). The UN humanitarian office says more than 1.4 million Palestinians are still in tents or makeshift shelters, with storms flooding about 70,000 tents (Al Jazeera citing UN OCHA, 2:00 PM ET).
  • West Bank. Israeli forces killed a 14-year-old boy in Burqa, and settlers torched an industrial area in Beita (Al Jazeera, 2:30 PM ET).
  • Israel politics. The new Knesset was sworn in as Gadi Eisenkot tries to form a government (Reuters, 6:50 PM ET).
  • Taiwan. ISW says a large Chinese military deployment around Taiwan looks like a major exercise simulating the island's isolation, bigger than the last one (ISW, 10:30 AM ET).

Other world news

  • France. Police searched luxury properties linked to the late Mohamed Al-Fayed, including the Ritz, in a probe of an alleged international network trafficking women for him (AFP, 6:45 PM ET).
  • Spain. The government demanded TikTok suspend operations until child sexual abuse material is removed (Reuters, 5:40 PM ET).
  • Argentina. Pope Leo drew about 650,000 people in another big turnout (Reuters, 10:20 AM ET).
  • Pakistan. TV anchor Rabia Ahsan was found shot dead at home in Rawalpindi, and police arrested her husband (X story summary).
  • Business. Anthropic plans to raise up to $64 billion in its IPO, which would value it at up to $1.4 trillion (AFP, 9:43 AM ET).
  • Climate. The Global Carbon Project projects fossil fuel CO2 emissions will dip about 0.4% this year, partly because high oil prices from the Iran war cut oil use (X story summary).
