World News Brief: Nov 7, PM

Top line

The U.S. expanded its Iran blockade on land and at sea, CENTCOM said it disabled a cargo ship in the Gulf of Oman, and Iran's IRGC Navy claimed two tankers hit mines near Hormuz as unexplained blasts were reported around Qeshm Island. In Israel, President Isaac Herzog said he will task opposition leader Gadi Eisenkot with forming the next government, a setback for Benjamin Netanyahu. In Ukraine, Zelenskyy accused Moscow of intensifying strikes to pressure Kyiv ahead of Nov. 10 talks in Abu Dhabi, where Russia said it will send a senior diplomat. Elsewhere, Houthi authorities blamed Saudi airstrikes for 27 deaths near Sanaa, heavy rain flooded tents across Gaza, and France saw another day of protests. Covers roughly 7 AM through 7 PM ET Saturday, Nov 7.

Iran and Israel–Iran

  • Blockade and the M/V Huda. Reuters reported exclusively that the U.S. military is launching a CENTCOM task force to restrict Iran's movement of advanced weapons and other sanctioned goods across its land borders and the Caspian Sea, expanding a months-long naval blockade (Reuters, 8:45 AM ET; Iran International, 8:41 AM ET). CENTCOM said Admiral Brad Cooper ordered Task Force Iron Gate, a team of about 20 military and civilian personnel based in Tampa. Later Saturday, CENTCOM said a U.S. military aircraft fired precision munitions at the stern of the St. Kitts-flagged cargo ship M/V Huda in the Gulf of Oman, disabling it after the civilian crew ignored repeated warnings and tried several times to breach the blockade; the ship had visited an Iranian port. CENTCOM said it had redirected 147 commercial vessels since July and disabled five that refused to comply, and that U.S. forces had previously destroyed 10 Iranian commercial oil tankers it linked to an IRGC shadow network (CENTCOM, 3:37 PM ET; Iran International, 3:55 PM ET).
  • Hormuz tankers. The IRGC Navy said Saturday that two oil tankers struck mines and caught fire in and near the Strait of Hormuz after using unauthorized routes and switching off their navigation systems—one in the eastern part of the strait and the other inside the waterway. It warned that ships cooperating with the U.S. military or ignoring Iran's restrictions risk hitting mines in prohibited areas. The statement gave no names, flags, cargo details, or casualty information, and there is no independent confirmation; treat this as Iran's account (Iran International, 4:16 PM ET).
  • Qeshm blasts. Iranian state media reported explosions Saturday on Qeshm Island in the Persian Gulf, and IRNA said there were two blasts in eastern Qeshm. State TV said similar sounds were also heard in Sirik, Jask, and Bandar Abbas; a reporter suggested they might be related to Iranian operations to defend the strait. The cause was initially unknown. IRGC-linked Tasnim later cited a source saying the Qeshm explosions were not related to missile strikes (Iran International, 2:38 PM ET; more, 3:25 PM ET; Tasnim, 3:40 PM ET).
  • Diplomacy and signaling. Turkish President Recep Tayyip Erdogan told President Trump in a Saturday phone call that Ankara would keep working to ease U.S.–Iran tensions through diplomacy, Erdogan's office said (Iran International, 12:55 PM ET). Iran's ambassador to Moscow said security chief Mohsen Rezaei delivered a message from Supreme Leader Mojtaba Khamenei during a three-hour Kremlin meeting with Vladimir Putin on Thursday covering U.S. and Israeli attacks, Tehran's conditions for reopening Hormuz, and nuclear cooperation, along with plans for Russian gas supplies to Iran (Iran International, 11:45 AM ET). Rezaei also publicly questioned who in Washington should receive Tehran's warning against another war, pointing to what he called turmoil in the Trump administration after the midterms (Iran International, 10:47 AM ET). Secretary of State Marco Rubio, speaking Friday at a CPAC event at Mar-a-Lago, said Trump confronted Iran rather than leave the issue to future presidents and called Tehran “the most radical, terroristic regime on the planet” (Iran International, 5:25 PM ET).
  • Missiles, medicines, and the nuclear file. Ali Balali, an adviser to the IRGC Aerospace Force commander, said Saturday that Iran's missiles can strike targets with precision at ranges of about 2,000 kilometers, citing IRNA (Iran International, 1:43 PM ET). Iran's mission to the UN in Vienna accused Washington of using its Rewards for Justice program to recruit help for attacks on Iranian nuclear scientists and facilities, and warned that anyone providing information, including the IAEA, could face legal consequences (Iran International, 2:34 PM ET). Food and Drug Administration spokesman Mohammad Hashemi said Iran is facing supply difficulties for about 40 of its roughly 700 vital and essential medicines (Iran International, 4:33 PM ET). Iraqi Foreign Minister Fuad Hussein said Iraq had lost about $60 billion from the halt in Hormuz oil exports and had resumed shipments after understandings with Washington and Tehran (Iran International, 11:23 AM ET).
  • Israel's next government. Israel's president, Isaac Herzog, said he will task opposition leader Gadi Eisenkot with forming the next government, a setback for Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu after last week's election (AP, 2:58 PM ET; BBC, 4:54 PM ET; AFP, 5:45 PM ET). The President's Residence said Eisenkot, who chairs the Yashar party, will formally receive the mandate Sunday at 5 p.m. Israel time (10 a.m. ET) and will then have 28 days to try to assemble a coalition (Times of Israel, 3:06 PM ET).
  • Pahlavi in Frankfurt. Exiled crown prince Reza Pahlavi accepted the Friedrich Naumann Foundation's Freedom Prize on behalf of the Iranian people in Frankfurt, a ceremony AFP said critics called illegitimate. Pahlavi said he favored a “third path” of maximum pressure on the Islamic Republic and maximum support for Iranians, and argued that military intervention against the regime's “machinery of repression” had become necessary. Videos sent to Iran International showed demonstrators in the city chanting “Long live the Shah” (AFP, 1:15 PM ET; Iran International, 10:46 AM ET; rally, 8:41 AM ET).

Russia–Ukraine

  • Kyiv and the grid. Zelenskyy said Russia fired 36 ballistic missiles overnight, all aimed at Kyiv and Kyiv Oblast, and struck substations linking the Rivne and Khmelnytskyi nuclear plants with the capital and central Ukraine. Mayor Vitali Klitschko said the Northern Bridge is closed in both directions after damage to the roadbed, trolleybus wires, and power lines, and that at least six people were injured in the city, including a 14-year-old girl; a kindergarten and a residential building were also hit. Reuters said the strikes strained power and heating as temperatures headed toward freezing. Russia said it hit targets in and around Kyiv. Zelenskyy accused Moscow of intensifying attacks to pressure Kyiv at upcoming talks (Kyiv Independent, 8:51 AM ET; Reuters, 9:00 AM ET; AFP, 10:25 AM ET; Reuters, 1:10 PM ET).
  • Talks. Zelenskyy said a humanitarian evacuation of civilians from Russian-occupied Oleshky in Kherson Oblast showed a ceasefire can be arranged quickly, and that the main obstacle is Putin's political will. Talks in Abu Dhabi are expected Nov. 10 among Ukrainian, Russian, and U.S. officials. Russia said it will send senior diplomats and military officials, including Deputy Foreign Minister Sergei Ryabkov, who oversees relations with the United States and arms control and will join the negotiations for the first time (Kyiv Independent, 11:51 AM ET; Ryabkov, 5:12 PM ET).
  • Ukraine's strikes and aid. Ukraine's General Staff said it struck Russian Iskander missile launchers and air-defense systems in Bryansk Oblast; these are Ukrainian claims and have not been independently verified (Kyiv Independent, 7:48 AM ET). German Defense Minister Boris Pistorius said Germany cannot provide Ukraine with more Patriot systems for now and called on other countries to do more (Kyiv Independent, 2:43 PM ET). Ukraine's military intelligence named Russian personnel and said four Su-34 fighter-bombers took part in the deadly strike on an aid center in Komyshuvakha; that identification is a Ukrainian allegation (Kyiv Independent, 7:09 AM ET).
  • Hybrid pressure and casualty claims. Italian Prime Minister Giorgia Meloni expressed solidarity with Foreign Minister Antonio Tajani after a cyberattack he said was tied to Italy's stance on Ukraine; Italian media reported she linked it to pro-Russian hackers (Kyiv Independent, 10:55 AM ET). Hackers claimed leaked records from Russia's statistics agency show up to 450,000 war-related deaths from February 2022 through August 2026; this is an unverified claim by the hacking groups (Kyiv Independent, 3:27 PM ET).

Other conflicts

  • Yemen. The Houthi-run health ministry said airstrikes it blamed on Saudi Arabia killed 27 people and wounded 36 in a village near Sanaa, in one of the deadliest reported attacks since fighting resumed in July; Saudi Arabia has not been independently confirmed as the attacker in the reporting cited here (Reuters, 3:00 PM ET). Houthi military spokesman Yahya Saree said the group had carried out six missile and drone operations against oil and military sites in Saudi Arabia and Yemen, Al Jazeera reported; those claims are unverified (Iran International, 12:19 PM ET). Multiple Yemeni officials told AFP that thousands of Syrian troops were set to deploy to Saudi Arabia and Yemen, with some already on the ground. A Syrian diplomatic source later told state news agency SANA that Syria has no plans to send troops to Yemen, while saying military cooperation with Saudi Arabia could expand (AFP, 10:00 AM ET; Reuters, 3:50 PM ET).
  • Gaza. Heavy rain flooded tens of thousands of tents housing displaced people across Gaza, Palestinian authorities said, soaking bedding and leaving many without shelter. Municipal and civil-defense officials said fuel shortages and damaged equipment hampered the response; storms also hit parts of the Israeli coast (Reuters, 10:10 AM ET; Times of Israel, 10:28 AM ET; AFP, 3:30 PM ET).

Other world news

  • France. Thousands protested across France on Saturday against the government and the cost of living. Paris police moved to block an unauthorized gathering near the Élysée Palace, while hundreds later gathered in Place de la République with music and placards backing the student-led movement. Reuters later reported that a police officer's home in France was destroyed by fire, according to the interior minister (AFP, 11:12 AM ET; Reuters, 12:20 PM ET; Reuters, 5:20 PM ET).
  • Paraguay. After Friday's impeachment and the swearing-in of Pedro Alliana, former president Santiago Peña appeared before prosecutors in Asunción and was charged with breach of trust and administration for personal gain. He remains under house arrest in a crisis tied to the collapse of digital bank ueno; protesters gathered outside his home (Reuters, 3:00 PM ET; Reuters, 9:40 AM ET).
  • New Zealand. There was still no clear winner after a tight general election. Prime Minister Christopher Luxon said his right-wing coalition had a path back to office but conceded the race remained too close to call (BBC, 10:01 AM ET; AFP, 7:57 AM ET).
  • Indonesia. Workers rallied in support of President Prabowo while women marched against him (Reuters, 9:00 AM ET).
