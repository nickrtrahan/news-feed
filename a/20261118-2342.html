World News Brief: Nov 18, PM

Top line

The day after the US strike on Iran's Qeshm Island, the fallout spread across the Gulf. Kuwait says it repelled its first Iranian attack in two months, and Saudi Arabia condemned Iran's attacks on Kuwait, Bahrain and a UAE tanker. Iran's army now says three oil tankers are burning after hitting mines east of the Strait of Hormuz. In Washington, the US intelligence director says threats from Iran have "increased substantially," and Reuters reports that Israel's spy chief lobbied for joint strikes on Iran. In Shenzhen, Trump, Xi and Putin shook hands ahead of Thursday's first formal three-way summit. Russia kept striking central Odesa and Kyiv, and Ukraine hit two Russian refineries. All times are ET. Coverage runs from about 6:40 AM to 6:40 PM Wednesday, Nov 18.

Iran and Israel–Iran

  • Gulf attacks after the Qeshm strike. AFP says Gulf nations scrambled to intercept incoming fire after Iran and its allies lashed out over the US strike on a strategic island. AFP adds that the attacks threaten efforts to end the war (AFP, 6:50 AM ET). Kuwait says it repelled its first Iranian attack in two months (BBC, 8:07 AM ET). Saudi Arabia's Foreign Ministry strongly condemned the Iranian attacks on Kuwait and Bahrain and the targeting of a UAE-owned oil tanker (Al Jazeera, 8:40 AM ET).
  • Tankers burning near Hormuz. Iran's army says three oil tankers are on fire after hitting mines east of the Strait of Hormuz (Al Jazeera, 3:56 PM ET). This claim comes from Iran's military, and I haven't seen it independently confirmed yet. A senior US Navy official says insurance for a single commercial transit of the strait has soared to $64 million, which raises doubts about Trump's claims that the waterway is "open for business" (Al Jazeera, 10:58 AM ET). Most Gulf stock markets stayed in the red (Reuters, 9:00 AM ET).
  • Washington. The US intelligence director says threats from Iran have "increased substantially" (Reuters, 2:20 PM ET; 6:30 PM ET). Reuters sources say Israel's spy chief lobbied for joint strikes on Iran during a visit to the US (Reuters, 12:00 PM ET). Al Jazeera's analysts say the limited US–Iran exchanges could go on for months without becoming all-out war, but they warn the situation is unstable (Al Jazeera, 12:50 PM ET; 10:23 AM ET).
  • Tehran's diplomacy. Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi says he held "constructive discussions" with Oman on an independent regional security framework (Al Jazeera, 4:42 PM ET). Foreign Ministry spokesman Esmaeil Baghaei criticized IAEA chief Rafael Grossi for saying there is no "double standard" in how the agency monitors Iranian and Israeli nuclear sites (Al Jazeera, 6:00 PM ET).
  • Iraq. Baghdad is trying to reassert control over Jurf al-Sakhar, a stronghold of the Iran-aligned Kataib Hezbollah, amid allegations of oil smuggling (Al Jazeera, 1:05 PM ET). Reuters has an explainer on the militia (Reuters, 4:45 PM ET).
  • Unconfirmed, from X News summaries. Explosions in Bandar Abbas were reportedly a supervised disposal of unexploded US ordnance, not a new strike, according to a Hormozgan deputy governor. A UN General Assembly committee reportedly passed its annual resolution on Iran's human rights record, 76–27 with 64 abstentions. Protests in Abdanan after the bus crash that killed 13 oil workers reportedly drew about 1,000 people, with arrests. I found no wire report on any of these in this window.

Russia–Ukraine

  • Strikes on Ukraine. Odesa's governor says Russia hit the city center three times in a single morning (Kyiv Independent, 7:52 AM ET). By afternoon, the toll in central Odesa was at least 4 killed. Al Jazeera puts the injured at 13, and the Kyiv Independent puts it at 9 (Al Jazeera, 3:20 PM ET; Kyiv Independent, 1:51 PM ET). Russian drones injured 3 people in Kyiv and hit a 28-story residential building (Kyiv Independent, 11:24 AM ET). Al Jazeera says Russian air attacks killed at least five people across Ukraine, a day after Ukraine's strike on the Kerch (Crimean) Bridge killed two and collapsed part of the structure (Al Jazeera, 11:05 AM ET).
  • Ukraine's strikes. Ukraine's General Staff confirmed an overnight drone attack on two Russian oil refineries in Krasnodar Krai (Kyiv Independent, 1:51 PM ET). It also says Ukraine struck a Russian supply bridge near Stanytsia Luhanska in occupied Luhansk Oblast and damaged three military aircraft (Kyiv Independent, 10:21 AM ET). The BBC reported from inside an old gas pipeline where Ukraine is fighting an underground war (BBC, 12:01 PM ET).
  • Peace talks. The Kyiv Independent has learned that US envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner warned both sides that this round may be the last realistic chance for a deal before Democrats take Congress (Kyiv Independent, 11:45 AM ET). Kushner says progress is being made (Al Jazeera, 3:20 PM ET). Ukraine's ombudsman says Kyiv and Moscow are negotiating another Oleshky-style humanitarian mission (Kyiv Independent, 1:39 PM ET). Zelensky says Thursday's Coalition of the Willing meeting in Berlin will focus on air defense and energy support (Kyiv Independent, 3:20 PM ET).
  • Money and sanctions. The European Commission approved proposals for up to €45 billion ($49.5 billion) in extra support for Ukraine in 2027. EU member states still have to sign off (Kyiv Independent, 7:44 AM ET). The EU also launched a new office to enforce sanctions on Russia (Kyiv Independent, 10:53 AM ET).
  • Espionage and sabotage. A Russian man was charged in the US with spying on a Palo Alto tech and defense company that supplies Ukraine (BBC, 6:30 PM ET; Kyiv Independent, 6:11 PM ET). Poland's Rzeczpospolita reports that Russia planned a high-casualty false-flag attack near the Kaliningrad border to halt aid to Ukraine. This is a single newspaper's report (Kyiv Independent, 6:59 AM ET). In Kyiv, a 19-year-old woman is suspected of fatally poisoning a foreign ex-soldier on Russian intelligence orders (Kyiv Independent, 9:12 AM ET).

Trump, Xi and Putin in Shenzhen

  • Trump and Putin shook hands, their first meeting since August 2025 (AFP, 6:57 AM ET). All three leaders exchanged greetings a day before their first formal trilateral summit (AFP, 10:34 AM ET; BBC, 7:42 AM ET).
  • Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov says Putin and Xi discussed ending the war in Ukraine (Kyiv Independent, 9:08 AM ET). A US official told Al Jazeera that Trump will hold a one-on-one with Putin on Thursday (Al Jazeera, 3:33 PM ET).
  • Canada's Mark Carney accused Putin of "lying" after Putin told the APEC CEO Summit that Western sanctions are "a thing of the past" (Al Jazeera, 12:35 PM ET). Russian state TV showed Putin's nuclear briefcase being carried at the summit (Al Jazeera, 4:15 PM ET).
  • ISW assesses that North Korean officials met Russian and Chinese counterparts before the summit, likely to signal that Pyongyang refuses to denuclearize (ISW, 11:46 AM ET). Trump also says the US wrapped up a "very big trade deal" with the Philippines (Reuters, 7:35 AM ET).

Israel, Gaza and Lebanon

  • New Gaza operation. Israel launched a rare ground operation beyond the "Yellow Line" in central Gaza (BBC, 3:16 PM ET). According to Al Jazeera, Israeli forces took sections of Salah al-Din Road and families are fleeing (Al Jazeera, 1:45 PM ET; 11:55 AM ET). The IDF says it killed two Hamas commanders in separate strikes (Times of Israel, 11:04 AM ET). Islamic Jihad and Hezbollah called the operation a "flagrant violation" of the ceasefire (Al Jazeera, 1:35 PM ET). Defense Minister Israel Katz vowed to hold Gaza, Lebanon and Syria indefinitely (Al Jazeera, 1:55 PM ET). The WHO called for unobstructed aid as temperatures drop (Al Jazeera, 6:21 PM ET).
  • Lebanon. Israeli excavators demolished homes in Zawtar al-Sharqiye, one of the first pilot zones for the Lebanese army under the June framework deal (Al Jazeera, 10:35 AM ET).
  • Israeli politics. Netanyahu's meeting with Naftali Bennett ended after more than two hours (Times of Israel, 8:20 AM ET). Gadi Eisenkot's bloc says its "highest priority" is replacing Netanyahu quickly (Times of Israel, 11:04 AM ET). In a preliminary vote, the Knesset voted 63–49 to repeal the media overhaul law (Times of Israel, 8:13 AM ET).

Other conflicts

  • Yemen. Reuters reports exclusively that the Saudis are preparing for a Yemen campaign lasting well into 2027 (Reuters, 8:20 AM ET). The Saudi-led coalition says it hit 60 Houthi military targets and destroyed missile tunnels (Al Jazeera, 8:40 AM ET).
  • DR Congo. DRC and Rwanda are reportedly expected to resume talks in Geneva on Thursday amid disputes over the Washington Accords (Al Jazeera, 10:10 AM ET).
  • Haiti. Salvadoran troops deployed in Haiti destroyed a gang leader's home, Bukele says (AFP, 2:00 PM ET).
  • Myanmar. The UN's top court rules Thursday on whether Myanmar committed genocide against the Rohingya (BBC, 6:05 PM ET; Al Jazeera, 12:27 PM ET).

Other world news

  • Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth visited Caracas and signed a deal restoring US–Venezuela military ties. It was the highest-level US visit since Maduro's ouster in January (AP, 2:01 PM ET; AFP, 3:30 PM ET).
  • House Democrats chose Hakeem Jeffries as their candidate for speaker. He is on track to become the first Black speaker (AFP, 11:32 AM ET).
  • Top security officials told Congress that the recent US tech outages were unrelated and not malicious (AFP, 4:15 PM ET). Experts point to a surge in software patches (Reuters, 6:40 PM ET).
  • Minutes of the Fed's October meeting show that "almost all" participants saw further rate hikes as necessary (AFP, 5:00 PM ET).
  • Anthropic priced its IPO at $83 a share, which values it at about $1.5 trillion ahead of Thursday's debut (AP, 5:43 PM ET).
  • The US lost its measles elimination status after 26 years (Al Jazeera, 3:45 PM ET).
  • Bulgaria uncovered a five-year cyberespionage operation (Reuters, 5:20 PM ET). COP31 talks in Turkey remain deadlocked as the summit nears its end (AFP, 7:40 AM ET).
