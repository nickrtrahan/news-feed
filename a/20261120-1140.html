World News Brief: Nov 20, AM

Top line

Russia hit Ukraine overnight with one of its biggest attacks of the war: Kyiv Post, citing preliminary figures, reports more than 100 missiles and about 400 drones. The strikes damaged three bridges over the Dnipro in Kyiv, killed several people in the capital and hit a key energy substation in western Ukraine. The attack came hours after Putin promised a "painful" response to Ukraine's strikes on the Crimean Bridge. On Iran, the Wall Street Journal reports that Mossad's chief pressed Washington for a new multi-day air campaign. Western powers warned the IAEA can no longer verify Iran's nuclear material, and Iran's foreign minister said "the blockade must be broken" as oil is loaded for export. All times are ET. Coverage runs from about 6:35 PM Thursday to 6:35 AM Friday, Nov 20.

Iran and Israel–Iran

  • Mossad chief pushed for new strikes (report). Mossad director Roman Goffman urged the Trump administration last week to launch a new multi-day air campaign against Iran, the Wall Street Journal reported, citing people familiar with the matter (Iran International, citing WSJ, 9:36 PM). Neither government has confirmed it.
  • Western powers warn on nuclear oversight. The US, Britain, France and Germany urged Iran to resume full cooperation with the IAEA. They warned the agency can no longer verify whether Tehran has diverted declared nuclear material (Iran International, 8:55 PM). An Iranian lawmaker said Friday that letting IAEA chief Rafael Grossi into Iran would threaten security and accused him of spying (1:48 AM).
  • "The blockade must be broken." Asked about reports that Iranian oil is being loaded for export, Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi said Iran will make every effort to export oil and to pursue negotiations (Iran International, citing ILNA, 4:19 AM).
  • IRGC rhetoric escalates. IRGC commander Ahmad Vahidi said Iran could review its military doctrine when he was asked about extending missile range (4:05 AM). He also told a Basij gathering that Iran is entering a "final battle" with "global imperialism." Other commanders warned of a missile response to any attack and a "decisive" response to any strike on Iran's dams (3:44 AM). An IRGC spokesman again called the broadcaster Iran International a military target (8:37 PM).
  • Araghchi mocks Israel's ambassador. Israel's ambassador to the US, Yechiel Leiter, told Newsmax that US troops could come home only after Iran is defeated. Araghchi mocked him, asking whether the ambassador now commands US forces (Iran International, 11:49 PM).
  • Dirty-bomb warning traced to Israel (report). CNN reported Nov. 16 that US intelligence picked up Iranian planning for a possible dirty-bomb or chemical attack in Washington, which its sources called option-building rather than an imminent plot. According to X News summaries, the New York Times has since identified Israeli intelligence as the source. Iran calls the claims fabricated. I could not confirm the Times report directly.
  • Protests in western Iran (unverified). Videos circulating on X show protests in Abdanan after a deadly bus crash, with some crowds chanting for Reza Pahlavi. These videos are not independently verified. Separately, Reuters published a first-person account from a Tehran protester who says she was hit by pellets (Reuters, 3:16 AM).
  • Proxies: Houthis and Iraq. The Saudi-led coalition said it intercepted two Houthi drones fired toward southern Saudi Arabia (9:01 PM), and Egypt condemned Houthi attacks on facilities in Riyadh (10:57 PM). In Iraq, PM Ali al-Zaidi told forces to prepare to take on Iran-aligned Kataib Hezbollah, which officials accuse of oil smuggling (Reuters, 6:05 AM). Reuters had earlier reported a deal to avoid a clash, and footage shows federal police entering the group's Jurf al-Sakhar stronghold (Iran International, 5:20 AM).

Russia–Ukraine

  • Massive overnight attack. The first explosions hit Kyiv around 6:25 PM ET Thursday (1:25 AM local) (Kyiv Independent, 7:18 PM). Citing preliminary data, Kyiv Post reports that Russia launched more than 80 cruise missiles, up to 20 ballistic missiles and about 400 drones (Kyiv Post, 3:00 AM).
  • Kyiv bridges hit, death toll unclear. Ballistic missiles damaged three Dnipro bridges, the Podilsko-Voskresenskyi, Darnytskyi and Southern, disrupting traffic between the banks (Kyiv Post, 2:02 AM; BBC, 3:07 AM). Officials first reported three employees of the city water utility killed and about 15 injured (Kyiv Independent, 12:39 AM). AFP later put the toll in Kyiv at six (AFP, 5:50 AM). Expect the figures to change. AFP also reports damage to a historic cathedral and to the Austrian and Dutch embassies (5:24 AM).
  • Energy grid targeted. An anonymous energy official told the Kyiv Independent that strikes hit a major 750-kV substation in western Ukraine, described as Europe's largest (3:45 AM). Kyiv's mayor reported power outages in several districts.
  • Poland evacuates a NATO hub tower. For the first time, Poland evacuated the control tower at Rzeszów Airport, a key hub for military aid to Ukraine, during the attack (Kyiv Post, 5:46 AM).
  • Ukraine strikes back. Zelensky said Ukraine hit three Russian ballistic missile launchers overnight, destroying one, with results on the other two still being assessed (Kyiv Post, 6:07 AM). ISW says Ukraine has now struck the Kerch Strait (Crimean) Bridge three times in three days (ISW, 11:10 PM). Reuters says the partial destruction of the bridge supports Kyiv's push to cut Russian supply lines to the south (Reuters, 3:30 AM).
  • Diplomacy after Shenzhen and Berlin. Zelensky said there is no common understanding yet of what "Article 5-style" guarantees would mean (Reuters, 6:35 PM). US envoy Jared Kushner said Russia is growing more comfortable with such guarantees (Kyiv Post, 5:36 AM). Chancellor Merz suggested Germany could support a gradual lifting of some sanctions as part of a peace deal (Kyiv Post, 5:31 AM). Putin said Russia has no plans to attack Europe and invited EU leaders to Moscow. ISW assesses that he used the APEC summit to present himself as a superpower while keeping his maximalist demands unchanged (ISW, 10:03 PM).
  • EU money. The EU completed what it called its largest-ever reform payment to Ukraine, almost €8 billion (Kyiv Independent, 6:33 AM).

Other conflicts

  • Gaza. Israeli forces this week ran a targeted operation near the Nuseirat and Bureij camps in central Gaza against what the military called a Hamas tunnel. According to X News summaries of Palestinian reports, homes were damaged and two people were killed. Hamas called it a ceasefire violation. Casualty figures are not independently confirmed.
  • Myanmar. Myanmar's presidential spokeswoman called the UN top court's historic ruling that Myanmar committed genocide against the Rohingya "not fair" (AFP, 5:40 AM). Separately, Malaysia says it will resettle 10,000 Rohingya refugees in Turkey (Reuters, 6:35 AM).
  • Congo and Nigeria (unverified). Posts on X describe drone strikes around Minembwe in South Kivu that hit homes and a radio station, and a viral video from Nigeria appears to show bandits receiving military uniforms. Nigeria's Defence Headquarters denies any military involvement. Neither has been independently verified here.

Other world news

  • UK–EU reset. Britain and the EU are set to announce food-trade and youth-mobility deals at a Brussels summit today (Reuters, 5:35 AM).
  • China–Australia. Xi hailed a "comprehensive turnaround" in ties with Australia (Reuters, 5:30 AM). Reuters also reports that US allies are looking beyond Washington as Trump tries to reshape the world order around great powers (5:20 AM).
  • Weather and quakes. Heavy overnight rain flooded neighborhoods in east-central Florida (Reuters, 6:23 AM). India's eastern coast was hit by its third cyclone in a month (5:50 AM). A magnitude 5.1 earthquake struck near Monterey County, California, according to the USGS (5:45 AM).
  • South Africa. Nearly 600 women were killed in South Africa in three months (BBC, 5:49 AM).
