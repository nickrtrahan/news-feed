World News Brief: Nov 11, AM

Top line

The biggest overnight news is from Abu Dhabi. Officials at the US-mediated talks say Russia and Ukraine have agreed to a large prisoner-of-war release as the second day of talks got underway. On Iran, Tehran is hardening its line. Its top security official says none of Iran's enriched uranium will leave the country and that Iran is waiting for a US answer to its seven conditions. Another tanker caught fire after hitting a mine near Hormuz, which pushed oil prices back up.

Iran and Israel–Iran

  • Nuclear red line. Mohsen Rezaei, secretary of Iran's Supreme National Security Council, told Al Mayadeen that Iran won't move any enriched nuclear material abroad, will use it itself, and has told Russia this (Iran International, 4:24 AM ET). He said Tehran is waiting for a US response to its seven conditions for peace talks and brushed off talk of negotiations with Washington (Al Jazeera, 4:53 AM ET).
  • Hormuz. Iran's navy says a tanker caught fire after hitting a sea mine east of the Strait of Hormuz, in an area it calls "unsafe and restricted." This comes from Iranian state media (Al Jazeera, 2:21 AM ET; Iran International, 1:56 AM ET). Oil rebounded as the shipping attacks cut into hopes for a truce (Reuters, 3:30 AM ET). Smoke was seen rising from Oman's Didamar Island after Tuesday's attack on navigation equipment, which Iranian state media blamed on the Revolutionary Guards' navy (Iran International, 4:28 AM ET).
  • Threats. Mohammad Mokhber, an adviser to Supreme Leader Mojtaba Khamenei, said any country that helps enforce a "hostile blockade" on Iran would be treated as taking part in an act of war (Iran International, 6:22 AM ET). Armed forces chief Ali Abdollahi warned the US and Israel of more lethal strikes on any "center of threats" (Iran International, 3:18 AM ET). The hardline newspaper Kayhan urged threats against US bases beyond the region, including in Europe (Iran International, 1:46 AM ET). Iraq's Iran-aligned Harakat al-Nujaba militia pledged loyalty to Khamenei and said it's ready for escalation, according to Kurdish media (Al Jazeera, 2:45 AM ET).
  • Military displays. Iran showed off an underground "missile city" run by the Revolutionary Guards' aerospace force. Details come from an X story summary of the unveiling, not from a major outlet (X story summary). Tehran's Guards commander said Iran kept making missiles and drones during the 40-day war and has "surprises" ready (Iran International, 4:58 AM ET). Iran also opened its first all-women military training camp (Al Jazeera, 11:00 PM ET Tue).
  • Diplomacy. Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi asked the UN and the IAEA to condemn a US offer of up to $20 million for information on Iran's nuclear program. Iran says the offer encourages the killing of its scientists (Iran International, 2:51 AM ET; Al Jazeera, 5:06 AM ET). Iran designated Argentina's navy and air force as "terrorist organizations" to retaliate for Argentina's blacklisting of the Revolutionary Guards (Reuters, 5:30 AM ET). Activists are pushing for a recorded vote today at the UN General Assembly on Iran's nomination to a UN program-review committee (X story summary).
  • US threats and politics. The US embassy in Riyadh told Americans across the Middle East to be extra vigilant, citing threats from the Houthis and alleged Iranian efforts to target US officials (Al Jazeera, 11:46 PM ET Tue). US media report that Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent moved onto a military base after Iranian threats against his life. This hasn't been independently confirmed (Al Jazeera citing US media, 9:59 PM ET Tue). Sen. Adam Schiff says Democrats will use upcoming government funding talks to try to cut off money for the war (Al Jazeera, 10:34 PM ET Tue). Rep. Thomas Massie, a Republican, brought the failed Hegseth impeachment bid and was the only member of his party to oppose killing it (AFP, 8:44 PM ET Tue).
  • Inside Iran. The labor news agency ILNA reports that workers buried in debt are advertising their kidneys and other organs for sale (Iran International, 6:30 AM ET). Students are reportedly in unheated classrooms as the government warns of gas shortages (Iran International, 5:29 AM ET).

Russia–Ukraine

  • Prisoner deal. Breaking: Russia and Ukraine agreed to a large release of prisoners of war, according to officials at the Abu Dhabi talks (AP, 6:59 AM ET). No numbers yet.
  • Talks, day two. The second day of the three-way talks between Ukraine, Russia and the US has begun, and Ukraine plans a separate meeting with European partners afterward (Kyiv Independent, 5:11 AM ET). Washington called for a "new" security framework on day one (AFP, 8:00 PM ET Tue). Zelenskyy said he's very happy with the messages he's getting from the US (Reuters, 12:36 AM ET). ISW says the Kremlin is trying to steer the talks toward reshaping European security, which would revive its 2021 demands to roll back NATO (ISW, 11:09 PM ET Tue).
  • Strikes on Russia. Ukrainian drones reportedly hit the Ust-Luga Baltic oil port in Leningrad region overnight (Kyiv Independent, 8:07 PM ET Tue). According to an X story summary, the local governor confirmed a fire and three injured workers (X story summary). Reuters industry sources say Ukraine's strikes since late October have cut Russia's refining capacity by about 30%. That figure comes via an X summary (X story summary).
  • Strikes on Ukraine. Russian drones hit power substations in Kryvyi Rih and caused widespread outages, according to an X story summary of local officials' posts (X story summary). Unconfirmed: Russia's defense ministry claimed it shot down four US-made AGM-188A "Rusty Dagger" cruise missiles, then removed the claim. Ukraine hasn't commented (X story summary).
  • Baltics and spying. Lithuania, Latvia and Estonia withdrew a flurry of shelter alerts over unidentified drones from Russia or Belarus (Reuters, 1:55 AM ET). Russia says it arrested two suspected Polish spies in Kaliningrad (Reuters citing Interfax, 1:50 AM ET). ISW says Iran and Russia are working together to get around US efforts to stop arms shipments over the Caspian Sea (ISW, 8:12 PM ET Tue).
  • Europe. EU Council President Costa is keeping Ukraine funding on the late-November agenda despite an appeal from Germany's Merz (Kyiv Independent, 10:25 PM ET Tue). Reuters reports that Ukraine's path to EU membership is narrowing as the war drags on (Reuters, 6:45 AM ET). Today is also four years since Kherson was liberated (X story summary).
  • Africa. US Africa Command warned Madagascar about its growing military ties with Russia (BBC, 2:34 AM ET).

Other conflicts

  • Gaza. An Israeli airstrike on a metal foundry in Gaza City's Sabra neighborhood killed three people, and four more, including a 13-year-old, were killed elsewhere, medics said (Reuters, 3:34 AM ET). Gaza's health ministry says at least eight were killed in the past 24 hours (Al Jazeera, 6:05 AM ET). Sen. Brian Schatz urged Israel to let in building and winter supplies after flooding hit more than 70,000 tents and killed four people, three of them children (Al Jazeera, 1:15 AM ET).
  • Yemen. Heavy fighting goes on. Government-aligned forces claim they killed or wounded at least 190 Houthi fighters (Al Jazeera, 1:45 AM ET).
  • Taiwan. Some US federal employees will work in Taiwan government offices for up to a year under a new program (Reuters, 2:00 AM ET). Taipei named Acer's founder as its APEC envoy and called for written guarantees from China (Reuters, 4:00 AM ET).
  • Congo. Confirmed Ebola cases have passed 11,000, with at least 5,387 deaths, according to government data (Al Jazeera, 12:56 AM ET).

Other world news

  • Italy. At least 300 schools were evacuated after receiving the same bomb threat (Reuters, 6:11 AM ET).
  • Climate. Brazil's Lula told the COP31 summit that climate change has "disappeared" from the global agenda (AFP, 5:58 AM ET). In Chile, transport workers blocked highways over surging fuel prices (BBC, 6:37 AM ET).
  • Economy. A hotter-than-expected US inflation report has revived fears of a bigger Fed rate hike next month (Reuters, 6:00 AM ET). Anthropic began its IPO roadshow at a valuation of up to $1.4 trillion (Reuters, 6:10 AM ET).
  • China tech. Reuters reports that a university linked to China's military bought Nvidia's H200 AI chips despite being on a US export blacklist (Reuters, 6:45 AM ET).
  • Venezuela. Lawmakers opened the struggling power sector to private investment (AFP, 10:16 PM ET Tue).
  • Ireland. Pro-Palestinian protesters threw milk at former President Joe Biden during a visit to Trinity College Dublin (Al Jazeera, 11:40 PM ET Tue).
  • Argentina. Pope Leo wrapped up his visit (Reuters, 5:45 AM ET).
