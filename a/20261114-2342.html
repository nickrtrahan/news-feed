World News Brief: Nov 14, PM

Top line

This evening: Iran's Revolutionary Guards say two more oil tankers hit mines while trying to slip through the Strait of Hormuz, and a UN official is pleading for fertilizer ships to be let through. Mossad's director is reportedly in Washington to discuss Iran. On Ukraine, Zelensky says Russia and Ukraine agreed to swap 1,200 to 1,300 prisoners each, and US envoy Jared Kushner says Washington will now focus on getting Russia and Europe talking directly. Trump plans to raise nuclear arms reduction at next Thursday's summit with Xi and Putin. Elsewhere, SpaceX caught a returning Starship for the first time, and the FAA expects US flights to be back to normal by the end of Sunday. All times are ET. Coverage runs from about 7 AM to 6:40 PM Saturday, Nov 14.

Iran and Israel–Iran

  • More tankers hit mines (Iranian claim). The IRGC navy says two oil tankers struck naval mines while trying to "secretly" cross the Strait of Hormuz after switching off their tracking systems and using an "unauthorised" route (Al Jazeera, 5:01 PM ET). Earlier, Iran's navy said an oil tanker was on fire after hitting a mine (Reuters, 2:50 PM ET). Unconfirmed: it isn't clear whether these overlap with the three tanker fires Iran reported this morning, and ship names and casualties have not been independently verified.
  • US drones downed? (unconfirmed). According to a summary of posts on X, Iran claims it shot down two US MQ-1 drones near Sirik and over Qeshm Island on Nov 13–14, with state media showing wreckage, and US Central Command has not commented (X News summary). I haven't found a wire-service report confirming this.
  • Fertilizer plea. Jorge Moreira da Silva, head of the UN Office for Project Services, called on Iran and the US to "immediately" allow fertilizer shipments through Hormuz to ease the crisis in vulnerable countries (Al Jazeera, 11:15 AM ET).
  • Mossad chief in Washington. Israeli media report that Mossad Director Roman Gofman is in Washington to meet Secretary of State Marco Rubio and CIA Director John Ratcliffe about Iran (Al Jazeera, 12:45 PM ET).
  • US posture. Reuters' sourced report that Trump may hold off on new strikes barring fresh provocations is still the main signal from Washington (Reuters, 10:20 AM ET). Al Jazeera says Iranian decision-makers are weighing what JD Vance's removal from the Iran file means, and some analysts think it could raise the odds of conflict (Al Jazeera, 10:30 AM ET).
  • France cyberattacks. The IRGC warned France against falling into an Israeli "false flag" trap and denied involvement in recent cyberattacks on refrigeration units in French supermarkets (Al Jazeera, 3:35 PM ET).

Russia–Ukraine

  • Big prisoner swap agreed. Zelensky says Ukraine and Russia agreed to exchange 1,200 to 1,300 prisoners each after US-mediated talks, and that all sides agreed to seat European delegates at the main table for the next round (Kyiv Post, 9:09 AM ET).
  • Kushner: Russia–Europe talks next. Jared Kushner says the US is now focused on "facilitating direct engagement" between Russia and Washington's European partners and on preparing the agenda for the next round of peace talks (Kyiv Independent, 5:00 PM ET).
  • Land for NATO. AFP confirms Zelensky's account that the US suggested for the first time that Ukraine cede land in exchange for NATO membership, and that Russia rejected an energy ceasefire (AFP, 9:45 AM ET). Zelensky says Putin's "humanitarian mission" proposal for Kostiantynivka is meant to seize the city and push toward Kramatorsk (Kyiv Independent, 11:21 AM ET).
  • Summit with Xi and Putin. Trump will raise nuclear arms reduction at next Thursday's summit with China and Russia (Reuters, 6:30 PM ET). Reuters reports that Xi is wary of hosting what some are calling a new "Yalta" (Reuters, 4:00 PM ET).
  • Russian attacks. At least 7 people were killed and 80 injured in Russian attacks across Ukraine. Zaporizhzhia Oblast was among the worst hit, with three killed, including a child, and 62 injured (Kyiv Independent, 7:32 AM ET).
  • Ukrainian strikes. Ukraine's General Staff reported a fire at an oil facility in Samara Oblast and a strike on a military airbase (Kyiv Independent, 9:42 AM ET). Russia has not confirmed the damage.
  • Starshield fight escalates. Zelensky says SpaceX's Starshield restrictions give Russia more room to attack and amount to treating occupied territories as "not ours" (Kyiv Independent, 7:34 AM ET; Reuters, 2:00 PM ET). ISW says it is still unclear whether the limits apply only over occupied areas or across all of Ukraine (ISW, 9:00 AM ET).
  • Aid and defense. Poland's Tusk says Poland will not fund US weapons for Ukraine under NATO's PURL initiative (Kyiv Post, 10:12 AM ET). Zelensky says Ukraine's homegrown FREYJA ballistic interceptor missile is ready, and the Nordic Five pledged continued aid next year (Kyiv Post, 9:40 AM ET).
  • Corruption probe. Zelensky says senior officials named on wiretaps in the "Operation Carthage" drug-ring probe are not implicated, citing NABU and SAPO (Kyiv Post, 8:16 AM ET).

Israel, Gaza and the West Bank

  • Hamas commander targeted. The Israeli military says it struck a senior commander in Hamas' military wing (Reuters, 5:35 PM ET).
  • Aid pressure. After this week's flooding, the EU urged Israel to reopen all Gaza crossings and allow "immediate, unimpeded access" for aid groups (Al Jazeera, 1:00 PM ET). Al Jazeera's tally says at least 1,614 Palestinians have been killed since the October 2025 ceasefire (Al Jazeera, 2:00 PM ET).
  • West Bank. Several Palestinians were reportedly injured in two settler attacks (Times of Israel, 1:55 PM ET).
  • Israeli politics. Caretaker PM Netanyahu again called for a unity government with Gadi Eisenkot, who dismissed the offer as a ruse (AFP, 4:15 PM ET).

Other world news

  • Starship caught. SpaceX caught its Starship vessel for the first time as it returned to its Texas launch site after a day in orbit (AFP, 5:45 PM ET; Reuters, 4:50 PM ET).
  • US air travel. The FAA says flights should be back to normal by the end of Sunday (AP, 12:00 PM ET). Reuters reports the shutdown was blamed on a router installed by L3Harris (Reuters, 3:20 PM ET).
  • New Orleans cruise ship. All passengers and crew are accounted for after a cruise ship capsized following a collision with a tanker on the Mississippi (Reuters, 4:30 PM ET). The port is suing the tanker's owners for $1 million (BBC, 5:29 PM ET).
  • France protests. Tens of thousands marched across France over the cost of living and a plan to give police more legal protection (AFP, 3:30 PM ET). Riot police used tear gas at Place de la Nation in Paris (Reuters, 2:40 PM ET).
  • Also: Italy fined Meta €805 million over access to users' photos (BBC, 11:32 AM ET). Microsoft says its services are fully restored after a worldwide outage (Reuters, 2:30 PM ET). Thousands protested at COP31 in Antalya, Turkey (AFP, 5:00 PM ET). Pope Leo visited a remote Andes town during his Peru tour (Reuters, 4:10 PM ET). Police searched an Illinois plant after a "credible threat" of an explosive device (Reuters, 3:45 PM ET). Thousands fled into Mali after Guinea cracked down on illegal mining (AFP, 6:30 PM ET).
