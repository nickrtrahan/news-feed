World News Brief: Nov 13, AM

Top line

This morning's lead: Iran says it hit back in the Gulf. The Revolutionary Guards (IRGC) claim they struck a US early-warning radar in Bahrain in retaliation for Thursday's attack on Sirik. That would be Iran's first claimed attack on US military assets in a Gulf country since early September. Iran also says an LNG tanker hit a mine in the Strait of Hormuz and that its navy shot down a US drone. The UN nuclear watchdog says it can no longer confirm that Iran hasn't diverted nuclear material. On Ukraine, Russia fired 117 drones overnight, and attacks over the past day killed nine people. Ukraine launched a huge drone wave of its own, and four people died in Volgograd. ISW says Putin is giving no ground on his core demands. Elsewhere, a major Microsoft outage stretched past 12 hours. All times are ET. Coverage runs from Thursday evening, Nov 12, to early Friday, Nov 13.

Iran and Israel–Iran

  • IRGC claims strike on US radar in Bahrain. Iran's Guards say they targeted a US early-warning radar in Bahrain. Reuters notes this is the first such claim against American military assets in a Gulf country since early September (Reuters, 12:20 AM ET). Tehran calls it retaliation for an attack on a telecommunications center in Iran, apparently the Sirik strike reported Thursday (Al Jazeera, 6:02 AM ET). Unconfirmed: satellite images circulating online appear to show a damaged desert radar site. As of this writing, neither the US nor Bahrain had commented (X News summary).
  • LNG tanker hit in Hormuz (Iranian claim). Iran says a tanker carrying liquefied natural gas hit a mine and suffered an explosion in the strait (Reuters, 2:50 AM ET). That adds to the string of vessels damaged around Hormuz this week.
  • US drone downed? (Iranian claim). Iran's navy says it shot down a US MQ-1 drone over the Strait of Hormuz, according to Iranian media (Al Jazeera, 5:52 AM ET). Photos of wreckage near the Sirik coast are circulating. US Central Command has not commented, and the claim has not been independently verified (X News summary).
  • IAEA alarm on Isfahan. In a confidential report, the UN nuclear watchdog says it is "increasingly concerned" about an underground enrichment plant at Isfahan that Iran has never let inspectors visit (Reuters, 4:45 AM ET). The report says the agency cannot confirm that Iran hasn't diverted nuclear material from peaceful use (Times of Israel, 5:45 AM ET). Iran cites ongoing military threats as its reason for denying access (X News summary).
  • Trump: regime survival would be "a defeat." Trump told US media he would "absolutely" consider it a defeat if Iran's current leadership is still in power when his term ends. He also warned of severe consequences if Iran kills a US official (Al Jazeera, 8:00 PM ET Thu; Times of Israel, 8:09 PM ET Thu).
  • In Washington. A procedural vote on impeaching Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth over the Iran war failed in the House. Eleven Democrats were absent, enough to have changed the outcome (Al Jazeera, 5:30 AM ET).
  • Also: the UN General Assembly elected Iran to its Committee for Programme and Coordination for a term starting in 2027. The US, Israel, Canada, Australia and the UK objected (X News summary). In Tehran, President Pezeshkian joined a large funeral procession this morning for 110 unidentified Iran–Iraq War dead (X News summary).

Russia–Ukraine

  • Overnight Russian strikes. Russian attacks killed nine people and injured 61 across Ukraine over the past day, and a fire broke out at a Kyiv hospital. Russia launched 117 drones overnight, 63 of them jet-powered, Ukraine's Air Force said (Kyiv Independent, 6:49 AM ET). In Odesa, a drone strike on vehicles killed two people, including a woman driving to work. A 17-year-old was reported killed in Zaporizhzhia (X News summary).
  • Ukraine's drone wave and Volgograd. Nearly 1,200 Ukrainian drones targeted Russia overnight, and fire broke out again at Lukoil's Volgograd refinery (Kyiv Post, 2:51 AM ET). The regional governor says four people were killed and two injured in an industrial zone, the deadliest attack on the city so far (Reuters, 4:35 AM ET).
  • Talks: Europe in, but Putin firm. Putin agreed to let European representatives join the next round of talks, a US envoy said. Putin himself said Washington must work out with its allies whether they "want peace or war" (Reuters, 9:45 PM ET Thu). Zelensky welcomed the move and hopes the agenda will include ceasefire steps (Reuters, 8:09 PM ET Thu). Putin also warned that European troops deployed to Ukraine after a peace deal would be "legitimate targets" (Kyiv Post, 1:42 AM ET).
  • ISW's read. ISW says Putin and his team restated their original territorial demands and their ultimatums against NATO and Europe's post–Cold War security order (ISW, 9:46 PM ET Thu). It also says Putin repeated his false claim to control Kostiantynivka. In ISW's view, he would likely use a local ceasefire there to mass forces for a push toward Druzhkivka (ISW, 11:30 PM ET Thu).
  • Reported Russian demands (unverified). Unofficial drafts said to come from the Abu Dhabi talks reportedly include all of Donbas, permanent Ukrainian neutrality, and even a Finnish exit from NATO. The Kremlin denied suggesting that any NATO member leave the alliance. Finland's defense minister called the ideas "absolutely scandalous" (X News summary).
  • Other items. A car explosion in Moscow reportedly injured Zavorotny, a former Ukrainian government press secretary who fled to Russia in 2014 (Kyiv Independent, 5:25 AM ET). A US Army officer arrested in England on suspicion of trying to spy for Russia has been identified in media reports as Lt. Col. Brandon Tyner (Kyiv Independent, 11:51 PM ET Thu). Czechia could deliver its first four L-39 Skyfox jets to Ukraine in 2027 (Kyiv Independent, 4:45 AM ET). In Kyiv, an anti-corruption watchdog says the prosecutor's office has been ordered to investigate a top Zelensky ally but still hasn't opened a case (Kyiv Independent, 5:23 AM ET). Russia has canceled its Army arms forum for a third straight year (Kyiv Post, 5:04 AM ET).

Gaza and the wider region

  • Nasser Hospital strike. The IDF says Thursday's strike on Nasser Hospital targeted Hamas commander Muhammad Abu Ismail and militants it says were using the complex. Palestinian officials and MSF condemned the attack, which killed at least six people, including surgeon Dr. Rizq Abu Shaira (X News summary).
  • Winter in the camps. A nine-month-old reportedly died of hypothermia in a flooded tent in al-Mawasi, according to his father and a doctor. Aid groups say more than 150,000 tents are waiting at the border (X News summary). Reuters profiles a six-year-old burn victim caught in an Israeli dispute over aid (Reuters, 5:00 AM ET).

Other world news

  • Microsoft outage. Outlook, Teams and Copilot were still down more than 12 hours into one of Microsoft's worst outages, disrupting businesses and governments worldwide (Reuters, 6:47 AM ET). Microsoft says malicious activity was not to blame (AFP, 5:10 AM ET). A union leader says the outage is having a "serious impact" on Britain's NHS (AFP, 6:10 AM ET).
  • Nvidia fallout. US lawmakers have summoned former Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang and Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick to testify (Al Jazeera, 6:20 AM ET). A Reuters review found more than $3 billion in Chinese computing projects built on Nvidia's restricted chips (Reuters, 4:25 AM ET).
  • Summits. EU leaders are meeting in Dublin for their first-ever summit on AI (AFP, 4:56 AM ET). Trump, Putin and Xi are all set to attend next week's APEC summit in Shenzhen (Reuters, 2:35 AM ET). The Philippines hosts the ASEAN summit Nov 15–17 (Reuters, 2:15 AM ET).
  • Pope Leo is back in Chiclayo, Peru, where he served as bishop (Reuters, 6:59 AM ET).
  • Haiti: El Salvador's President Bukele released video of armed robots in an operation against gangs at Port-au-Prince's main stadium, which gangs have held for two years (Al Jazeera, 6:34 AM ET).
  • China: Beijing's anti-graft watchdog is investigating Wuhan's Communist Party chief from the COVID era (Reuters, 6:45 AM ET).
  • Kenya: six people died in a building collapse near Nairobi, Citizen TV reports (Reuters, 6:10 AM ET).
