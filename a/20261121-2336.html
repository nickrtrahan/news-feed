World News Brief: Nov 21, PM

Top line

Saturday evening, Nov 21, covering roughly 6:45 AM to 6:30 PM ET. Iran's Revolutionary Guards said they struck US Patriot radars in Bahrain, and Bahrain said an Iranian attack set an industrial site on fire. Qatari mediators were in Tehran, while Trump said he wants a "permanent" deal and CBS reported the US and Israel are weighing new strikes. In the Russia–Ukraine war, a Russia-bound cargo ship was hit by drones in the Gulf of Finland, and Estonia is moving to seal its border with Russia.

Iran and Israel–Iran

Attacks on Bahrain. Bahrain said an Iranian attack hit an industrial facility while its air defences intercepted other attacks (Reuters, 4:35 PM ET). Iran's IRGC said it hit two US Patriot radars at bases in Bahrain with missiles and drones, citing US drone flights over Iran (Al Jazeera, 4:45 PM ET). The IRGC's claim about the radars has not been independently confirmed. Bahrain later issued an all-clear and asked the UN Security Council to take "firm measures" against Iran (Al Jazeera, 6:16 PM ET).

Shipping near Hormuz. The UK maritime agency reported two tanker incidents near Oman, and Iran said a vessel hit a mine east of the Strait of Hormuz (Reuters, 9:30 AM ET). An Iranian military official told state media its forces carried out "one of the most intense" operations against ships and US aircraft overnight (Al Jazeera, 8:38 AM ET). That claim comes from Iranian state media and is unverified.

Diplomacy and threats. Iran says Qatari mediators traveled to Tehran; Deputy Foreign Minister Kazem Gharibabadi said the talks are not yet formal negotiations (Al Jazeera, 6:00 PM ET). Gharibabadi also called the US blockade an "act of war" (Al Jazeera, 10:58 AM ET). Foreign Minister Araghchi said reopening Hormuz is "not difficult" if the US accepts Iran's conditions (Al Jazeera, 11:24 AM ET). Trump said he wants a "permanent" deal that keeps Iran from getting a nuclear weapon (Al Jazeera, 10:52 AM ET), while CBS sources said the US and Israel are considering a new assault on Iran soon (CBS News, 11:00 AM ET). The strike planning is based on unnamed sources.

Pressure inside Iran. ISW assessed that Iran's worsening economy is pushing officials toward trying to break the US blockade (ISW, 9:00 AM ET). Araghchi said China and Russia oppose any change in Iran's nuclear doctrine, according to state media (AFP, 7:15 AM ET).

Russia–Ukraine

Kryvyi Rih. Overnight Russian attacks killed at least 2 people and injured 83 across Ukraine, with heavy damage in Kryvyi Rih; Ukraine's Air Force counted 3 ballistic missiles and 148 drones (Kyiv Independent, 4:09 AM ET).

Ship hit in the Gulf of Finland. Russia said a cargo vessel was struck by drones, and Finland said the ship was on fire in Finnish waters (Reuters, 8:00 AM ET; BBC, 1:25 PM ET). Who launched the drones has not been confirmed.

Estonia closing its border. People lined up to cross from Russia before Estonia seals the border over claims that Russia's GRU was behind an arson plot on a drone factory; Moscow denies it (AFP, 2:45 PM ET).

Poland and the Crimean Bridge. A Gripen pilot had NATO clearance to shoot down a suspected Russian missile over Poland but held fire due to bad weather; two missiles and a drone came down in southeastern Poland (Kyiv Post, 8:08 AM ET). Ukraine says Russia may need a year to clear the collapsed section of the Crimean Bridge (Kyiv Independent, 8:08 AM ET).

Peace talks. Zelensky said Ukraine has still not seen written commitments on "Article 5-like" security guarantees (Kyiv Independent, 9:00 AM ET) and wants the next round of talks to push for an all-for-all prisoner exchange (Kyiv Post, 7:04 AM ET). ISW said US proposals remain incompatible with the Kremlin's stated positions (ISW, 12:00 PM ET).

Other conflicts and world news

Gaza. An Israeli strike in Gaza City killed 3 people; the IDF said it targeted terror operatives, while witnesses said the vehicle was carrying aid (Times of Israel, 1:03 PM ET). Hamas asked ceasefire mediators for an urgent meeting (Al Jazeera, 1:53 PM ET).

Lebanon. President Joseph Aoun said Lebanon faces two "fateful tests": Israel's occupation of parts of its territory and interference by figures in Iran's regime (Al Jazeera, 1:01 PM ET).
