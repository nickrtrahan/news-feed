World News Brief: Nov 19, PM

Top line

Trump, Xi and Putin wrapped up their first three-way summit in Shenzhen with a joint "Declaration toward Peace" on UN reform, oil supply and nuclear risk, but nothing specific on Ukraine. Putin then invited European officials to Moscow and vowed a harsher response to Ukraine's strikes on the Crimean Bridge, while Russian drones damaged two European embassies and St. Volodymyr's Cathedral in Kyiv. In Berlin, the Coalition of the Willing called for a winter energy truce, and Zelensky asked for $115 billion in 2027 aid. On Iran, Trump said he is "not rushing" and will "see what happens" after January 1. The Senate rejected a war powers resolution 51–48, and Iran appears to be preparing tankers to run the US naval blockade. All times are ET. Coverage runs from about 6:45 AM to 6:35 PM Thursday, Nov 19.

Iran and Israel–Iran

  • Trump holds off on a decision. Trump said the US will "see what happens" in the conflict with Iran after January 1 (Reuters, 11:40 AM). Axios quoted him as saying, "I am not rushing into any decision" (Iran International, citing Axios, 1:47 PM). This morning's brief reported that Netanyahu has been pushing for a short new campaign.
  • Senate defeats the war powers resolution. The Senate voted 51–48, mostly along party lines, against a resolution that would have required Trump to end the Iran war or get approval from Congress to continue it (Iran International, 2:48 PM). Separately, a Pentagon official told lawmakers the campaign has cost about $48.3 billion since late February. That figure leaves out repairs to US bases damaged by Iranian attacks (Iran International, 5:49 PM).
  • Tankers lining up to run the blockade. TankerTrackers says Iran "appears to be preparing to ship out oil again" for the first time in four months. IRGC-affiliated outlets say Tehran is considering resuming exports despite the US naval blockade, and Fars urged sending a convoy of tankers to China (Iran International, 12:53 PM; 2:00 PM). Posts summarized by X report satellite images of more than a dozen tankers near Kharg Island with their trackers off (X story summary). Unconfirmed by major wires.
  • Strait of Hormuz. Iran's navy said a tanker it accused of "violating regulations" caught fire and stopped after hitting a mine east of the Strait (Iran International, 2:08 PM). Citing a US official, The Jerusalem Post reports that Iran has started using anti-ship cruise missiles against vessels, which are deadlier than drones (Iran International, 2:35 PM). Qatar called the closure of the Strait an "international crisis" for energy and supply chains (Iran International, 4:33 PM).
  • Weapons claims denied. Iran's Foreign Ministry spokesman Esmaeil Baghaei rejected Trump's claim that Tehran is exploring chemical and biological weapons. He compared it to the case made for the 2003 Iraq invasion (Iran International, 3:07 PM). Iran's IAEA envoy warned that information the agency gathers at Iranian sites "will certainly be used for further attacks" (Iran International, 1:07 PM).
  • Houthis hit Riyadh. A Houthi missile attack damaged Riyadh's tallest building, the headquarters of Saudi Arabia's sovereign wealth fund. It was the second strike on the city's financial district this month (Reuters, 1:55 PM). Saudi Arabia said it "would not tolerate" further attacks, and Bahrain called them terrorism (Iran International, 5:14 PM).
  • Iraq militia standoff eases. After Iraqi forces surrounded its Jurf al-Sakhar base, Kataib Hezbollah is expected to withdraw gradually within a month and hand over to Iraqi security forces, Al Arabiya reports (Iran International, 4:44 PM).
  • Inside Iran. Rights groups and activists report a new crackdown. In Dezful, security forces reportedly beat and arrested activist Sepideh Qolian's elderly father and teenage niece to pressure her to turn herself in. Protests after a bus crash in Abdanan turned into anti-regime chants, and several people were arrested (X story summary; X story summary). These are activist accounts, not independently confirmed.

Russia–Ukraine

  • Shenzhen summit. Steve Witkoff said Trump discussed Ukraine and ending the war during the talks with Putin and Xi (AP, 1:04 PM). The joint declaration did not mention Ukraine specifically. Zelensky said Ukraine will not accept decisions about it made without Ukraine present. "If you want to stop war, stop trade with Russia," he told reporters (Kyiv Independent, 5:33 PM).
  • Putin: Europeans to Moscow, and a warning. Speaking in Shenzhen, Putin denied any plan to attack Europe and invited European officials to Moscow to lay out their positions on ending the war. He also warned against foreign troops in Ukraine after a ceasefire (X story summary). On Ukraine's repeated strikes on the Crimean Bridge, he said "we will not negotiate" and promised a harsher response (Kyiv Independent, 3:03 PM).
  • Kyiv drone attack. During an hours-long Russian drone raid on Kyiv, a 26-year-old man was killed and four people were injured, city authorities said. A drone came down near the Dutch Embassy, and the Austrian Embassy and St. Volodymyr's Cathedral were also damaged (Kyiv Independent, 3:03 PM; 11:31 AM). It's not confirmed whether the embassy area was targeted. The Netherlands, Austria and Sweden reportedly summoned Russia's envoys (X story summary).
  • Crimean Bridge. Ukraine confirmed it hit the bridge's railway with Neptune missiles and halted train traffic. There are reports of fuel restrictions and gas-station lines in occupied Sevastopol (Kyiv Independent, 3:03 PM).
  • Berlin: Coalition of the Willing. The leaders of Germany, France, the UK and Ukraine called on Russia to accept a winter energy ceasefire and a Black Sea truce for food exports (Kyiv Independent, 4:31 PM). Merz said he expects new peace talks "soon," with Europe at the table alongside the US, Russia and Ukraine (AFP, 2:16 PM; BBC, 2:20 PM).
  • Security guarantees. US envoy Jared Kushner said Trump is open to security guarantees for Ukraine as part of a peace deal (Reuters, 3:00 PM). Posts summarized by X say Kushner also claimed Russia is now comfortable with Article 5-style guarantees (X story summary). Zelensky said there is no shared understanding of what such guarantees would mean (Reuters, 6:35 PM), and he said Ukraine would welcome US bases (X story summary).
  • Money. The European Commission proposed another €45 billion ($49 billion) loan for Ukraine in 2027 (Kyiv Independent, 3:14 PM). Zelensky reportedly told allies Ukraine needs about $115 billion next year (X story summary). Euroclear told the European Parliament there are "no viable suggestions" yet for using immobilized Russian assets (Kyiv Independent, 1:27 PM).
  • Battlefield note. ISW says reported speed restrictions on SpaceX's Starshield network over parts of Ukraine are still in place. ISW says they are hampering Ukraine's defense against long-range strikes (ISW, 1:09 PM).

Israel, Gaza and Lebanon

  • Gaza. The Israeli military said it had finished a one-day ground operation in central Gaza near Nuseirat and Bureij, which it said targeted a Hamas tunnel. Palestinians returned to assess the damage (AFP, 4:30 PM). Hamas called it a ceasefire violation. Palestinian officials say Israeli drone strikes on tents in Gaza City killed at least five people (X story summary).
  • Lebanon. The IDF said it struck a Hezbollah missile production and storage site near Janta in the Beqaa Valley, citing ceasefire violations. Lebanese media reported several blasts but no immediate casualties (X story summary).

Other world news

  • Shenzhen declaration. The US-China-Russia "Declaration toward Peace" commits to UN reform, stable petroleum supplies, opposing ICC "overreach," and talks to reduce nuclear risk. It also restates that a nuclear war cannot be won (X story summary). Trump returns to Washington and has scheduled a news conference for noon ET Friday (X story summary).
  • Russian bombers near Taiwan and Japan. Two Russian Tu-95 bombers flew through Taiwan's air defense zone and between Japanese islands during the summit, and both countries scrambled fighters. Neither country reported a violation of its territorial airspace (X story summary).
  • Venezuela. Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth made an unannounced visit to Caracas and signed a defense pact with acting President Delcy Rodríguez covering training, intelligence sharing and counter-narcotics operations (X story summary).
  • Eastern Congo. Local activists report drone strikes on villages around Minembwe that damaged homes and schools and injured a child. The Congolese army says rebels use schools to store weapons (X story summary). Not independently confirmed.
